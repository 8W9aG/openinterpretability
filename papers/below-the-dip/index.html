Below the Dip

Training moves most of GPT-2's neurons to a resting point left of GELU's minimum. Here is what we measured about that, what we ruled out, and what stays unknown. Each scene uses the recorded results of two papers: what default-on negative poles do and why training rests neurons below the dip.

The valley

On small GPT-2s, the rest deepens with training. When centring stops the weights from setting it, a learned value for each neuron digs nearly the same valley. Neither stops at −1.

Which one is the neuron?

Below are the low tails of six units at L9. One is the real neuron N1046. The other five are random directions in the MLP's input, each at the same share of tokens. Larger type means the token falls below the dip more often. A dot marks a leading space. Pick the one you think is real.

What stays open

We do not know why one-sided activations favour a low rest. Small models and few seeds limit these results. One long run went wrong with no error, and we discarded it and say so in the write-up. The next-token lead of the negative side may come from weights learned for the positive side, because both read the same h.
