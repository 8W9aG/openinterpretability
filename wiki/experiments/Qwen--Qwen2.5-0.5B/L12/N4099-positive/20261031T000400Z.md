## Hypothesis

> The positive side of this neuron is the counterpart of its verified negative pole: it fires on a token that is a later/internal piece of a machine-formatted string - a snake_case/camelCase identifier or argument, a dotted qualified import name, a number such as a year or version, or a repeated format element - when that string sits inside a code, markup, comment or template construct with a fixed, rehearsed form (e.g. `_length` in `CharField(max_length = 255)`, `files` in `{% load staticfiles %}`, `.slf` in `import org.slf4j.LoggerFactory;`, the interior digits of `2016` in a `// Copyright` header, the third `M` of `"EEEE d MMMM y"`, `-` in `encoding="utf-8"`). It stays near zero when the same words occur in prose, on whole-word tokens, on pieces of ordinary alphabetic words, and on the rare names that drive the negative pole (Coldwell Banker, Daler-Rowney, HMC5883L), which is the distinction the two poles draw: rare name whose completion must be retrieved (negative) versus familiar machine-formatted string whose continuation is set by the format (positive).
