## Hypothesis

> The negative pole fires where a clause is framed as someone's report, claim, guess or open question rather than as an asserted fact: it peaks on the complementizer ' that' after reportative or epistemic predicates ('reports that', 'claimed that', 'alleged that', 'suspects that', 'seems that', 'it is likely that'), on 'whether'/'if' introducing an embedded question, and on first-person hedges and direct questions ('I think', 'I guess', 'I'm assuming', 'at a guess', 'Do I...?', 'Is that...?'). The same connectives inside a factive or certain frame ('confirmed that', 'knew that', 'the fact that', 'it is clear that'), with a demonstrative 'that', and in plain declarative prose stay near baseline, while the opposite pole is the explanation direction: its output promotes cause and source words ('the reason', 'cause', 'culprit', 'origin', 'blame') and it fires on explicit explanation ('the reasons can be explained by', 'possible mechanisms for', 'what could account for').
