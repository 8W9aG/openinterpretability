## Hypothesis

> Retrospective detector of metatextual reference: the pole fires across a span that points at the text being written or read, at another written text, or at a named part of one - 'in this article / post / blog / chapter / section / page / report / guide / piece', 'This article will...', 'discussed in Chapter 7', 'see / refer to Section 3, Appendix A, page 12', 'for more information, see X', 'click here', 'if you are reading this'. ## Discussion

<!-- Free-form notes. This page is never regenerated, so edits here are kept. -->

First audit of this neuron, drawn by the planner's explore arm (audit seed 4170748528). The
hypothesis — a retrospective **metatextual-pointer** detector, not a publication-noun or
`see`/`click` token detector — came through at **VERIFIED 0.85**: 11/13 hard negatives, 6/7
holdouts, 8/8 counterfactuals, retrospective lookahead check. Token identity accounts for
R² 0.19 and the never-active baseline for 0.49, so the probe result is well clear of the
trivial baselines that usually explain a surface-token pole.

The most informative probes are the two long ones, because they pull topic and pointer
apart. A 300-word beekeeping passage scores **1.378** (active) purely on two self-references
inside it — `As noted earlier in this guide` and `the table on the previous page` — while a
long, equally print-themed passage about a night shift at a newspaper plant stays at
**0.308** despite containing `an article about the harbour development` (indefinite, generic).
A code comment pointing at `Appendix B of the deployment manual` also fires (1.027). That is
the pattern the hypothesis describes: a document pointer inside prose of any genre, not the
genre itself.

The three failures mark the boundary of the claim rather than overturning it. `Click the
button on the left` (0.828) and `This chapter of my life` (1.224) both fire, though the
hypothesis predicted they would not: the detector generalises the demonstrative/imperative
pointer frame (`this X`, `click ...`) to referents that are not text, so it is better read as
a pointer-construction detector tuned on document vocabulary than as a strict text-reference
detector. Conversely, a legal contract clause cross-referencing `schedule 2` and `clause 3`
lands at 0.585, below the 0.737 active threshold — dense legalese with part-of-document
references does not trip it. Net specificity 0.85 and sensitivity 0.86 absorb these.

Explanation scores are the strongest signal in the record: detection 0.85 (TPR 0.70,
FPR 0.00) and fuzzing 0.80 (TPR 0.80, FPR 0.20) from a judge that saw only the hypothesis,
though the embedding AUROC is 0.56. Causal metrics are high (sufficiency 0.97, necessity
0.94; median next-token KL 7.7e-04) and the lens suppresses the publication vocabulary it
has just read (`ISSN`, `Newsletter`, `ISBN`, `博客`, `博文`). The steering and Patchscope
sections are flat, consistent with a read-only detector whose write direction is not a
token predictor.

Caveat: proposer and reviewer were both `external/deepseek-flash[1m]`, and the proposer
measured its own counterfactuals while drafting, so the review is self-reviewed. A
replication with a different reviewer model is the natural first follow-up, and the
`Click`/`this chapter` failures give it concrete probes to sharpen.
