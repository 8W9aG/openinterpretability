claim — the negative pole fires on the token after a feminine article (`la`, `una`, `las`,
`die`, …) and carries into the phrase, regardless of the noun — came out **NEEDS_REVISION
at 0.71**: 11/13 hard negatives, 4/7 holdouts, 10/10 counterfactuals, only sensitivity
failed. Held-out scoring is unusually supportive for once: detection 1.00 (4/4,
FPR 0.00) and embedding AUROC 0.88, with all four active windows being `La …` fragments
(`La Paille-au-nez`, `La Palma`). The pole is very sparse — 4 of 4000 held-out windows at
threshold 1.26 — so the eight detection windows are coarse and the probe verdict carries
the weight.

What fires is broad and cross-lingual: German `Die Straße` (3.29), invented `la flurbex`
(3.32), Spanish `Varias… Otras` (2.00), French `La météo` (2.28). What fails is equally
instructive: Italian `Per preparare la focaccia` (0.47), Spanish `Aquella ventana… toda la
mañana` (0.98), and the ~600-token English travelogue with `la catedral de Sevilla` buried
in it (0.66, so the buried phrase never becomes the max). Both hard-negative failures argue
that the rule is real but wider than stated: Portuguese `as portas` fires at 2.82 — a
feminine article the hypothesis never lists, so that "failure" is a correct generalisation
in disguise — while French `Je la connais` fires at 1.92, a genuine false positive for
`la` as an object clitic rather than a determiner.

The Italian/Spanish holdout failures do not fit a simple article account. `la focaccia`
and `la catedral` are common whole words that likely tokenize as one piece, whereas
`la flurbex` is unknown and splits into subwords; the passes cluster around rare or
split nouns (`flurbex`, `Straße`, `météo`, `personas`) and the failures around frequent
single-token nouns. That is the same word-completion/word-shape signature the L4/N1360+
audit ended on, so a revision should separate "feminine article" from "article + unfamiliar
noun" with matched nouns of controlled tokenisation, and should add Portuguese `as`/`uma`
and the French/Italian clitics to the stated inventory.
