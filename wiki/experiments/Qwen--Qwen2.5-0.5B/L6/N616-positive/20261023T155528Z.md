## Hypothesis

> The positive pole fires at a clause's left edge on a fronted prepositional phrase headed by 'at' or 'on': at the start of a sentence (a capitalised 'At'/'On' after a full stop, colon, semicolon, line break or the start of the text) and after a coordinator, colon or semicolon that opens a new clause ('and at the meeting', 'but on the table', where lowercase 'at'/'on' fires too). It peaks on the head itself (' At' about 0.9 and ' On' about 0.8 of the scan's max of 2.04) and again, most reliably, on the determiner 'the' that immediately follows ('At the ...' 0.8-1.9, 'On the ...' 0.6-1.5). The same prepositions inside a clause ('met at the meeting', 'a letter lay on the table'), a lowercase 'at' straight after a full stop (0.24), and every other clause-initial head stay below the active threshold of 0.51: 'in', 'not', 'for' and 'upon' reach only 0.2-0.8 and are not predicted to fire, while 'with', 'once', 'now that' (where this neuron's negative pole fires instead), 'after', 'before', 'when', 'during', 'by', 'since', 'from', 'through', 'despite', 'given', 'against' and 'unless' stay at 0.0-0.4.
