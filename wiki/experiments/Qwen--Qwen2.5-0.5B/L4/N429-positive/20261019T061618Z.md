## Hypothesis

> The positive pole fires on the token that completes a gendered civilian title of address (Mr, Ms, Mrs, Miss, Mister, Madam, Mme, Monsieur; more weakly Madame, Herr, Señor), peaking on the abbreviation period of 'Mr.'/'Ms.'/'Mrs.' (~9-12) or on the title token itself when no period follows (~7-9), whether or not a personal name follows ('Mr. Coffee', 'Mr. Universe', 'Thank you, Mr. President'). Professional or noble titles (Dr ~2.5, Prof ~1, Sir ~1), other abbreviation periods ('p.m.', 'U.S.', 'St.') and bare names stay near zero; its Jacobian-lens output promotes the same title tokens (' Mr', '先生'), so it is a retrospective 'Mister/Missus title just read' detector rather than a predictor of the upcoming surname.

## Discussion

<!-- Free-form notes. This page is never regenerated, so edits here are kept. -->

- **Not an independent review.** The proposer and the reviewer were the same model (`external/claude-opus-5-5`, one Claude Code session), because no other provider key was available. The `independent review` gate shows ✅ only because it is no longer gated. A review by a different model is the obvious follow-up.
- **The failed hard negative is the known weak case.** `Dr. Patel …` measured 3.87, above the active threshold. Before submitting, the proposer measured `Dr.` at 2.4–3.9 depending on context, so "Dr ~2.5" understates it. Professional titles get a partial share of the feature (Dr > Prof ≈ Sir), and a sharper claim would say so.
- **Sequence-initial blind spot.** `Mr`/`Ms` as the very first token always reads about 0.03 (attention-sink position), while the period after it still fires. Probes should not put the title at position 0.
- **Causal effect is small.** Sufficiency and necessity are about 5e-4, and zero-ablation KL is 0.012. At layer 4 this looks like an early detector whose information is redundant downstream, not a driver of the output. Steering (`neuron-steering-tests`) and the negative pole are unstudied.
