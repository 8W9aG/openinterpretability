## Hypothesis

> The negative pole is a retrospective lexical detector for the time/duration/measure-and-degree vocabulary of a text - temporal nouns and adverbs (year, month, week, day, hour, period, season, date, age, Tuesday, before, after, during, always, current), quantity and degree words (two, number, long, short, old, young, fast, Celsius, part, point, group) - together with the light grammatical words such statements are built from (of, to, the, for, as, be, been, not, said, used, like, help, these). In matched carrier sentences where only the target word changes it collapses to essentially zero (measured 0.00-0.02 of its prose range of 0.1-0.4) where that word names a concrete place or object (tree, mountain, valley, forest, city, island, village, river, garden, table, window, door, boat, stone, wall, horse, bird, fish, world, child, hand, log, cat, dog) or is a core connective or pronoun (and, or, but, is, that, it, we, at, by, on, which); a few concrete nouns ('mat', 'bread') and predicates ('scored', 'calls') do fire, so the boundary is lexical rather than fully semantic and the pole should be read as a graded direction, not a closed word list.
