First audit of this pole (random draw, audit seed 1414748066; 200k fineweb-edu tokens as
512-token windows; max 2.90). The **specific-amount** claim — fires on a numeral or number
word together with the unit or measured noun it quantifies, drops to under half when the
same property is described qualitatively, and is unchanged when one amount replaces another
— came out **NEEDS_REVISION at 0.74**: 8/13 hard negatives, 6/7 holdouts, 9/9
counterfactuals, failed gate specificity. The never-active baseline is 0.38, so the
hypothesis (0.74) is well clear of it.

The positive half holds. Firing: `leaning by forty-five millimetres` (2.06), `40 litres of
diesel` (1.76), `250 grams of butter … 25 minutes` (1.67), `minus 12 degrees … 90
kilometres per hour` (1.67), `two terabytes … 45 megabytes` (1.55), `3.2 kilometres` (1.16).
The failure is the qualitative exclusion: `The old truck was heavy, slow and thirsty`
(1.07), `This summer has been much hotter` (0.86) and `freezing and the wind was brutal`
(0.86) all fire without any numeral, and the idiomatic `a hundred and one reasons` fires
highest of the negatives at 1.98. In the other direction, the vague quantity `well over a
century` stays at 0.33, so it is not quantity in general either.

The pattern that fits is **degree/intensity**: specific magnitudes (numerals + units),
comparatives (`hotter`), and intensity predicates (`heavy, slow and thirsty`, `freezing …
brutal`) all fire, while a hedged approximation (`well over a century`) does not. A
revision should state a gradability/degree claim — how strongly the property holds rather
than whether it is quantified — and treat numerals as one case of it. Held-out explanation
scores: detection 0.62 (TPR 0.36, FPR 0.12), fuzzing 0.56, AUROC 0.64.

measured its own counterfactuals. The five qualitative negatives are the seeds for the
revision.
