## Discussion

<!-- Free-form notes. This page is never regenerated, so edits here are kept. -->

First experiment on this pole (random draw, audit seed 2805295159; 200k fineweb-edu tokens
read as 512-token windows). The "specific-reference" hypothesis over-generalises. It holds
on 11/13 hard negatives, but the two failures are a generic-plural rabbit sentence
("Rabbits are social animals…", 0.594) and an everyday shopping list ("milk, bread,
apples, rice, and soap", 0.622): familiar concrete nouns that are not specific references.
Only 3/7 holdouts fire, and every failure is a *novel-domain* specific reference (geology
minerals, music-theory terms, a named ship and sea, pangolin/axolotl). With the low scan
maximum (2.11), the 0.53 threshold sitting just below several of these, and negligible
zero-ablation KL, the pattern suggests a weak, word-familiarity-flavoured noun response
rather than reference specificity as such. A narrower claim or a broader/longer rescan is
the natural follow-up, and the separate self-review caveat stands: the proposer and
reviewer were the same model.
