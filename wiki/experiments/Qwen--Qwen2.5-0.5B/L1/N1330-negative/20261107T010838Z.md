## Hypothesis

> A layer-1 spelling-fragment detector: the negative pole fires when the current token is one of a learned set of unusual letter fragments that occur as non-final pieces of names, acronyms and technical or foreign words - 'US' after 'V' (VUS), 'PT' after 'F' (FPTCs), 'MT' after 'E' (EMT), 'OP' after 'C' (COPD), 'AY' after 'H' (HAYNE), 'orm' after 'c' (cormorant), 'ank' after 'K' (Kankakee), 'uv' after 'ma' (mauvaises), 'æ' after 'hj' (hjælpe) - and on the name-onset tokens ' Hay'/'Hay' (Hayne, Hayao), ' Tay' (Tayyip), ' Bog' (Bogut) and ' Anderson', plus a smaller glue family of hyphen pieces ('-free' after 'risk'/'gun', '-engine' after 'reverse', '-' in 'X-Plane', the minus in '3*a - 7'). The trigger is the specific fragment, not the word's rarity or meaning: equally rare names and technical words with ordinary spellings (Kowalski, Nakamura, apoptosis, cholangitis), the same letters inside common words (bay, dorm, bank, corner, corn, GTP, 'haystack' as a single token) and ordinary prose all stay near zero, while the positive pole of the same SwiGLU unit fires on a disjoint fragment set ('ao' in Hayao, 'ot' in luaotfload).
