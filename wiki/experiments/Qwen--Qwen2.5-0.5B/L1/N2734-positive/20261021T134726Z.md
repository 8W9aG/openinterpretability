## Hypothesis

> Retrospective word-piece detector: the positive pole fires on a token that is a non-initial piece of a multi-token word — a token with no leading space that continues the orthographic word begun in the previous token — and peaks (~0.9-1.0) on the continuation piece, usually the first one, of an uncommon name, identifier or loan/technical word (C|orm|orant, J|op|lin, proper|Id, Ab|und|ant, eag|erness, Upps|ala, J|alan); the same string starting a fresh word after a space (property Id 0.39, property id 0.27) or a word-initial token fires far less, and word-specific rare fragments (Reykjavik's 'jav'/'ik', Kubernetes) do not fire at all. A second, distinct SwiGLU double-negative regime (gate<0 and up<0) fires on 'by' as the passive agent after a past participle (measured by, generated by), while every other 'by' variant is the pole's most-suppressed token in the vocabulary (negative pole = a 'by' detector); a weak whole-word component also fires on a few common words when they are the second element of a tight compound/collocation (student| pilot, right| now, to| have).
