## Discussion

<!-- Free-form notes. This page is never regenerated, so edits here are kept. -->

First experiment on this pole (random draw, audit seed 2379130190; 200k fineweb-edu
tokens read as 512-token windows; max activation 5.84). The claim is semantic and
unusually clean: the pole fires when text quantifies over *how much else there is* besides
the item just named — fronted exclusion/addition connectives, negative-polarity remainder
quantifiers and exhaustiveness predicates — and is flat where the same words name no set.
The review verifies it at 0.89 with sufficiency and necessity both 1.00, well above the
never-active baseline (0.53).

Both misses are the same construction, the `That's …` idiom, and they cut in opposite
directions. Deictic `That's it right there, the little blue house…` fires (2.022) although
the hypothesis says it is flat; exhaustiveness `That's not all: two more passages…` misses
at 1.337 against the 1.461 threshold although the hypothesis says it fires. So the
exhaustiveness-vs-deictic distinction is not clean for this idiom, while the exclusion
connectives and NPI quantifier families carry the claim.

Held-out explanation scores rest on very few windows (5 of 4000 active; detection 0.60),
so they are weak evidence either way. Steering is a null and the mechanism is
retrospective; proposer and reviewer were the same model, so the record is self-reviewed.
