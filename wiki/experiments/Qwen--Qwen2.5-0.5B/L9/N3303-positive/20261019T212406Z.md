## Hypothesis

> Fires on the word that expresses how often or at what rate an event happens, when it modifies an event that comes after it: indefinite-frequency adverbs placed before the verb ('Do your emotions sometimes/often/occasionally/frequently [rarely, usually, regularly weaker] dictate', strongest inside questions and weak in plain declaratives), the rate article or quantifier before a unit ('three times a year', 'twice a day', 'every 5 minutes', 'X percent of the time', 'spending more and more time'). It also fires on the closely related 'afraid/scared/reluctant to' (an inclination about whether the action will happen). It stays near zero for non-frequency adverbs (really, also, personally), for 'never' and 'always', for 'per'/'this' in place of the rate 'a', for sentence-initial 'Sometimes', and for frequency words after the verb ('go to the park often').
