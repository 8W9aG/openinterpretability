## Hypothesis

> The negative pole fires on a personal pronoun (you, I, we, she, he, they; weaker for me/your/her and for 'one'/'someone') that opens the subject slot of a new, usually subordinate or embedded clause right after a linking word (when, if, in case, should, so, hoping, help, let, sparing, what), whose referent is NOT an earlier-mentioned person: deictic or generic 'you/I/we', or a hypothetical 'she/he/they' in text about objects or advice. It is the contrast to the positive pole (pronouns that refer back): it drops sharply when a third-person pronoun has a matching earlier human antecedent ('The nurse ... when she'), and stays near zero on 'it', on names and full noun phrases in the same slot, on sentence-initial main-clause pronouns, and on non-pronoun words, while deictic 'you' is unaffected by an earlier person.
