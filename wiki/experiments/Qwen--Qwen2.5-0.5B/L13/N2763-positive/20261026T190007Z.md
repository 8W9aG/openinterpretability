First experiment on this pole (random draw, audit seed 582134507; 200k fineweb-edu tokens
read as 512-token windows). At max activation 0.71 it is the second-weakest pole recorded —
2.2nd percentile of layer-13 positive-pole maxima. The proposer correctly diagnosed it as a
near-floor, non-selective unit (61% of tokens in the SwiGLU double-negative mode, strength
tracking only |u| at r = 0.97, 9.9% of tokens above a quarter of max), and then made a
binary prediction: long running prose active, short or non-prose text inactive.

The review rejects that prediction in the strongest way: all 7 holdouts fire, but only
2/13 hard negatives do, so balanced accuracy is 0.58 and the token-identity baseline (0.62)
beats it. 89% of held-out windows (3566/4000) are active. Even short draft notes (0.199)
and a one-line forum comment (0.340) cross the 0.176 threshold. So the non-selectivity is
broader than the hypothesis allowed: at a quarter of this max, ordinary text of any length
or type is active.

This is the same lesson recorded on L13/N2147−: a floor-level pole is always "active" under
the protocol's relative threshold, so the honest claim is about graded amplitude, not a
binary detector. The mechanistic diagnosis here stands (and causal metrics are
correspondingly weak: sufficiency 0.55, necessity 0.36, median effect ~6e-6); only the
"short/non-prose exempt" clause is refuted. Proposer and reviewer were the same model, so
the record is self-reviewed.
