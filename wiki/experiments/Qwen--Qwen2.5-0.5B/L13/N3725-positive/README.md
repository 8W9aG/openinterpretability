> The pole fires where a clause finishes a self-contained statement: on the last content token(s) of the predicate and on the boundary token that closes it (',', '.', '."', '?"', '!"', ')', '),', ':', '。', ';\n', or a closing LaTeX '$'), i.e. exactly at positions where the sentence could end and a new paragraph could begin - the strongest peaks sit on main-clause completions of a specific, information-bearing statement (e.g. 'had broken loose,', 'were dead.', '(TLRs),', 'Idris Elba | with', 'Sumerian | is', 'fallen pregnant'), and it is near zero inside a constituent (within a multi-token word, after a determiner/adjective, between a verb and its required object). Its output raises paragraph/sentence terminators ('.', '.\n\n', '<|endoftext|>') and lowers continuation tokens (',', ' and', ' but', ' then'); activation is graded main clause > relative/participial clause > phrase-internal, needs preceding multi-sentence context, and is usually quiet in isolated one-line inputs.
