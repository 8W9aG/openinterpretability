## Hypothesis

> The negative pole fires on the tokens that close a statement or expression in code and mathematical notation: the `false`/`true`/`None` literal that ends a `return` or a boolean assignment (or the bare `return`/`break`/`continue` keyword that ends its own statement), the `;` and `}` that terminate the statement and its `if`/`while`/`for` block, and the binary operator (`-`, `=`, `==`, `) -`) that follows a completed operand in an equation. The pattern is surface-level (a code-like line fires even inside a comment or string), and ordinary prose and code without such a closing construct stay quiet.
