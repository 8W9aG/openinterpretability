## Hypothesis

> The pole marks the close of a free-standing direct quotation: it peaks on the punctuation token that ends a quotation which occupies its own sentence - typically the comma-glued close ',”', ',"', '!”' or '?”' that hands the utterance back to the narrator, as in '“We need cash,” he said' - and it is near zero on the identical close-quote token when the quotation is embedded in the running sentence (after a reporting verb, 'He said “We need cash,” and left'; as a quoted term, 'The phrase “we need cash,” was used'; or after a mid-sentence colon), on scare quotes closing mid-phrase, on '”' used for inches, on opening quotes, and on a quotation that ends its sentence with '.”' at a paragraph break. Within the free-standing class the strength is graded rather than categorical: it grows with the amount of preceding news-style reporting (about 1.2 with no context, 2.8 after one reportative paragraph, 3.9 after two, up to 5.9 in long articles), is suppressed to near zero when another quotation close falls within a few tokens, and does not depend on what follows the close (the same token fires whether an attribution, a conjunction or another clause comes next).
