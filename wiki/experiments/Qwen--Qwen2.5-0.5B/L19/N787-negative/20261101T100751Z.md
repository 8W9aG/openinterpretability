## Hypothesis

> The negative pole fires when the current token is a word-initial subword fragment — the first piece of a longer word that the tokenizer split into several tokens — with a learned per-fragment gain that is large for p-/re- technical-vocabulary onsets (' pem' in pemphigus, ' pe' in peptidase, ' p' in pumice or penguin, ' per' in percutaneous, ' rep'/'rem'/'ref') and small for most other fragments (' ps', ' pl', ' pr', ' punct'); pieces after the first ('ph', 'ig', 'ands') are much weaker. It is near zero for ordinary complete words (even rare or p-initial ones: patients, people, pneumonia, granite) and for nonsense fragments, weakens when the same word already appeared in the context, and also fires on pieces of non-word strings — DOI digit runs after a citation cue, page references ('p. 2'), p-values ('p = .05'), 'P.3d' citations, 'per cent', hyphenated compounds ('air-powder').
