First audit of the negative pole of this neuron (opposite-pole follow-up; the positive pole
is the machine-literal feature recorded in `L19/N937-positive/20261024T153002Z`). Scan: 200k
fineweb-edu tokens as 512-token windows; max activation 17.5. The claim is a "cool, dry,
shaded, sheltered spot" place-phrase feature: the pole fires where the model commits to a
condition-qualified place phrase inside a keeping/placing/locative clause, strongest on the
preservation idiom `keep/store it in a cool[, dry/dark] place` (measured 19–26, above the
scan max) and on animals taking shelter. The review verifies it at 0.96 — 13/14 hard
negatives, 6/6 holdouts, sufficiency and necessity both 1.00.

The one failure is informative: `We sat on the porch after dinner, enjoying a cool breeze
and a cool drink while the sprinklers ran` fires at 6.487, although the isolated phrases
`a cool breeze` (2.6) and `a cool drink` (2.1) were calibrated flat. In a comfort scene
with sprinklers and a porch, the pole fires anyway — either the condition extends to
cooling/comfort situations more broadly, or several partial cues accumulate within a
sentence.

The pole pair is a clean register split: the positive side is machine-formatted literals
(IP/port digits, URL path pieces, byte-fallback fragments) and the negative side is natural
prose about places and their conditions. This also refines the positive audit's note that
the negative pole peaks on "ordinary function words in narrative prose" — those tokens
(` a`, ` in`, ` under`, ` out`, ` to`) are the introducers of condition-qualified place
phrases. Steering shows only mild place/condition drift at x4; the never-active baseline is
0.46. Proposer and reviewer were the same model, so the record is self-reviewed.
