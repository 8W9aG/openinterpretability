## Hypothesis

> L19/N2185+ is a lookahead adjective neuron: it peaks on the token at which the next word (or the next word after a determiner, e.g. 'described as " a dark velvet"') is a descriptive, gradable adjective, whichever syntactic slot announces it - copula or linking verb (is/are/was/be, sounded, tasted, looked), degree adverb (so, most, very, extremely, generally), determiner or possessive opening an attributively modified NP ('its expressive...', 'a web-friendly...'), or a coordinator/comma continuing an adjectival series ('ADJ and', 'ADJ,'). Its transported output direction promotes exactly this class ('crisp', 'soft', 'warm', 'sharp', 'bold'), and activation collapses to near zero when the same surface position instead predicts a noun, verb or manner adverb ('wood and', 'ran smoothly', 'Victorian and'), so it tracks the model's expectation of an upcoming adjective rather than the part of speech already read; the negative pole is not a mirror and stays an order of magnitude weaker.

## Discussion

<!-- Free-form notes. This page is never regenerated, so edits here are kept. -->

First audit of this pole (random draw, audit seed 3135178046). A **lookahead adjective**
claim — the pole peaks at the token announcing a descriptive gradable adjective, and its
transported output direction promotes that adjective class — came out **NEEDS_REVISION at
0.60**, and the most important failure is the mechanism gate: `measure_lookahead` says
**retrospective** (KL=0.0018 across the swapped adjectives ` quiet`, ` firm`, ` dark`,
` refreshing`, ` fresh`) although the submission claimed lookahead. Specificity was fine
(12/13), counterfactuals 7/7, but only 2/7 holdouts fired.

The probe evidence shows what the pole is actually doing. The scan's maximum (10.41) is on
` as` in `described as “dark velvet”`, and the one hard negative that fires is
`Reviewers described the sequel as a rehash of the original…` at **5.10** — the same
`described as a` frame with a noun phrase after it. So the frame fires whatever follows,
which is exactly why the lookahead gate fails: the activation is on the frame token
already read, not on the expectation of an adjective. The holdouts that pass
(`The mattress felt lumpy…` 3.41, the long rose-garden passage 3.92) contain linking-verb
slots, but three equally canonical adjective slots fail (`The verdict was harsh…` 1.43,
`The lake was calm, glassy…` 2.00, `So thick was the fog…` 0.25) — and so does a code
comment with `is stale` (0.15). The claim's own announcement inventory is therefore not
what the pole tracks.

Two caveats about the design. The counterfactuals all replicate because they swap the
*wording after* the announcer inside the same frame, and with a max-over-text measure the
peak can sit on either token; they do not separate frame from adjective. And the 10× range
between the max (10.4) and the 2.6 threshold means two holdouts pass simply by containing
a familiar phrase.

Held-out explanation scores: detection 0.70 (TPR 0.88, FPR 0.48), fuzzing 0.69,
embedding AUROC 0.53. Self-reviewed (proposer and reviewer both
`external/deepseek-flash[1m]`).

Next: treat the pole as retrospective and test the comparison frame directly —
`described as` / `regarded as` / `known as` / `hailed as` with noun-phrase complements,
and linking verbs with noun objects (`felt the fabric`, `looked the part`) — before any
further adjective account.
