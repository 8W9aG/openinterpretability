The work continued through the following winter, slowed by a burst pipe that closed the west wing for three weeks. When the building reopened, the team found that a box of unlisted correspondence had been moved to the attic, where it stayed for another year before anyone noticed. The correspondence turned out to be the most interesting material in the collection: letters from a nineteenth-century naturalist who had travelled along the coast and sent specimens back to a museum in the capital. His descriptions of birds and shellfish were detailed, and his sketches, though faded, were clear enough to identify most of the species. The archive decided to publish a small selection online, with transcriptions prepared by a local history group. The transcriptions were checked twice, once against the original and once against a style sheet that listed the abbreviations used in the letters. The images were stored as uncompressed files, and backup copies were kept on two drives in different rooms. Staff checked the drives at the start of every month, replacing any that showed errors during the routine test. Readers who consult the digital collection can download a copy of every letter, and view the images at full resolution with a zoom tool. The project team hopes to add the remaining correspondence by the end of next year, subject to funding.
