## Hypothesis

> Retrospective detector of literal, vivid descriptions of physical scenes and bodies in motion. It fires on the concrete noun, or on the 'nearly/almost' + motion verb, when a natural object or body part does something physical: strongest on the literal word 'sun' (the sun came up/rose/set), then on things like branches pushing through a roof, eyebrows/heart 'nearly' touching or stopping, an eagle's wings 'and soared', waves 'crashed' on rocks, leaves 'rustled', and on 'fast' in the stock phrase 'X is fast approaching'. It stays near zero for the same words in non-physical, figurative or institutional use ('the band came up', 'Sun Microsystems', 'over the moon', 'raining cats and dogs', 'moving fast.', 'nearly finished the report') and on technical or administrative text; sporadic peaks on mid-word subword pieces of rare words (capecit|ab|ine, Gurt|ov|oy, Danish ' ø|konomien') are leftovers that this hypothesis does not predict.
