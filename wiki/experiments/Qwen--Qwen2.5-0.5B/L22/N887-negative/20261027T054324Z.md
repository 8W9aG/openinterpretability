First experiment on this pole (random draw, audit seed 2897750145; 200k fineweb-edu
tokens read as 512-token windows; max activation 11.00). The claim is a definite-article
feature: the pole peaks on ` the` in running prose, roughly 5–9× stronger than `a`/`that`/
`this`/`one` in the same left context, with strength set by the preceding span rather than
by the article's probability or next-token entropy; it also fires on a small lexical set of
word-initial subword tokens (` Her`, ` Ple`, ` Gre`, ` Middle`, ` clar`, `am`) that respond
to token shape. The review verifies it at 0.86 — 13/13 hard negatives, 5/7 holdouts, 8/8
counterfactuals, sufficiency and necessity 0.875 — above the token-identity baseline (0.79).

Both misses are informative. A long Federal Reserve/Treasury report reaches only 1.836
against the 2.75 threshold even though it is dense in ` the`, so long financial prose is a
weaker setting than the calibration passages. And the `am` fragment holdout
(`amphibians / amalgamated / amphibious`, 0.326) fails, so that member of the lexical set is
much weaker than ` Her`/` Ple`/` Gre` are.

The token-identity baseline is high (0.79) because ` the` is the dominant token; what the
hypothesis adds is the determiner contrast within the same frame and the fragment set.
Causal metrics are 0.875/0.875; held-out explanation scores are mixed (detection 0.52 with
TPR 0.84 and FPR 0.80, fuzzing 0.83, AUROC 0.45). Proposer and reviewer were the same
model, so the record is self-reviewed.
