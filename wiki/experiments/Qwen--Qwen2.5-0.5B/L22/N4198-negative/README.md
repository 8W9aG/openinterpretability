> The pole is a retrospective word-assembly detector: it fires on a token that is one sub-word piece of a word the model must build from several pieces instead of retrieving it whole - the opening piece of a rare, technical, foreign or proper-name word (' fl' in flanked, ' cl' in clapped, ' h' in hedges, ' U' in Uley, 'p' in p70S6K), or an interior piece of such a word ('rop' in neutropenic, 'ap' in extrapulmonary, 'op' in bronchopulmonary). It is near zero on single-token words in ordinary prose and on pieces of common words (' blo' in bloomed, ' uns' in unsolved), and the same fragment fires several times harder inside a stretch of unfamiliar vocabulary - a list or table of contents of proper names ('England, Wales and Northern Ireland', '#### Uley') - than in plain prose; its few whole-word peaks (' deprived' in a deprivation index, 'last'/'before' in one dated news passage) are unexplained leftovers.
