First experiment on this pole (random draw, audit seed 93564625; 200k fineweb-edu tokens
read as 512-token windows; max activation 9.75). The claim is unusual and precise: the pole
fires on a **tokenizer-split subword piece** (` fl`, ` sul`, ` cl`, ` cav`, ` pl`, ` conv`,
` sk`, ` carb`, word-internal `um`), with strength set mainly by which piece it is, while
the same letters inside one fused token (` flower`, ` clock`) and the leading pieces of other
rare/technical words stay flat; ` air` is a weak context-gated member. The review verifies
it at 0.86 (13/13 hard negatives, 5/7 holdouts, 10/10 counterfactuals), above the token
identity baseline (0.71).

Both missed holdouts are informative. A code-comment `clamp` (0.331 against a 2.437
threshold) shows the piece family is far weaker inside code than in prose, and
`skirmish / plundered / carburetor` in one sentence reaches 2.109 — just short — so some
pieces respond less than ` fl`/` cl` do. The proposer's own honest leftover is worth
repeating: a small residue on ordinary single tokens (e.g. ` system` 4.7) is not covered by
the piece rule.

Causal metrics are thin — only one non-control pair yielded a usable sufficiency reading
(0.66) and the necessity percentile is 0.125 — so the causal story here rests on little.
Held-out explanation scores are moderate (detection 0.70, fuzzing 0.68, AUROC 0.53).
Proposer and reviewer were the same model, so the record is self-reviewed.
