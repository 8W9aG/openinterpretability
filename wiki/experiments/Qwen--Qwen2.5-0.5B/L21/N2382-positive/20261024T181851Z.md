## Hypothesis

> The pole detects reader-facing service copy: it fires where a website, app, shop or company states what it does, offers or enables for its users, peaking on the connective or light function word that opens the purpose, capability or benefit clause (usually ' to', ' can', ' and', ' you', ' by', ' we'), sometimes on a word of the promise itself ('Keep', 'customers', 'better'). It is strongest (8-12) on cookie-consent and privacy-notice sentences ('We use cookies to ...', 'Our partners use cookies to ensure ...', 'This website uses cookies and similar technologies to ...'), moderate (3-6) on app, feature and benefit copy ('the app uses your camera/location to ...', 'other users can recognize you ...', 'our parties are a great way to make new friends', 'we'll keep you notified'), and weak (<2) on the very same tokens in detached narration, news and scientific reporting with a purpose infinitive ('the factory uses coal to ...', 'the committee met on Tuesday to discuss ...', 'she used a knife to ...'), so token identity alone does not explain it: 97% of the scan's 2,791 ' to' occurrences stay below 3. A weaker (<5) leftover fires on rare Greek-root technical subwords (pharmacophores, Sephadex, electroencephalography) and a few non-English function pieces.
