First audit of this pole (random draw, audit seed 3648219874; 200k fineweb-edu tokens as
512-token windows; max 9.28 — one of the strongest poles seen this session). The
**sub-word-fragment** claim — the pole fires on a short word-internal letter chunk
(` Am`, ` em`, `Ex`, ` sil`, ` imp`, …) inside a multi-token, non-lexicalised name or
technical word, is word-invariant once the fragment is fixed, and stays silent when the
same letters form a complete common word or a single-token word — came out **VERIFIED 0.78**:
11/13 hard negatives, 5/7 holdouts, 8/8 counterfactuals, all gates. It beats the
token-identity baseline only narrowly (0.78 vs 0.75; token identity R² 0.37), so the
result rests on the probe design rather than a wide margin.

The core contrast holds. Holdouts fire: `Amankwah` 6.60, `emerald` 4.01, `impeller` 2.42,
`silversmith` 2.41, the long refurbishment passage 3.90 (threshold 2.32). Negatives stay
low: `I am not sure…` 0.66, `America` 0.30, `Amsterdam` 0.76, `Amazing` 0.36, ordinary
prose 0.28–0.67, `My ex rang` 0.28 and the shopping list 0.28.

Two hard negatives fire and are genuine counterexamples: `Put an em dash between the two
clauses` (3.42) and `Professor Kowalczyk from the institute in Wroclaw` (4.71) — a rare
foreign name with none of the claimed chunks, so the inventory is incomplete: the pole
tracks rare non-lexicalised names/technical words more broadly, not just the listed
fragments. Two holdout "failures" (`Bijlmer` 0.54, `astronomer` 0.60) may be probe
mis-specifications rather than misses: both are likely single-token words, which the claim
explicitly predicts stay silent, yet the reviewer marked them active for the `ij`/`astr`
pieces.

Held-out explanation scores show the usual asymmetry and one strong number: detection 0.57
(TPR 0.20, FPR 0.05), embedding AUROC 0.57, but **fuzzing 0.94** (TPR 0.88, FPR 0.00) — the
claim locates active tokens well when asked to highlight them. Self-reviewed (proposer and
reviewer both `external/deepseek-flash[1m]`).
