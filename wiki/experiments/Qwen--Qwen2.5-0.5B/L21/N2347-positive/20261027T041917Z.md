First audit of the positive pole of this neuron (opposite-pole follow-up; the negative
pole was the rejected leading-fragment claim at 0.48 in
`L21/N2347-negative/20261026T181226Z`). Scan: 200k fineweb-edu tokens as 512-token
windows; max activation 12.57. The claim is a mid-word-fragment detector: the pole fires on
a token that does not end its word (the next token finishes it) and is selective for which
fragment/word is being spelled, while fused tokens and word-final pieces stay flat. The
review gives perfect specificity (13/13 hard negatives) but only 1/7 holdouts fire, so the
verdict is REJECTED at 0.57, and the never-active baseline scores 0.93.

The missed holdouts are all fresh split words in new genres, far below the 3.14 threshold:
`pseudopseudohypoparathyroidism` (0.814), `dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane` (0.993),
`quasi-experimental / semi-autobiographical` (0.250), `counterproductive / uncompromising`
(0.506), `professionalization` deep in a long narrative (0.769) and `inextricably` in a code
comment (0.353). So the mid-word condition is necessary but nowhere near sufficient: the
pole's high peaks are tied to particular fragments and words seen in the scan (fragment
identity explains 31% of its variance), which is a token-family residue rather than a
general token-boundary feature.

That reading matches the negative pole's rejection: both sides of this neuron fail to
generalise a split-word story. Causal metrics are weak (sufficiency 0.75, necessity 0.53,
two usable pairs). Held-out explanation scores are mixed (detection 0.65, fuzzing 0.81,
AUROC 0.50). Proposer and reviewer were the same model, so the record is self-reviewed.
