## Hypothesis

> Retrospective subword-fragment detector: the negative pole fires when the current token is a BPE fragment piece — a token that typically appears inside a longer word (prefix pieces like Sub/sub/subs/SUB/Fle/arbe/ery/vir/Pr/Bib/VIS/vy/fle/ple/E; mid-word pieces like ub/ry/é/erv/v) — and it fires on that token even when the word happens to end there ('a sub sandwich', 'vice versa'), while single-token words containing the same stems (obedient, subordinate, submarine, operator, virus, surgery, Troy) stay near zero and the sequence-initial token (position 0) never fires. The peak does not depend on which continuation follows ('Sub' fires ≈9.4 in Sub ordinate, Sub marine and Sub alone), and zero-ablating the pole at a peak removes next-token probability from the word's continuation pieces ('erv' after 'subs', 'ordinate' after 'ub', 'iss' after 'Fle', 'gia' after 'ry'), so its job appears to be marking 'the word is not finished' to support spelling out the rest; pieces of non-English words (arbejde, fleste, plekje, Februar) fire the same way.
