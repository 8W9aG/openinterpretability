## Hypothesis

> The negative pole tracks French-language text: it fires on the French adverb ' plus' (15-22) and, far more weakly, on a handful of other French tokens ('moins' ~6, 'chat' ~5, the word-pieces of long French words such as 'principale'/'supplémentaires' ~3), while ordinary English vocabulary produces essentially no activation (peak < 1 even for the same token: 'Two plus two', 'a big plus'). The trigger is the language of the immediate context, not the token string: the token ' plus' fires after French 'Deux'/'Trois'/'Il' (10-18.5) but not after English 'Two'/'The' (~0), so label a position active only when ' plus'/'moins' or another French word-piece sits in a French run of text; the only English-side firings I found are rare proper names such as 'ley' in 'Dyneley'/'Bletchley' (a leftover this reading does not explain).
