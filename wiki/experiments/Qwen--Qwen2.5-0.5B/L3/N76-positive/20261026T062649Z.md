<!-- Free-form notes. This page is never regenerated, so edits here are kept. -->

First experiment on this pole (random draw, audit seed 1337963623; 200k fineweb-edu
tokens read as 512-token windows; max activation 12.86). The claim is word-level, not
token-level: the peak lands on the final fragment of a word naming a skeletal/limb
structure, and the identical fragment behaves differently by word (`ula` fires 8.0 in
"fibula" but 0.3 in "uvula" and 0.1 in "nebula"). The review supports it at 0.92 —
14/14 hard negatives and 5/6 holdouts pass, against a never-active baseline of 0.58 —
with sufficiency and necessity both 1.00.

The one missed holdout marks the weak part of the claim: a strength-training sentence
listing `trapezius/rhomboids/latissimus dorsi/serratus` reaches only 0.950, well under the
3.21 threshold, even though the hypothesis counts skeletal muscles as active. Latin bone
names are the strong band (5–13); Latin muscle names sit in a much weaker 1–5 band whose
lower edge the threshold cuts through.

Two observations worth carrying forward. First, the proposer's plain-English comparison
(femur 7.4 vs "thigh bone" 2.0, patella 4.5 vs "kneecap" 0.3) says the pole is tuned to
the technical register of anatomy, not to the anatomical concept alone. Second, steering
is strongly supportive: at ×2 every neutral prompt turns into skeletal/fracture prose
("the most important artery, and the femur, and the tibia", "the patient was fractured in
the femion shaft"), while ablation reproduces baseline. At ×4 the text degenerates.

Proposer and reviewer were the same model, so the record is self-reviewed.
