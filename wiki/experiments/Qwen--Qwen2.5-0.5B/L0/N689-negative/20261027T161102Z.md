First audit of this layer-0 pole (random draw, audit seed 132924467; 200k fineweb-edu
tokens as 512-token windows; max 0.823). Token identity explains **87%** of scan variance,
by far the highest in this project, so the proposer wrote an explicitly token-identity claim
rather than a semantic one: a fixed layer-0 key centred on markup/code ids (`<X`, `>`),
CamelCase continuations, ` &`, bare capitalised title words and the word-internal pieces
`able`/`ination`/`occus`/`iers`/`ians`, with space-initial spellings, stems and other
suffixes inert. It came out **NEEDS_REVISION at 0.71**: 11/13 hard negatives, 4/7 holdouts,
9/10 counterfactuals, and — despite the 87% scan R² — it clears the confound baseline at
probe level (never-active 0.64 < 0.71).

The claim does what a token-id account should. `if(count>limit){return items[i];}` scores
0.969, the CamelCase sentence `the MatchBall trophy was presented during GameView Live, a
PlayDiscover production` 0.600, `Research & Development` 0.344, and the plain sentence
`the surplus grain was unmarketable after the flood, and contamination of the store…`
0.598 — the last exactly on the `able`/`ination` pieces, which no semantic account would
predict.

The failures are about tokenisation detail, not the mechanism. Lowercase markup
`<table><tr><td>Invoice</td>…` scores only 0.169 (the claim's `<X` examples are capital
tags like `<T`/`<string`), the lab-notes passage with line-start capitals scores 0.079, and
the museum-site sentence 0.204. Two "hard negatives" fire: `the DataFlow pipeline uses
SocketReader and PageCrawler modules` (0.347) — which the claim actually predicts, since
CamelCase continuations are in its positive inventory, so the probe expectation looks
mis-specified rather than the hypothesis — and a plain sentence `the old mill by the river…`
