## Hypothesis

> N2560's negative pole marks the end of a written quantity: it peaks when the current token is a short (at most 3 characters) alphabetic or unit-symbol fragment that cannot continue a hexadecimal numeral - any single letter g-z, or unit tokens such as ' m', 'mm', 'nm', 'km', 'h', '%', 'x', '×' - and the immediately preceding token ends in a digit ('The cable is 25 [[m]] long.' 1.55, 'rose 65[[%]]' 0.55, '#519[[m]]' 1.19, 'loc3[[g]]' 1.07); it fires much more weakly when the quantity is a spelled-out number ('twenty [[m]]' 0.67, 'twelve [[m]]' 0.44) and not at all after a non-quantifier ('the [[m]]' 0.00), so 90.8% of the 371 scan peaks at or above 0.25 x max sit on a token whose predecessor ends in a digit (13x the base rate, and 37x once the short non-hex-token condition is added). It is silent when the next token could continue the numeral or hex run (digits, and the hex letters a-f: '25 f' 0.00, '#519f' 0.00), when the preceding token is letter-final ('2s m' 0.31), and when the next token is a full word ('25 metres' 0.00, '65 percent' 0.06); its mirror-image positive pole fires on exactly those hex-letter fragments (a-f: 'fad', 'bbc', 'df') inside numeric/hex/ID strings, which multi-character abbreviations fire is token-specific (mm, nm, km, hr, min, mol do; ms, GB, MHz do not), and the residual 9% of scan peaks are unexplained word-internal continuation fragments ('acter', 'ycl', 'opol').
