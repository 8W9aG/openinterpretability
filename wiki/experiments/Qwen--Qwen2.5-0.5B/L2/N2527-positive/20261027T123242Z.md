is specificity).

The failures are the interesting part, because they all point away from pure orthography.
The reviewer put `Суд принял решение отложить слушание…` in as a hard negative precisely
because Russian `решение` ("decision") shares none of the `deci-` letters; it fires at
**1.76**. German `Die Dezimalzahl 3,14…` (spelled `Dezimal-`, not `decim-`) fires at
**4.33** against a hypothesis that says z-spellings stay near zero, and `They DECORATED
the whole hall` fires at **2.25** although `decorate` is listed as a near-zero `dec-` word.
Meanwhile two of the claim's own forms sit just under the 1.50 threshold: Spanish
`El comité anunciará su decisión` (1.44) and the code comment `stored as a decimal string`
(1.49). The stem family is real — `DECIDED` 7.43, `decisões` 5.35, `indecisive` 6.36,
`decibels` 4.18, and the 600-token bakery narrative with `decision` buried near the end
(2.68) — but the pole is a **semantic/lexical decision-and-decimal feature**, not the
letter string: it fires on the concept in another script and spelling, and it can miss
whole-token forms.

Held-out explanation scores are strong and consistent with the original: detection 0.83
(TPR 0.65, FPR 0.00), fuzzing 0.80 (TPR 0.60), embedding AUROC 0.65 on 43/4000 active
windows. So the evidence supports the family, and the disagreement with the original
reading is about the *mechanism's scope* (orthographic stem vs lexical concept), not about
whether the unit is real.
