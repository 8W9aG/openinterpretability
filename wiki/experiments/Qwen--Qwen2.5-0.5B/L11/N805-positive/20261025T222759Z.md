## Hypothesis

> The positive pole fires when the text refers to a specific-but-unnamed individual, or an indefinite subset, that is picked out of a plurality already live in the context, and it peaks on the determiner or quantifier that does the picking: partitives ('one of them', 'some of those', 'any one'), selection verbs taking a singular object ('select a partition' once the drive's partitions have been introduced, 'pick one', 'choose the second one'), and superlatives or ordinals that select one member ('the most serious', 'the first', 'which of the two'). It stays at the floor when the same word lacks that antecedent plurality ('You can just select a partition inside the drive.' alone: 0.21 vs 2.66 once a plural is introduced), when the phrase names a class rather than an instance ('it was one of the most serious': 0.14 vs 0.76 for 'the most serious happened'), and when the whole set is quantified or exactly counted ('all/each/both of them', 'two of them', 'three bicycles'), which drives the negative pole together with bare cardinal counts ('12 countries', 'eight rabbits').
