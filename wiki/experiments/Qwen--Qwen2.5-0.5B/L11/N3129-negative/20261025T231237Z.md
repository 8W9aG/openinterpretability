## Hypothesis

> The negative pole is the non-human side of a person-referent feature: it fires retrospectively on a token that carries no human reference, and its peak in a snippet is the most strongly non-human content word there - a concrete object, substance, plant, animal or natural phenomenon ('pan', 'clouds', 'rain', 'nature', 'rates', 'mould', 'water'), or, when the snippet has none, a numeral or punctuation mark ('=', '0', '1', sentence-final '.'). It sits at the floor, with the positive pole firing instead, when the current token names a person or a group of people - above all kinship, social-role and collective nouns ('family', 'friend', 'colleague', 'neighbour', 'teacher', 'patient', 'child', 'children', 'students') - so a snippet whose content words are all person nouns is predicted inactive (measured: 'for the family' +1.9, 'the neighbour' +1.4, 'the friend' +1.5, 'the teacher' +0.8, 'the patient' +0.7, versus 'the table' +0.0 and 'the river' +0.0), while swapping a non-human subject or object for a person in an otherwise identical frame lowers the pole (in an otherwise identical frame, 'The workshop repaired...' peaks at 0.34 and 'Her brother repaired...' at 0.11, against a scan scale of 1.19).
