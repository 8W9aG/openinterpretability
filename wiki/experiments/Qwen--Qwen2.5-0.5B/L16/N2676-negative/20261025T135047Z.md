## Hypothesis

> The negative pole is a digit-slot detector: it peaks on the token immediately before a digit token - the space, comma, decimal point or ' to' that opens a number, or an interior digit of a multi-token number - when the passage is presenting numbers as data (measurement lists, date/year chronologies, ranges, decimals, IDs, IP addresses), and it reads the accumulated numeric context rather than the local token: the same number in plain prose, or with the numbers spelled out, stays at baseline, while a repeated numeric list roughly doubles it. It does not encode which digit follows - swapping 60 for 70 in the list, or 1922 for 1975 in a date, leaves the peak identical (3.78 vs 3.78), the output direction promotes no token (Jacobian-lens kurtosis 3.2, max logit projection 0.06) and zero-ablating the pole at a peak moves next-token probabilities by KL about 1e-4 - so it is a retrospective number-context detector, not a next-token predictor; a residual sensitivity to which numbers precede (changing an earlier year 1842 to 1860 cancels the peak at a later date) shows the read is over the numeric context, not the adjacent token alone.

But the first batch came out of the tin the colour of honey, and the smell carried out into the street, and a woman walking a small dog stopped and asked whether she was open yet.

On the first morning she unlocked the door at dawn, set the radio to a station that played old songs, and began with the cake.

Preheat the oven to 180 C. Cream 250 g of butter with 200 g of caster sugar until pale, then beat in 4 eggs, one at a time. Fold in 300 g of plain flour, 2 teaspoons of baking powder and a pinch of salt, then stir in 120 ml of milk and 1 teaspoon of vanilla. Divide between 2 tins and bake for 25 to 30 minutes, until a skewer comes out clean. Cool for 10 minutes in the tin, then turn out onto a rack.

By eight the shop smelled of caramel and the first customers were at the window, pointing at the trays. She sold out before noon, wrote the day's takings in a notebook, and sat on the back step with a cup of tea while the street filled up with people.” (activation 0.444)
