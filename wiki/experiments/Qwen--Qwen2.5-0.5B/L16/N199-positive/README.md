**Latest status:** VERIFIED (2026-10-20)

> The positive pole fires on a token that is a word-piece rather than a word: a single alphabetic letter standing as a symbol - a variable ('Let q be', 'Suppose y equals'), an option/label/grade letter ('Option C was', 'Plan K', 'Grade A eggs', 'Class B', 'Figure C'), a judge's initial ('Smith, J.,'), a list or enumeration marker ('a, b, and c', 'a) b) c)'), or a letter being named ('the letter d') - at roughly 0.4-2.5 of max, and, more weakly and only when the piece is locally unpredicted, the first piece of a longer word that the tokenizer splits off ('Men|strual', ' rest|orative', ' ad|vertising', ' i|Frame', ' C|QL'). It stays near zero on complete multi-letter words even when they are surprising or collocation-breaking ('banana', 'reverence', 'zebra', 'wrestling', which drive the negative pole instead), on digits, punctuation, code and non-English text, and on single letters whose continuation is already fixed by a conventional term ('Section B describes', 'Vitamin B deficiency', 'A:' answer markers).
