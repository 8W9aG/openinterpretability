> The negative pole detects an explicit scalar comparison: the comparative degree of a property, i.e. analytic 'more/less/fewer + X', synthetic or irregular '-er' comparatives (better, faster, greater, higher, simpler, broader, worse) and the same comparative morpheme in other languages (Chinese 更/更加, Spanish más, French plus, Dutch -der), staying active across the comparison clause (degree adverbs such as 'significantly'/'even', the compared dimension, 'than', 'compared to/with X, Y is ...', and the pivot 'on the other hand' when the passage is already comparing two items). It is inactive for superlatives (best, most, least), plain intensifiers (very, extremely, quite, fairly) and bare adjectives; peaks sit on the comparison-bearing token itself rather than the token before it, and the pole's output direction writes against the analytic comparative marker family ('more'/更/更多/更加), the trigger-suppression signature of a retrospective neuron (a minority of peaks on code '=' assignments and on 'but' after a comparison is not yet explained by this account).
