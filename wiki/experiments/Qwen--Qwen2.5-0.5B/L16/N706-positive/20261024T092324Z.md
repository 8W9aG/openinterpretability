## Hypothesis

> The pole fires while a passage is enumerating or contrasting geographic and national referents — country, continent, city or river names, national currencies, and nationality adjectives — in a report, listing, survey, institutional or competition frame: its peak lands on the connective that joins the names (the commas and 'and' of 'The report mentioned France, Spain, Portugal and Italy', the 'to' of 'from the West Indies to North Africa', the ' and' of 'India and Australia') or on the first subword of a multi-token place or nationality name that follows another national referent ('the U.S. and one in the U.K.', 'Scottish emigrants to New Zealand', 'Brazilian ... a Mongolian fighter', 'AVUSTURYA' inside a country-by-country list), so the peak is rarely on the name itself; it needs a geographic referent already on its left, which is why ', and' in 'France and Alice' peaks (1.92) while the same ', and' in 'Alice and France' stays low (0.76). It sits at its floor (about 0.14-0.6 of the 4.51 scale) on a single place name mentioned in passing ('France is a country', 'one recent example, in the U.K.'), on coordinate lists of people, animals, products, abstract nouns, numbers or subnational regions ('Alice, Bob, Carol and Dave' 0.55, 'apples, pears, plums and grapes' 0.14, 'Ohio, Kansas, Nevada and Oregon' 0.48), on a bare geographic pair in a static-relation frame such as 'the border between France and Spain' (0.17), and on plain prose with no geographic referents.
