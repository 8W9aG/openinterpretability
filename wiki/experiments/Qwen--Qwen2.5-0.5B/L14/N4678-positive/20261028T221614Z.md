## Hypothesis

> The pole fires retrospectively on the token that completes a conventional time reference used as a clause-level time adjunct: the '.m' piece of an a.m./p.m. clock time ('The shop shuts at 5 p.m.', 'open from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.'), a weekday or schedule word that fixes a recurring time or a deadline ('on weekdays', 'Tuesday through Sunday', 'by Friday'), or a period word inside an explicit comparison ('compared with last month', 'than the previous quarter'). It is not a fixed-collocation or number+unit detector: the same time word stays near baseline in attributive use ('the 6 p.m. train was cancelled', 'the 11 a.m. train'), as do vague time words ('noon', 'later'), bare durations that are the object of a verb ('lasted 45 minutes'), full numeric dates ('on 12 June 2019') and non-temporal measures ('weighed 3 kg', '1200 metres long').
