## Hypothesis

> The positive pole is a word-level detector for the concrete hardware-and-materials vocabulary of technical prose: it peaks on the nouns for casings, fittings and mechanical parts (casing, enclosure, housing, shell, chassis, cabinet, seal, bearing, screw, lock, spring, frame, filter, tank, pump, motor, generator, door) and on their material words (metal, plastic, aluminum, concrete), reaching several times its 0.34 scan maximum on that word alone - 'The equipment was sealed in a metal casing' 5.9 on 'casing', 'The sensor is enclosed in a plastic housing' 5.6 on 'housing' - while the same frame with the everyday near-synonym leaves the word empty ('We replaced the casing' 4.75 vs 'We replaced the container' 0.00; 'We replaced the motor' 0.26 vs 'We replaced the engine' 0.00). It reads the word rather than preparing the next one (the Jacobian and logit lenses share no token, kurtosis 2.9, and zeroing the pole moves the next-token distribution by KL 3e-5 with no concentrated target), and the words themselves stay at zero for everyday objects, people, animals, food and abstract nouns (car, table, cup, book, dog, cat, bread, idea, policy) and for the common mechanism/material words engine, machine, valve, pipe, gear, wheel, steel, wood and glass. Caveat for labelling: the scan's corpus is 9-21-token sentences and never contained this vocabulary, so its 0.34 maximum is the pole's shallow SwiGLU double-negative background, which ordinary prose also reaches (0.05-0.3, often above the scan-scale active threshold); the reliable signature is a large peak on the hardware/material word itself, not any single token of a plain sentence.
