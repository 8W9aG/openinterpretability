## Hypothesis

> The negative pole tracks forward-looking time reference: it peaks where a clause places an event after the narrative present, either through an explicit future-time expression (' soon', ' shortly', ' imminent', ' coming', ' upcoming', ' next week/year/spring', ' in the near future', ' eventually', ' before long', ' any day now') or through a prospective predicate whose complement is still unrealized ('is set to', 'is due to', 'is expected to', 'is scheduled to/for', 'plans/intends to', 'is going to', 'is about to'). It returns to baseline when the same vocabulary describes past or completed events ('arrived soon after', 'was released in June', 'had expected to win', 'was about to leave'), present states or habituals ('leaves at six every morning'), present-tense epistemic complements ('is said/believed/reported/known to be'), and ordinary infinitival 'to' ('wants to leave'), while a bare 'will' plus a lexical verb with no explicit future-time expression is only a weak cue (typically 0.1-1.2 on the pole's 7.5 scan scale).
