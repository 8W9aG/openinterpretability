## Hypothesis

> The negative pole is a graded semantic detector for temptation and enticement: it fires hardest on the word that names an allurement or impulse pulling someone toward an action they might resist or yield to — tempt/tempted/tempting/temptation (strength 5–15), urge (5–6), and entice/enticement, seduce/seductive, lure, alluring (2–7) — and, less reliably (1.5–6), on the adjacent vocabulary of yielding to or holding out against that pull (resist, give in, yield, succumb, can't help but, devil, sins, Satan); it is lexical-semantic, not propositional, so a mention fires whether the clause urges, forbids or denies the temptation ('Don't do it — this can be tempting'; 'They are tempted by nothing'). Bare function words, ordinary volition and purpose stay near zero (sentence peaks: 'I want to go' 0.42, 'He needs to go' 0.10, 'They plan to contest the ruling' 0.09, 'John and Mary travelled to Paris in spring' 0.16, 'He sat on the mat' 0.04), the propensity rim (tend, tendency, desire, inclined, attracted, appealing, craving; 0.7–2.7) is not a reliable positive, and a following 'to'/'toward' peaks only when it is the complement of one of these pull words ('we tend to', 'an urge to', 'a tendency to').
