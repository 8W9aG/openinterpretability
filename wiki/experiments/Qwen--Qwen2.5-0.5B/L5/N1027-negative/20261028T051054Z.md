## Hypothesis

> The pole fires on the punctuation that closes a written unit: the sentence-final full stop, exclamation mark or question mark, and above all the fused forms that also break the line ('.' < '.\n' < '.\n\n'), which is why the same closure is amplified several-fold at the edge of an enclosed block - a period inside a /* ... */ comment line measures 2.0-2.9 against 0.28 in plain prose, the line-leading ' *' and '@' of a doc comment 0.8-3.5, and the word or two that open a quotation right after a double quote 0.6-3.2 ('"The bus', '"I am'), though not for every opener ('"The plan' stays at 0.00). It is not about topic, and not about quotation as such: plain unquoted prose peaks on its sentence-final stop (ten of ten such sentences measured 0.23-0.30, clear of the 0.174 active threshold), while the first token of any text (0.004-0.012), the content words inside an unclosed clause, and a sentence whose closing stop is deleted stay near zero, so a stop-less version of a sentence reaches only about half the peak of the same sentence with its stop.

- **Activating tokens:** `.`, `.\\n`, `.\\n\\n`, `!`, `?`, ` *`, ` @`, `"The`, `"I`, ` are`
- **Mechanism claimed:** retrospective
- **Max activation (scan):** 0.6970
