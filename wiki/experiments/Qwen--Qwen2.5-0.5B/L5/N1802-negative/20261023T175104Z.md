## Hypothesis

> The negative pole fires on the word that takes alternatives out of a proposition the preceding clause has just set up - the exception word 'except', the restrictive focus particle 'only/Only', and the clause-level adversative 'but' - and its strength tracks how strongly that preceding clause establishes the set being restricted. 'except' peaks at 2.5-4.8 when the clause first asserts sameness or near-identity ('the result is the same as before except the name', 4.8; 'almost identical to a regular result set, except that ...', 3.7; the scan's own 'the same as a regular SQL result set, except that ...', 3.0), gives roughly half that after 'different' ('The result is different except the name', 0.9), and only 0.2-1.0 after a plain quantified statement ('All the students passed except John', 0.2; 'Everyone was there except me', 1.0, versus 0.05 for 'with me'); 'only/Only' fires when it restricts a quantity, condition or instrument inside a running sentence ('...advisor, only 6 per cent cited debt', 3.7-4.6; 'Fade out, only if the URL ...', 3.1; 'using only a metre stick', 2.2) but not as an attributive or idiomatic modifier or at the start of a text ('the only map that works', 'if only', 'only just', all at or below 0.15); 'but' fires only as a clause-level contrast, strongest after 'the same ...' ('The same thing happened, but nobody noticed', 2.8) or a first-person/agentive claim about a definite thing ('I like this map, but ...', 2.4-2.6), and is weak after a bare copular property clause ('The map is large, but ...', 0.1). Near-synonyms never fire (excluding, apart from, other than, besides, save for; yet, though, however, although; just, merely, solely), phrasal and correlative uses of 'but' stay at baseline ('everyone but John', 'not X but Y', 'not only ... but also', 'anything but', 'last but not least', 'but for'), the clause after the marker has no effect on the peak (it is already fixed before that clause is read; 'but I found it easy' and 'but I found it difficult' both give exactly 2.394), and the pole's few other fires ('.accept', '$provide', 'if' in Kotlin) are isolated singles at 1.0-1.5 rather than a token family.
