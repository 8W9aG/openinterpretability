## Hypothesis

> L5/N1425+ fires on the punctuation token that closes a clause the text asserts in its own voice - the sentence-final '.' (including the hard line break of line-wrapped prose) and the ':' that introduces an apposition or specification ('looked at perspective-taking: the degree to which ...') - and its strength is passage-gated rather than set by the punctuation: a boundary is ~7x more likely to fire when the previous sentence boundary of the same passage fired (0.30 vs a 0.04 base rate), so 98% of '.' tokens sit near zero (mean -0.02) while every sentence-final period of a firing passage spikes to 0.7-1.8. At that identical closing position it collapses when the clause is reported from someone else ('The director said the clinic offers ...' 0.02 vs 'The director saw the clinic offers ...' 0.70), is enclosed in quotation marks, or closes a list item rather than a sentence - the negative pole takes over on the tokens just after a speech-reporting verb (' said' 0.38) - and the Jacobian-lens token list is incoherent (kurtosis 3.6, ' \n\n\n' / '/bower' / Chinese fragments), so no single upcoming token is prepared.
