First audit of this pole (random draw, audit seed 3944844843; 200k fineweb-edu tokens as
512-token windows; max 8.28). The **literal armed-conflict register** claim came out
**NEEDS_REVISION at 0.68**: 12/13 hard negatives (specificity 0.92) but only 3/7 holdouts
(sensitivity 0.43), 9/9 counterfactuals, and the never-active baseline (0.75) ahead of the
hypothesis (0.68). This is a sparse pole — threshold 2.07, 13 of 4000 held-out windows
active — and token identity is high at R² 0.38, the strongest confound in this session.

What fires is phrase-specific: `The government sent troops into the border provinces`
(5.35), the wire-style `KABUL — A roadside bomb in the capital…` (3.63) and the long
grain-warehouse passage (2.66). What fails is the interesting part: canonical military prose
does not reach the threshold — `The shelling in the northern districts of Kharkiv` scores
0.33, `The artillery barrage in the Ardennes` 1.95, `Insurgents overran the outpost` 1.62,
and `The guns fell silent at dawn over the Somme` 0.78. Meanwhile the one hard-negative
failure is comedy: `The stand-up was bombing in the second half` fires at 2.66. So the pole
is not tracking an armed-conflict register; it tracks particular frames and words
(`troops into`, `bombing in`, `roadside bomb`, `war on terror`) and does not distinguish
literal from figurative `bombing`.

The negative probes that pass are consistent with that reading: price war, war of words,
war on illiteracy, trade fair, apprentices into the warehouse, scout troops, defence
procurement, invasion of privacy, the Iraqi restaurant and Islamic art galleries all stay
low — the frames have to be of the specific conflict-phrase type, not merely military-adjacent.

Held-out explanation scores: detection 0.62 (TPR 0.25, FPR 0.00), fuzzing 0.67 (TPR 0.33),
embedding AUROC 0.64. Self-reviewed (proposer and reviewer both
`external/deepseek-flash[1m]`); the proposer measured its own counterfactuals.

Next: a `new_hypothesis` draw should test the literal/figurative contrast directly on
`bombing` and `shelling` in their non-military senses, and treat the specific frames
