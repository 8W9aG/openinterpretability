## Discussion

<!-- Free-form notes. This page is never regenerated, so edits here are kept. -->

First experiment on this pole (random draw, audit seed 1863070028; 200k fineweb-edu
tokens read as 512-token windows; max activation 12.88). The proposer claimed this is the
rare case of a **lookahead** neuron: it fires on the word closing an expositional frame
(`the problem`, `one simple factor`, `arose`) when the successor opens the explanation with
`:` or a that-complement, and both lenses rank `:` first. The review returns NEEDS_REVISION
at 0.60 with three failed gates — sensitivity 0.29, lookahead inconsistent, and the
confound baseline — and the lookahead failure is the interesting one.

**The lookahead measurement disagrees with the proposer's ablation evidence.** The claimed
mechanism was measured retrospective (KL = 0.00177; promoted tokens `:`, ` this`, ` in`), so
the gate fails even though per-position zero-ablation moved almost only the `:` logit
(P(`:`) 0.65→0.55, 0.57→0.43, 0.27→0.14) and the record's causal metrics show large target
effects (median ±0.38 on the claimed tokens). The proposer's explanation is that the
batch-mean concentration criterion is diluted by long prompts; the protocol takes the batch
measurement as authoritative. This is a genuine protocol-vs-tool tension worth a dedicated
follow-up (a pre-registered single-position lookahead test), not something to settle here.

The probe results tell the other half. Only 2/7 fresh holdouts fire: new frame words before
colons reach 1.5–2.5 against the 3.22 threshold, while calibration examples reached
5.0–12.9. And a failed negative shows the frame boundary is too narrow: `A problem arose
when the battery died` (5.253) fires when the successor is a `when`-clause, not an
explanation opener. With five holdouts inactive the never-active baseline scores 0.82.

Proposer and reviewer were the same model, so the record is self-reviewed.
