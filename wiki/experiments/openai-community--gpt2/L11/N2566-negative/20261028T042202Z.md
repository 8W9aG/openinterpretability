Second GPT-2 cap-artifact audit (random draw, seed 1560711181; 200k fineweb-edu tokens as
128-token windows). The scan maximum is **0.1700408**, the `gelu_new` cap, and the proposer
traced the pole to the same artifact as L7/N1883−: this neuron is live — its positive pole
fires on 7.5% of tokens with peaks to 3.76 and a median pre-activation of −1.07 — but the
negative pole is `-min(gelu_new(pre), 0)`, and its tokens keep landing in the cap band, so
120/120 full documents peak at ≥0.999 of the cap and 88% of random windows within 1e-4 of
it. Every one of the 8 holdout genres — code, Spanish, dialogue, math, a legal clause,
Japanese, a narrative and a SKU manifest — sits at the cap (0.1699–0.1700).

The verdict is **NEEDS_REVISION at 0.62**: 8/8 holdouts and 8/8 counterfactuals pass, but
only **3/12** of the contrived short spans drop below the active threshold. ` Glory` (−0),
` polit` (0.0003) and ` Supreme Court` (0.024) stay inactive; the other nine do not —
` take` 0.154, ` took` 0.153, ` an` 0.119, ` utterly` 0.115, ` went Supreme` 0.131, and the
three-token spans 0.128–0.154, all far above the 0.0425 line (itself 25% of the cap). So the
artifact reading is right, but "inactive only for contrived 1–3 token spans" is far too
broad: most such spans are still active, and the inactive region is narrower still. The
position baseline (0.88) beats the hypothesis (0.62), which fails the confound gate because
span length alone explains the pass/fail pattern.

This record, together with [L7/N1883−](../../L7/N1883-negative/20261028T032706Z.md),
establishes the gelu-cap artifact as a real class in this registry: a VERIFIED/NEEDS_REVISION
verdict on a negative pole can describe the measurement floor rather than a learned feature.
A revision should characterize the genuinely inactive region token by token (` Glory`,
` polit`, ` Supreme Court` versus ` take`, ` an`, ` took`) rather than by span length. The
record is signed, so CI skips reproduction.
