## Hypothesis

> N358+ is a retrospective detector of closing/statement-boundary syntax in structured text: its strongest and most reliable trigger is the HTML/XML closing-tag opener - the `</` token in any merged form ('</', ' </', '></', '"></'), or the split `<` + ' /' pair - which fires at 1.2-3.5 (most often 2.0-3.5, i.e. 55-95% of this pole's max) regardless of tag name, language, or whether it occurs in real markup, code, running prose or a quoted string, and the `?` that closes a PHP-style block (`?>` after a code expression such as a call, literal or closing quote) fires at 1.2-2.6. In imperative code (Python, JS, Java, C, shell) the pole additionally fires at 0.9-2.2 on the keywords and line breaks that close statements and blocks (if, in, else, return, break, 'do', a statement-ending newline) and on '+' inside assignment expressions, whereas prose, JSON/YAML/SQL data, opening tags, self-closing `/>`, HTML comments, lone `<`/`>`/`/`, and short one-line expressions stay near zero.
