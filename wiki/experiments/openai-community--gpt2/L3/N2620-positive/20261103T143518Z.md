## Hypothesis

> The pole detects the English partitive construction: it fires when the preposition 'of' is directly governed by a quantity, fraction, portion or superlative expression - the quantifiers most, some, many, all, much, more, less, few, several, any, one/two/three, half, a third, a quarter, percent/%, majority, bulk, whole, rest, total - or by a superlative adjective (oldest, tallest, highest, richest, fastest, best), and on the first word of the set being quantified over (the, these, those, them, it, us, this, my, his, their, our, its), peaking highest when that word is a relative pronoun: 'of which', 'of whom' and 'of whose' fire at full strength even when the preceding noun is ordinary ('the book, of which I have three copies', 'the people of whom we spoke'), and 'following' in the fixed phrase 'the following X' also fires. It stays at zero on 'of' governed by an ordinary content noun (the colour of the sky, the level of the water, the weight of the package), on measure nouns (a cup of coffee, a piece of cake), on verb or adjective 'of' (because of, made of, full of, spoke of), on quantifiers with no 'of' (most students, all the delegates, much has been written) and on other prepositions before a relative pronoun (in which, to whom, from which); with a weak head (each, both, none, part, number, amount, lots) it clears the activity line only when the complement begins with these/those/them/it, and a bare superlative with no following 'of' gives only a partial, usually sub-threshold response.
