There were receipts, an old passport, a folded map of a country that no longer existed under that name, and a stack of newsletters from a birding society that her grandmother had never once mentioned. Marta read a few of them and laughed out loud at a complaint about the price of seed.

She worked through the box until the room grew dark. Somewhere above her a door moved, though no one was upstairs, and she told herself it was the wind. She thought about the archive in the city, the building with the green lamps where she had spent a month of her twenties reading other people's letters, and how she had always believed that a thing written down was a thing that could be saved. It was a comforting idea and she had never quite given it up, even after the archive flooded, even after the boxes came back stained and unreadable and the staff apologised in a letter that ran to two pages.

When she finally stood, her knees ached. She carried the box to the car and locked the house, and on the step she turned and looked back at the front window, where the dust she had disturbed was still turning slowly in the last of the light. Mrs. Adler had left a note on the gatepost, written in a careful, upright hand, with a number to call about the roof. Marta read it twice. The cover of the piano stayed closed, and she thought that was probably right.” (activation 1.697)
