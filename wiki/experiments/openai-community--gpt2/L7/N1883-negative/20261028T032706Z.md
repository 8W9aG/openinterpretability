This is not a feature record. The audit (random draw, seed 1608197416; 200k fineweb-edu
tokens as 128-token windows) found a pole whose maximum is **0.1700408** — exactly the
`gelu_new` global minimum, reached by 3,071 of the 3,072 L7 neurons on this corpus — and the
proposer traced it to the definition of pole strength: the negative pole is
`-min(gelu_new(pre-activation), 0)`, so a neuron that is silent on 88% of tokens shows the
gelu floor on almost every snippet. 97% of the scan's snippets peak within 1e-4 of that
ceiling and the top-20 examples are float32 ties one ULP apart.

The claim that follows — "predicted active for any running text of ≥8 tokens; predicted
inactive only for a span every one of whose tokens flips the pre-activation positive" — came
out **VERIFIED 0.96**: 12/13 hard negatives, 7/7 holdouts, 7/7 counterfactuals, against a
position baseline of 0.54. All twelve short all-silencer-token spans score exactly −0
(` In past could might won`, ` seems looks easily easier`, …) and every running text sits at
the ceiling: code 0.17004, Spanish 0.16998, a math sentence 0.16993, a flipper-dense English
sentence 0.17004, a 600-token lighthouse passage 0.17004, a dialogue 0.17003, and even a
15-token span with only two non-silencer words 0.1569. The single failure is precisely the
edge the reviewer engineered: a 10-token span mixing `names name` into the silencer list
scores 0.046, just over the 0.0425 threshold, because one non-silencer token is enough to
reach the ceiling.

The verdict is real but describes a **measurement artifact**, not a learned feature: this
pole carries no information beyond "this neuron did not fire". It should be read as a dead
pole in the registry, and it is a useful counterexample for anyone treating a high
balanced-accuracy VERIFIED as evidence of a feature. Held-out explanation scores are
degenerate by construction (detection and fuzzing 1.00 with no inactive windows; AUROC
undefined).
