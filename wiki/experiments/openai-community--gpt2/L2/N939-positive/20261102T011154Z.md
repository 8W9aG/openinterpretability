One fisherman pointed out that the fleet had lost eleven days of work the previous season and could not absorb another spring of waiting. A retired teacher asked why the board had waited until the berths were nearly unusable before ordering a survey at all. The board's chair, who has held the seat for six years, defended the timetable. She said the engineering firm had been chosen through a competitive process and that the draft had arrived within the agreed cost and schedule. She also noted that the wall option carried an environmental review that could take two or three years, and that the state agency responsible for the estuary had already signalled doubts about any hard structure in the channel. The firm's lead engineer, speaking after her, agreed that the wall was the most durable answer but added that it would shift erosion further downstream, where several private docks stand on pilings. Those owners, he said, had not been consulted and would likely object. He proposed a compromise: a seasonal dig for the next three years, with a portion of the annual cost set aside for a full wall design, so that the board could revisit the question once the design and permits were ready. That proposal carried by a single vote. The board directed the engineer to prepare bid documents before the end of April so that work could begin after the ferry schedule eased in June. It also created a small reserve, funded by a surcharge on mooring fees, to pay for the wall study. Whether the compromise will hold is unclear. The fishing fleet wants faster action; the dock owners want assurances that any wall will not worsen the current at their pilings; and the state agency has asked for a monitoring plan that the town has not yet paid for. The engineer's report closes with a warning that the channel will keep filling no matter which option the board chooses, and that the cost of doing nothing will be paid, sooner or later, by whoever needs to use the harbor. The town has basically bought itself three more years to argue.” (activation 0.551)
