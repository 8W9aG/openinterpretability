## Hypothesis

> Neuron 2800 detects non-Latin script: tokens carrying non-ASCII UTF-8 bytes (Greek, Hangul and most other non-Latin characters; 91% of such tokens keep the neuron on) drive the neuron on and hold its negative pole - the GELU dip, which opens only while the neuron is off - near zero, whereas ASCII-only text leaves the neuron off and puts the pole at the 0.170 dip ceiling. Concretely, any Latin-script passage is predicted active (English prose peaks at 0.166-0.170, and even stopword lists, digits, punctuation, code and URLs reach 0.10-0.17), text written in Greek or Hangul is predicted inactive (peak about 0.012, ASCII words embedded in it included), while Cyrillic, Arabic, Thai and CJK passages are predicted active (0.05-0.17) because their UTF-8 lead bytes drive the unit on only weakly and some of their characters still land in the dip.
