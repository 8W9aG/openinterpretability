## Hypothesis

> L2/N1424+ marks the end of a written-English sentence: it fires on the token that closes a clause - a sentence-final '.'/'?'/'!'/';' or a newline between sentences - and not inside the clause (',' reads ~0.10, ':' ~0.02, and the points of abbreviations, decimals and digit-final clauses stay at 0.00-0.37, e.g. 'a.m.', 'U.S.', '3.9.', '...for 5.'), so the trigger is a boundary token that follows an ordinary word. At a genuine word-final boundary its size tracks the closing word: 0.6-0.9 of max after complete, common abstract/institutional head nouns and clause-final adverbials ('...interest rates.' 0.82, '...the situation.' 0.76, '...played anyway.' 0.76, '...the decision.' 0.63, '...was important.' 0.62), 0.25-0.5 after concrete nouns, adjectives and particles ('...the kitchen.' 0.25, '...was in.' 0.33), near 0 after rare technical words ('...glucose.' 0.02), and highest of all on a newline between sentences (1.4-1.7, above the scanned max); the first token of any prompt also reads a fixed 0.31-0.49 floor whatever it is, a layer-2 position-0 artifact rather than part of the feature.
