## Hypothesis

> L9/N1018+ detects the causative/resultative frame of 'make': it fires on the verb 'make' in any inflection ('makes', 'make', 'made', 'making') when that verb takes a (pro)noun object plus a predicative complement that states the object's resulting property, state or experience - an adjective ('makes it convenient', 'makes the Avenues unique', 'makes it mandatory', 'make it all the more interesting'), a mental-state or perception verb ('made me wonder', 'make you feel', 'makes it seem so far away', 'make your food taste sweet'), or 'to be' - or, with no separate complement, in the two degree idioms 'make (a lot of / little / no) sense' and 'make (little / no / big) difference'. It is the frame, not the word form and not the word: all four inflections reach near the pole's maximum of 2.69 in this frame (the 20 top peaks contain 7 ' makes', 6 ' make' and 1 ' made', and ' make' has more firing instances in absolute terms than ' makes' despite its lower mean), whereas the same token stays near zero in physical creation ('make a cake', 'made him dinner', 'makes cars in three factories'), in light-verb action nouns ('make a decision', 'make a call', 'make a mistake'), and in fixed, phrasal or commercial uses ('make money', 'make it to the party', 'make up', 'the make and model'), and near-synonymous causatives do not fire ('render it mandatory', 'deem X Y', 'regard X as Y' sit at 0.24-0.40 mean, 9-15% of max). The peak sits on 'make' itself and spills onto the object and degree words (' little', ' a', ' it', ' you'), 29% of input attribution lies more than 16 tokens back (the frame's subject), and with Jacobian kurtosis 3.12 and 40% lens overlap the output promotes no coherent next-token set, so this is retrospective frame detection rather than lookahead. Known residue this claim does not cover: about 30% of the top peaks (both ' El' peaks are 'Elmore', both ' j' peaks are 'jpg'/'jellies', plus ' of' in 'Idylls of the King' and ' to' in 'device to enhance') look like a separate weak sensitivity to BPE word-onset fragments in name, URL and formatting contexts (the confound list's '@', ' www', 'EL', ' u'), and should be probed separately.

## Discussion

**NEEDS_REVISION 0.77** — specificity 0.692 (9/13), sensitivity 0.857 (6/7), and **counterfactual reproduction 0.125 (1/8)**. The frame claim is the problem: every pair that changed the complement while holding the subject failed to move the pole, because on short prompts the resultative bases often did not fire at all. The token-identity baseline is 0.742 against the hypothesis's 0.775 — the claim barely beats matching trigger tokens.

**The counterfactual failure is the finding.** Predicted vs observed Δ: `made him comfortable` vs `made him dinner` 0.50/**0.00** (the resultative base itself reads −0.04, i.e. no firing at all), `makes cars cheaper` vs `makes cars in three factories` 0.55/**0.00** (base 0.20), `made the merger profitable` vs `made a call about` 0.45/**0.01** (base 0.02), `make passengers angry` vs `make headlines` 0.55/**0.00** (both 1.83 — the counterfactual fires as hard as the base), `make it mandatory` vs `renders it mandatory` 0.45/**−0.05** (both ~1.1 — ` renders` fires too, contradicting the near-synonym exclusion). Only the one same-frame control passed.

**What actually separates the firing cases is the subject, not the complement.** The bases that fired have non-agentive, abstract or event subjects — `The size of the budget made me wonder` 1.23, `Long delays make passengers angry` 1.83 — while the bases with agentive subjects stayed at the floor — `The nurse made him comfortable` −0.04, `The board made the merger profitable` 0.02, `The company makes cars cheaper` 0.20, `They make the harbour tours popular` 0.60. The scan's own top examples fit this: `It also makes sense`, `meat, birds or flour makes little difference`, `which makes it convenient`, `that makes it seem`, `The success of this project made me wonder`, `diversity makes the Avenues unique`, `what does this make everyone's character` — all abstract/event/pronoun causers. The revision should replace "causative/resultative frame" with **causal-explanatory `make` whose subject is a non-agentive cause**, i.e. the "what makes X Y" construction.

**The one counterexample to that refinement is itself the creation sense**: `The Ridgeway Bearing plant … makes the sealed roller bearings` fires 0.963 with an inanimate subject and a created object — so animacy of the subject is not sufficient; it is closer than the frame, not perfect.

**The other three false positives are the acknowledged residue and fixed `make`**: `catalog_scan.jpg` (no `make` token at all) 1.101, `made up at the wedding` 0.875, and objectless `make sure the valve is closed` 0.783 — the last is a fixed expression with a complement but no noun object, which the claim excluded but the pole fires on. **The one holdout miss** is `Make it clear to everyone…` at 0.300: sentence-initial imperative `Make` does not fire even with object + adjective, so the construction is not purely syntactic.

**Explanation scores are correspondingly poor**: detection 0.56 (TPR 0.12, FPR 0.00), fuzzing 0.50 (TPR 0.00), AUROC 0.46, on 780/4,000 active windows — a judge given the frame explanation marks almost nothing active. Causal pull is weak (sufficiency 0.28 / necessity 0.44 percentile, median KL 2.5e-05), unlike the chunk-detector pole found in the previous draw.

**Revision**: condition on a non-agentive cause subject ("what makes X Y"), keep the `make sense`/`make a difference` idioms, add `make sure`, move `render/deem/regard` to unexplained (they do fire), and decide whether the `Elmore`/`jpg` fragment sensitivity is part of this pole or separate — it fires 1.10 on a filename alone, which no frame claim covers.
