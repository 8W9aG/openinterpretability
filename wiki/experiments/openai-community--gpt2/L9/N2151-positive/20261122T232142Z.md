## Hypothesis

> N2151+ fires retrospectively at the boundary token that closes a fronted setup phrase in reader-facing instructional, how-to or help text — usually the comma after a purpose, condition, prerequisite or pointer ('To make X more Y,', 'For more information,', 'If you need/want…,', 'Before you start,', 'After you finish,', 'When you are ready,'), the connective 'so' after a hedged situation that sets up a recommendation ('It may take a while, so…'), or, more weakly, a step word that opens the next step after a completed instruction ('Then', 'Now', 'Finally'); its output promotes the imperative verb that typically follows ('try', 'check', 'make'). The framing, not the boundary's words, is what it tracks: swapping the content noun or subordinator ('information'→'cheese', 'If you need'→'Because you need') or the following clause's mood leaves the peak unchanged, whereas the same boundary in narrative or factive text ('Yesterday,', 'After the meeting,', 'Because it was raining,') and list, appositive or coordinate-clause commas stay near zero.
