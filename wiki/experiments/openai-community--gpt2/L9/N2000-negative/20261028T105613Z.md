## Discussion

**Fourth `gelu_new` cap-artifact audit (seeds 2166433489 / 1669756692), and the cleanest since L2/N1944−: VERIFIED 1.00** — 13/13 hard negatives, 7/7 holdouts, 8/8 counterfactuals, lookahead consistent, all gates passed. As with L7/N1883−, L11/N2566− and L2/N1944−, the "feature" is the measurement: the negative pole is `max(-gelu_new(z), 0)`, capped at the GELU minimum **0.1700408**, and the scan maximum is exactly that constant (0.170040756). The neuron is alive in the other direction (positive pole sparse tail to 3.215), so this is not a dead unit — the negative pole is simply its pre-activation dip.

**The counterfactuals are the strongest evidence**: all eight reproduced, four of them to three decimals (0.751/0.751, 0.826/0.826, 0.796/0.796, 0.670/0.670), because the prompts are exact prefixes of scanned 128-token windows and the pole is deterministic given the position. The two zero controls also reproduce exactly, and the same-length control landed at 0.000 against the predicted −0.030 (inside tolerance). A one-token deletion that removes a cap token moves the peak by 0.75–0.83 of scale; adding or deleting text after a cap token cannot move it because the ceiling is already reached.

**The probe set draws the boundary exactly at sequence length 1.** Every single-token prompt — ` location`, ` to`, ` of`, ` them`, ` my`, ` us`, ` land`, ` are`, ` be`, `7`, `?`, `A`, ` de` — reads 0.0221–0.0240, far below the 0.0425 threshold, and every multi-token prompt reads 0.1690–0.1700, including a 15-token run of the three "flat" tokens (` are as be` repeated), a spaced numeral sequence, Spanish prose and source code. I confirmed this myself on all 20 probes: 20/20 agree with the claim, and at position 0 the pre-activation is z ≈ −2.33 for every token identity (range −2.306 to −2.342) — the floor is a property of the *detached first position*, not of the word. In context ` are` reaches the cap (0.169), which is why its scan mean of 0.045 is not a token-level fact.

**This is an artifact record, not a feature record.** The verified claim is equivalent, on any text, to "the sequence has more than one token": the confound baseline `position` also scores 1.00 (the gate passes on a tie, which the policy allows), and 22.5% of tokens stay below the cap only through their pre-activation leaving the dip (z ≥ 0 or z ≲ −2), which no text-level feature predicts. The record's value is the boundary itself — position 0 is the only below-cap class decidable from text, and 2–9-token spans are explicitly undecidable.

**Explanation scores are the best recorded for a GPT-2 pole in this loop**: detection 1.00 (TPR 1.00; no FPR because all 4000 held-out windows are active — correct for a pole that fires on 77% of tokens) and fuzzing 0.84 (TPR 0.84). They are high because the claim says "any multi-token running text is active", which is true here. Causal pull is negligible (sufficiency 0.50 / necessity 0.77 percentile, KL ~1e-5, no co-activation partners met the lift threshold), consistent with the pole being uninformative about the neuron's function.

**Follow-ups worth running, if drawn**: the positive pole is the informative one here (max 3.215, 90% exact zeros) and the planner's `opposite_pole` draw is the natural next step; a sibling-model check (other GPT-2 checkpoints) would confirm the 0.1700408 cap is universal.
