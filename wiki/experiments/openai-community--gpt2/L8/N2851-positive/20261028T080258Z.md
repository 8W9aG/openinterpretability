## Discussion

<!-- Free-form notes. This page is never regenerated, so edits here are kept. -->

First audit of this pole (random draw, audit seed 4190825423; 200k fineweb-edu tokens as
128-token windows; max 2.59). The **mid-word continuation** claim — peaks on the token that
continues a word the previous token began (the second BPE piece of a split spelling, or the
hyphen of a hyphenated compound), while the first piece, the same strings as whole tokens,
and single-token words stay near zero, with peak size following the fragment pair rather
than meaning — came out **NEEDS_REVISION at 0.71**: 13/13 hard negatives, 3/7 holdouts, 7/7
counterfactuals. Sensitivity failed, and the never-active baseline (0.79) beats the
hypothesis because most probes are inactive.

Specificity is perfect: whole tokens `ad`, `on`, `Ed`, `Gettysburg`, `consonants`, `wine`,
`ended`, numeric ranges `9-5`, spaced dashes, bullet lists, `ad hoc`, the King Arthur topic
and a court sentence all sit at 0.00–0.55, under the 0.647 threshold. Positives that fire:
`She took her medicine` 0.80, the `Press release` county-archive passage 1.50, `The tenant is
liable for any damage` 1.89.

The failures split usefully. `The minister spoke` (0.00), `The gardener was trimming`
(0.00) and `Saladin and his army` (0.38) are probably words GPT-2 keeps in a single token —
which the claim correctly predicts stay silent, so those three are likely probe
mis-specifications rather than misses. The genuine miss is the hyphenated compounds:
`A last-minute change to the cross-country route` reaches only 0.44, so compound hyphens do
not reliably cross the threshold in running text even though the claim lists them.

Held-out explanation scores show why the judge cannot help here: detection 0.44 with
TPR 0.88 and FPR 1.00 (a 32-token window almost always contains some mid-word
continuation), fuzzing 0.65, AUROC 0.50. Self-reviewed (proposer and reviewer both
`external/deepseek-flash[1m]`, via the sonnet alias); the record is signed, so CI skips
reproduction. The revision should verify tokenisation per host word and test compound
hyphens separately.
