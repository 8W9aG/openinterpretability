## Hypothesis

> The neuron fires retrospectively on a token that continues a referent, construction, or narrative thread already established earlier in the text. Most strongly it fires on the pronoun it/It when it refers to an inanimate thing introduced earlier in the story (a seed, flower, apple tree, peanut, nut, model, razor, nose), and on the infinitive marker to completing a verb of starting or wanting (started to, wanted to). It also fires, more weakly, on other continuation tokens that resume an established thread: the auxiliaries will, would and can after an established subject, the coordinator and joining a second predicate or adjective, and the complement word how after mark down. Weaker still are nouns like story and party and the markers last and 's when they refer back to something already introduced. It does not fire on it in prepositional-object position (on it, of it), on first-mention nouns, or on content verbs and adjectives themselves.
