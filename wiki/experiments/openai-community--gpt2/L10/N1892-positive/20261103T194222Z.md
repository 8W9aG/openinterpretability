The report notes that the new trunk main on Cedar Street crossed the old gas line at a shallow angle, a condition that inspectors flagged in 2019 and that has still not been corrected. Engineers estimate the repair at a hundred and forty thousand dollars, which the department says it can absorb within the current capital budget if the schedule slips by one quarter. A secondary line along the rail corridor was relined in August; that work finished three weeks ahead of schedule, well before the autumn rains. The eastern pumping station holds a hundred and twenty thousand gallons in its concrete tank, and the report says the station set a new record for uptime this quarter, running two thousand hours without an unplanned shutdown. The western station, which is older, logged fourteen hours of downtime, most of it during a lightning storm in early July. Staffing across both stations was set at two operators per shift, and the department says a third operator will be added when the new control system goes live next spring. The report does not say when that will be. Road resurfacing accounts for the largest share of the budget. The department milled and repaved fourteen miles of arterial road this quarter, mostly on the north side, where winter frost heaves had cracked the surface. A stretch of Route 9 near the quarry was repaved twice because the first contractor used a mix that failed inspection; the department recovered the cost, and the second job was completed in eleven days. Residents on Oak Hill Road have complained for years about drainage, and the report acknowledges that a culvert there is undersized. A design study is scheduled to begin in January and would take about nine months. The report also includes a section on bridges. Of the county's ninety-one bridges, four are rated in poor condition, down from seven a year ago, and two are closed to heavy trucks until further inspection. The bridge over Mill Creek, built in 1938, will be replaced starting next summer if the state approves the matching funds. The county's share would be roughly four million dollars, drawn from a bond issue approved two years ago. At the end of the document the director thanks the crews who worked through the summer heat and notes that the department's complaint line received nine hundred calls this quarter, a figure that has held steady for three years. The report will go to the board of commissioners next month for review, and a public hearing is scheduled for the following week.
