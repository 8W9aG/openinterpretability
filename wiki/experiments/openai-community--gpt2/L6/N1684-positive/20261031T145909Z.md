## Hypothesis

> The pole is a retrospective clause-boundary detector: it fires on the linking token itself - a comma, or a clause-joining connective such as ' and', ' but', ' while', ' although', ' yet', ' as' or ' so' - when the tokens before it already form a complete subject-predicate clause and the prefix leaves the model expecting a new clause rather than more of the same noun phrase ('She opened the door and' 0.97, 'in a kingdom far away,' 1.07), and it stays at zero where the same tokens join sub-clausal material ('the cat and the dog', 'two cups of flour, one egg, and', 'I like cats and', 'The man, who was tall,', ' or', and sentence-final '.'). The peak is bit-identical whatever follows the boundary (1.063 for 'and stared', 'and on the floor' and 'and the dog barked'), so it encodes 'a finished clause is being linked to a continuation', not any particular upcoming token, with a small unexplained offshoot on high-frequency 'can't + verb' stems ('can't believe' 0.70, 'can't wait' 1.17, 'can't help' 0.67).
