## Hypothesis

> The positive pole is a retrospective word detector for the vocabulary of specialized professional, technical and institutional domains: it fires on field, sector and discipline nouns (finance, education, healthcare, engineering, chemistry, linguistics, history, law, medicine, retail, marketing, agriculture, industry, sports) and on the relational adjectives and technical nouns of those domains (medical, clinical, financial, economic, environmental, cognitive, behavioral, molecular, immune, actuarial, photovoltaic, fiscal, systolic; health, market, credit, tax, insurance, employment, cancer, diabetes, security), peaking on that word at roughly 0.2-3.8 against the scan's 0.49 maximum and most strongly inside multi-word technical or institutional terms ('Financial Surveillance Section', 'cognitive neuroscience', 'mental health problems'). It is not a next-token predictor (Jacobian-lens kurtosis 3.1, zero lens overlap, and ablating the pole moves the next-token distribution by KL 6e-5 with no concentrated target), and it stays at exactly zero on everyday narrative vocabulary (table, cup, kitchen, cat, bread, kettle, lantern, bicycle, umbrella), generic abstract nouns (idea, question, reason, time, world), generic adjectives (important, various) and person/role nouns (professor, consultant, surgeon, cardiologist, lawyer, engineer), even when those name the same field; strength is context-modulated, so a domain word in an ordinary frame can fall toward the 0.12 active threshold while the same word in a technical term reaches several times the scan maximum.
