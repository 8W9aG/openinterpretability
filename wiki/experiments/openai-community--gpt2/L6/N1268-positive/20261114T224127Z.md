## Hypothesis

> The pole is a geographic place-name detector: it fires (typically 1.2-2.5 of its 2.51 scale, weakest around 0.3-1.0) on the tokens of a proper name that denotes a place - a country, US state or province, county, region or city - above all when that name is doing location work, either in a location phrase ('in the United States', 'in the United Kingdom', 'She lives in Scotland') or as the region in the comma appositive 'City, Region' ('Newport, Rhode Island', 'Boise, Idaho', 'Jackman, Maine', 'Ajaccio, Corsica'). It stays at the near-zero floor for ordinary prose, for non-place names and appositives ('Smith, John', 'the company, Microsoft', 'the novel, Moby Dick'), for place names used attributively ('the Colorado river', 'the Rhode Island library') and for list frames of unrelated nouns; the one non-place token that fires on its own is the legal formula 'herein'.
