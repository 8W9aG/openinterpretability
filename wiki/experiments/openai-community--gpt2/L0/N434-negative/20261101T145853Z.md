For years they argued about the road, the drains, the price of mooring, and never quite settled any of it. A visitor might find this charming. The residents found it slow.

In the spring a survey was done on the old pier. The engineers parked a van on the promenade and spent two days measuring the timbers. Their report said what everyone already knew: the structure was sound in places and rotten in others, and the cost of repair would fall on the town. The collapse of a smaller jetty farther along the coast the previous winter had made the question urgent. At the public meeting the practicalities of funding were raised first, and the recognitions due to the volunteers who had kept the jetty open were put off to another night.

The town had one factory, which made rope for the fishing fleet and, in good years, for export. It employed forty people, fewer than half the number it had held twenty years before. The owner was old and had no children who wanted the work. When he died the building stood empty for a year, and then a firm from the city bought it and turned half of it into storage for furniture. The other half was left as it was, with the machines still under dust sheets, because removing them cost more than the space was worth.

Boats came and went. In summer the number of people there doubled, and the cafes put tables on the street. In winter the wind came through the gaps in the windows and the town seemed to hold its breath until March. Nobody said the place was dying, but everyone counted the empty houses on their walk home. The approximation used by the council for next year's budget assumed three new families would move in.

The library had a room at the back that was used for storage more than reading. Boxes of papers from the old courthouse filled the shelves, and a broken piano stood against the wall. A retired teacher came in twice a week to sort the papers into folders, and she had reached the year 1904 when the roof began to leak again. She put a bucket under the drip and kept working. When asked why, she said that someone had kept the records for a hundred years, and it seemed wrong to stop now.” (activation 0.147)
