## Hypothesis

> The negative pole is this layer-1 neuron's shut state (GELU's trough, whose floor value 0.170 is the unit's maximum), and it is read out almost entirely from the current token's identity: it saturates on nearly every token of ordinary English prose and code - content words (hand, world, city, day, part, fact, thing, good, return), word-internal pieces (ology, ments, per, rib) and symbols ({, }, &, $, +) - and it steps off the trough toward 0 only on a stable per-token class: the most frequent grammatical single-token English words (the, is, are, can, has, they, some, most, other, which, only, may, two, three, first, through, we, I, people, table, book, time, way, place, here, years) and non-ASCII byte pieces and bare symbols (backslash, /, ?, %, parentheses, Cyrillic/Greek/Hebrew/Arabic bytes). The split is lexical rather than semantic or grammatical - 'be' sits at the trough while the same lemma 'are' does not, and 'hand'/'world'/'city' saturate while 'table'/'people'/'place' sit at 0 in the same carrier - so a word keeps its side in any context; token identity explains 73% of the variance, and the residual is a weak pull toward the off side when the preceding token is itself one of those high-frequency words (' to' reads 0.16 after want/due/according but 0.02 after and).
