Farmers wanted better access to markets, trucking companies wanted faster routes between cities, and mayors wanted to ease congestion downtown. Critics complained about the damage to cities and the displacement of residents, while supporters pointed to the jobs and the economic growth that followed. By the 1970s, a driver could cross the country without stopping for a traffic light, and suburbs spread outward along the new exits.
