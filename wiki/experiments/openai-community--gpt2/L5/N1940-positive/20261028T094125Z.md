## Discussion

**Recorded verdict REJECTED (0.50), but the probe evidence is dominated by one start-of-sequence effect.** Every one of the 20 probes — all 13 hard negatives and all 7 holdouts — reaches its maximum at **token 0**, at 0.767–0.789 against a 0.424 threshold: the neuron is strongly active at the first token of a text, where attention has no preceding token. I re-ran the model on all 13 hard negatives and the argmax is index 0 in every one (0.767–0.789), while the highest non-first-token activation anywhere in those texts is 0.627 and most stay ≤0.30. This single effect produces all three failed gates:

- specificity 0.00 — every "inactive" negative fired at token 0, though its actual content was quiet as claimed;
- counterfactual reproduction 0.50 — all six pairs observed Δ = 0.000 exactly, because the maximum is the same token-0 activation in both members, so the three pairs predicting a real Δ (0.24–0.32) could not reproduce;
- confound baseline "position" at 1.00 — `position` predicts a text active if it contains any scan position whose mean exceeds threshold, and position 0 (scan mean 0.775) is the only hot position, so it predicts every text active; every text was indeed active.

The same effect is in the scan, not created by the probes: position 0 has mean activation 0.775 against 0.035 for all other positions (about 22×), and carries an estimated ~18% of the pole's total activation mass. The proposer's "near zero on the first ~10 tokens of a sentence" does not cover the start of the *sequence*, which is the strongest single state the pole has.

**Out-of-protocol re-measurement of the mid-context claim.** Prepending a neutral seven-token prefix ("The report was long and detailed. ") moves the maximum into the probe and separates the two regimes: **18/20 probes then agree with their labels** — 12/13 hard negatives quiet (max 0.023–0.38) and 6/7 holdouts active (0.545–1.078). So the long-sentence wrap-up claim is substantially right once the no-context floor is excluded, and the recorded REJECTED verdict reflects a claim that omitted its own strongest state rather than a feature that is absent.

Two genuine misses survive the prefix:

- **Appositive comma deep in a long sentence fires.** "The toolbox my grandfather carried across three continents held a hammer, the one with a steel claw, …" peaks on the comma at token 26, 0.717 — the hypothesis explicitly excludes commas introducing appositives. Clause-separating and appositive commas are not distinguished by this pole.
- **`?` does not close a sentence for this pole.** The 36-token question "After three decades of collecting invoices … who could blame the auditor …?" peaks at 0.220 after the prefix; the closing `?` is silent, while `.`, `).` and ` </` all fire. The trigger list is right to omit `?`, but the claim's "closes a long sentence" then overstates: only declarative and markup closes count.

The other probe families behaved as the claim predicted: short sentences with only near-boundary stops (0.023–0.27), enumeration commas (0.26), a date-apposition comma (−0.13), citation semicolons (0.38), decimal points (0.19), a mid-sentence `).` (0.12), and a relative-clause ` which` three tokens in (−0.09).

**Held-out explanation scores are degenerate.** All 4000 held-out 32-token windows are active — any prose window contains a full stop or comma deep enough to cross 0.424 — so detection is 0.44 (TPR 0.44, FPR undefined) and fuzzing 0.46 (TPR 0.00). Neither measures the claim; with a third of all windows containing a long-sentence closer, a window-level active/inactive judgment cannot test it.
