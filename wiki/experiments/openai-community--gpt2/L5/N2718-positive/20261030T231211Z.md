## Hypothesis

> Sparse, retrospective detector of the short high-frequency 'carrier' tokens that hold a running clause together: the indefinite article 'a'/'an', short prepositions and particles ('to', 'on', 'at', 'in', 'from', 'with', 'up', 'out', 'about', 'after'), pronouns and demonstratives ('it', 'them', 'this', 'these'), light modifiers ('same', 'new', 'first', 'following', 'should', 'could', 'need'), and the concrete noun that closes a short 'of'-complement ('salt', 'rice', 'water', 'coffee'); it is near zero on conjunctions and punctuation ('and', ',', '.', a newline) as the peak token. It is a context detector, not a token detector: with the token held fixed it flips with the 1-3-word frame ('of' = 0.96 after 'I ate a bowl' but 0.00 after 'I placed the bowl'), it is suppressed by definite, already-committed reference ('the' is its weakest determiner; swapping 'a' to 'the' in 'I ate _ bowl of rice' cuts the peak from 1.04 to 0.77 in every frame tested), and it is weakest at clause or line onsets (mean 0.016 for tokens right after '.', ',', ';', ':' or a newline versus 0.048 elsewhere; 1.7% vs 5.8% exceed 0.25).
