## Hypothesis

> L13/H7 is a preceding-nominal copy head with a first-token sink: it writes near its mean while attending the text's first token, and at a token that follows a noun phrase - the finite verb of that phrase's clause, or a possessive 's, preposition, particle, determiner, conjunction or following word right after the phrase - it locks onto the phrase's head noun (its last token, 1-13 tokens back) and copies that noun's value into the residual (OV copying +0.31), crossing the active line only where 0.6-0.95 of its attention lands on one content noun. Such decisive locks are the rule for a possessive's noun (the economy's outlook, Lee's loss, the judge's final order), for a person, role or animal subject at its verb (the judge signed, the clerk filed, the nurse recorded, the postman slipped, the waiter handed) and for a dependent or predicate after a noun that sits four or more tokens into the text (the fog lifted off, the last light left the room, the case sat open, the box having, the college team), so an ordinary multi-clause prompt is usually active somewhere (the head is active at about 9% of scanned tokens), while the sink beats a weak mass or abstract noun that opens the text at its first verb (the weather improved, the music stopped, the gate swung open, the engine hummed), a retrieved quantifier or demonstrative pronoun (everyone, that) stays at the floor because its value is near the mean, and function-word-only text and existential 'there is/are' frames with inanimate nominals never supply a decisive lock.
