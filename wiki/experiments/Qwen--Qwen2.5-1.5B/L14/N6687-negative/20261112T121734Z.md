## Hypothesis

> The negative pole is a sparse, graded detector for the spatial or temporal setting of a situation: its peak falls on the preposition, motion/location predicate or date-clock expression that says where or when something is, goes or happens ('moved to the Canary Islands', 'staying in Kolkata', 'marching up from Saint Augustine', 'lived in', 'their attention elsewhere', 'moving toward the Internet', 'Friday (March 4)', 'meet at 8 P.M.', 'In 1881'), while abstract or evaluative statements with no such setting stay near zero. The lesson, then, is about reversibility. A reform that depends on persuasion is fragile, because the argument can always be reopened. A reform that dismantles the machinery of the old arrangement is harder to undo, since restoring it would require effort that nobody wants to spend. The archives of a ministry are a good example. In 1953, the ministry transferred its records to a warehouse in Leiden, and the transfer became a model of irreversible change: once the files were beyond reach, the old procedures had nothing left to defend. Officials who had resisted the reform for years found themselves unable to argue for a system whose paperwork they could no longer produce.

None of this implies that outcomes improve. It implies only that some changes endure while others are quietly reversed. Whether the same trick can be repeated elsewhere is doubtful, because it depends on a coincidence of timing, money and indifference that is hard to arrange twice. The general point remains: arguments are cheap, and furniture is expensive.” (activation 0.748)
