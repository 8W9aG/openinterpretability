## Hypothesis

> The pole fires on prefabricated rather than composed wording: the terse credit/apparatus formats that name a document's source — a byline ('By <name>', or a name alone on its line above the agency/role, e.g. 'Maria Sanchez' over 'Bloomberg News'), a photo credit ('<name>/<agency>', 'via AP'), a citation's '<surname>, ed.', and the head of a 'References'/'External links'/'Category:' block — and, at similar strength, conventional clause templates in running prose, above all a relative clause whose subject is focused by 'only' with a modal predicate ('the kind of view that only a grandmother can cook up', 'the road that only buses can use') and a clause-final elliptical modal ('like a proper movie should'). It falls to the floor (under 10% of max) when the same information is composed as an ordinary sentence ('Maria Sanchez of Bloomberg News wrote about the warning', 'Jane Doe took the photos for Reuters', 'which T H Grengy edited', 'external files'), when the template's 'only' becomes 'any' or its modal is dropped, and in plain narrative prose; the negative pole instead fires on the specific named agent a claim is sourced to ('analysts', 'Reuters'), not on the generic role nouns a composed sentence uses ('the staff', 'the news agency').
