## Hypothesis

> The pole fires on the count/numeral token that states how much a thing actually contains or has, when an earlier clause in the same sentence has just given that thing's apparent, claimed or expected amount ("the bag looks like a single portion but really contains two portions"; "the sign says one lane, but the road actually has two lanes."), the peak landing on the actual-amount count. Neither half alone fires it: a bare count ("the bag contains two portions"), a bare appearance claim ("the bag looks like a single portion") and a plain quantifier ("all of the students") all stay inactive, so the cue is the apparent-versus-actual amount contrast rather than the token or the noun.
