## Hypothesis

> L10/N286's negative pole fires on the token that carries temporal reference for its clause - the word that states when, for how long, how often, or in what order in time the statement holds: temporal nouns and span words (year, time, era, century, season, period, past, history), frequency/duration and deictic adverbs (always, often, sometimes, usually, never, ever, again, nowadays, today, earlier, later, before), and temporal adjectives/ordinals (early, later, old, medieval, first, second, last). It is not a detector of the scan's particular trigger tokens: any temporal-reference word fires it (annually, era and never were never seen in the scan), while non-temporal words that can fill the same slot (only, main, mainly, mostly, overall, story, centre) leave it near zero, and the response is graded by temporal span and past-orientation (past > next; year/era/century > month/week/morning). The pole reads the word already present at its own position (retrospective), and a passage that already carries temporal anchors about recurring or historical events raises the gain on a later time word.
