## Hypothesis

> The pole fires on the second member of a comparison the text has already set up: one alternative has just been predicated, and the current clause now predicates something of the other one - 'the side windows are tempered glass, but the windshield is much stronger', 'the old laptop was fast, but the new one is even faster', '70 species were found dead, but crows are the most conspicuous'. It peaks on the subject or auxiliary just before that second predicate (or on its grading word) and is strongest when the second predication grades the alternative (much, even, more, substantially, most, only, also, an additional, a major), while it stays near zero for a contrast with no alternative to compare against ('However, the weather turned cold'), for the same degree words in non-comparative sentences ('thank you very much', 'an even surface', 'the score was even'), for a single-clause comparison with no prior predication ('The new engine is much more efficient than the old one'), and for a first mention with nothing to contrast against; its output direction promotes the grading continuations 'even / further' (更是 / 进一步), and the opposite pole fires on explicit realised scalars ('only', 'better', 'further', 'as if that wasn't enough').
