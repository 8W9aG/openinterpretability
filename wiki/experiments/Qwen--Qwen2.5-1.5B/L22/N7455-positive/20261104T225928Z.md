## Hypothesis

> The positive pole fires on text that reports a statistical result or an epidemiological quantity: it peaks inside hypothesis-test and effect-size notation ('P<0.001', 'p = 0.03', 'OR = 1.34; 95% CI 1.10-1.63', 'SE 0.08', '80% power', '± 8.1') and on the measures such results are stated in (incidence, prevalence, cohort, lesion, morbidity, mortality), taking its peak on the digit or punctuation that completes the notation (the '.' of '0.001', the ';' after 'OR = 1.34'); a clinical value recorded for a patient ('temperature 38.5 C', 'blood pressure 140/90') is a weaker trigger of the same kind. It is a statistics detector rather than a citation- or medical-topic-register detector: 'Smith et al., 2020', '[1],[2]', Table/Figure references, qualitative-methods prose, narrative clinical text, non-statistical numbers in everyday prose (recipes, prices, bridge spans, sports scores) and 'clinical practice' prose all stay inactive, with adjacent LaTeX citation markup ('[@ref1],[@ref2]', peak about 3.6, half the statistical peaks) the one non-statistical trigger found, while the 'clinical practice' clause drives the negative pole instead (strength 19.7 on ' clinical'), so one sentence can be strongly positive on its p-value and strongly negative on its 'clinical practice' clause.
