## Hypothesis

> The negative pole is a retrospective detector of explicit numeration in the text just read: it fires hardest on the tokens that carry or bind an expressed count, date, version or identifier - digit runs (years such as '1929', thousands-separated numbers such as '5,820,730', patent/ISBN/phone/hex strings) and the punctuation and conjunctions inside such an expression (the comma after a year, '1928 and 1929', '1942 to 1943', the hyphens of an ISBN, 'As of C++11') - and about half that hard on the quantifier/partitive heads that introduce a counted or measured referent ('some of', 'a total of', 'the most', 'both', '... days old', 'more than the 365 day threshold').
