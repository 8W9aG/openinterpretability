Exclusion criteria included chronic kidney disease at stage four or worse, active malignancy, and pregnancy. The primary endpoint was change in HbA1c at 26 weeks. Secondary endpoints included body weight, episodes of hypoglycemia, and self-reported treatment satisfaction. Randomization used a sequence prepared by an independent statistician and stratified by site and baseline HbA1c. Participants and investigators were masked to assignment. The two schedules differed only in the timing of the basal dose: once daily in the evening in one arm, twice daily in the other. Adherence was tracked through pharmacy refill records and weekly diaries kept by participants. The registry entry was updated in February 2022 to correct a typographical error in the list of exclusion criteria; no other amendments were made.

Of the 614 participants, 587 completed the 26 week visit. Baseline characteristics were well balanced: mean age 54 years, mean HbA1c 8.4 percent, mean body mass index 31. The population was recruited from a single regional health system, which may limit generalizability, and the sample was predominantly composed of adults over fifty. Treatment satisfaction scores were slightly better in the once daily arm, though the difference did not meet the prespecified threshold for significance. Adherence was greater than 90 percent in both arms and did not differ by assignment. Two participants left the study before the first dose and are included in the safety set only; the statistical plan pre-specified this handling.

Adverse events were balanced, and no new safety signals were identified during the trial. The data monitoring committee reviewed an interim report at the halfway point and recommended continuation without modification. Several limitations deserve mention. The trial was powered only for the primary endpoint, so subgroup comparisons are underpowered and should be treated as exploratory. Follow-up ended at 26 weeks, and longer term outcomes remain unknown. Finally, adherence was measured indirectly, and diary data may be incomplete.

The primary analysis, conducted on an intention to treat basis, yielded no significant difference between arms in HbA1c reduction at 26 weeks (difference 0.06 percentage points, 95 percent confidence interval minus 0.11 to 0.23). Body weight changed little in either arm, and the rate of severe hypoglycemia was low and similar between groups. An exploratory analysis restricted to participants older than 65 years showed a similar pattern, and a sensitivity analysis excluding the two participants who never received a dose did not alter the conclusion.

The investigators conclude that either schedule is a reasonable choice for adults beginning insulin, and that the decision should reflect patient preference, cost, and local practice. A full protocol, statistical analysis plan, and de-identified dataset are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request. Funding came from the regional health board with no industry support, and the authors report no competing interests. A companion editorial argues that the result matters less for what it says about insulin than for what it reveals about how weakly powered trials shape clinical habit.
