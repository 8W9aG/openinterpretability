## Hypothesis

> L1/H2 is a broad, dense previous-token pass-through head: its attention at each position is split between the immediately preceding token (mean weight ~0.35 over prose, but 0.80-1.00 at the positions that peak), the position itself (~0.29) and the text's first token (~0.14), and what it writes is W_O applied to the value of whichever token it lands on, so its strength above the 0.883 floor is |W_O(v_attended - mean write)| - how unusual the attended (almost always preceding) token is, not what the current token means (the current token only sets the query, hence how sharply attention lands). It therefore peaks at 0.25-0.7 x max when the preceding token is substantive or unusual - existential 'there' before is/'s/were (0.6-0.7 x max), the first piece of a word the tokenizer splits (p|ang, Ch|ag, br|im, Sch|re, S|atur, 0.3-0.7), 'all'/'last'/'third' before a noun (0.3-0.55), a verb before its pronoun or particle (told him, handed him, sat down, came back), over/on/across/behind before 'the' (0.25-0.35), comma-separated digit lists (0.35-0.6) - and sits at 0-0.05 x max whenever the preceding token is 'the'/'The'/'a', a period or comma, or 'in'/'to'/'of'/'at'; so ordinary prose of 12+ tokens is where this head is broad and straddles the line (measured peaks 0.15-0.5 x max, 33 of 50 sentences above the line), while only texts of 2-7 tokens built from the commonest words ('The N.', 'The N is ADJ.', 'The N V the N.', 'The N is on the N.', one single-token word + '.') are reliably inactive (0 of 61 measured).
