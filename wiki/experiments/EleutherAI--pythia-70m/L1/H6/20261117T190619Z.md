## Hypothesis

> L1/H6 is a broad copy head: each token attends to one earlier word, usually 1-7 back, and copies that word's value. Almost any text of 8+ tokens that contains ordinary words crosses the line (measured 24/30 plain prose sentences at 0.85-1.4, usually on the noun or verb after the subject). It is strong (1.5-2.6, about 0.5-0.75 of max) on the noun after a demonstrative 'that' ('paid that bill', 'that's', 'that is') and on 'is' or the adjective after a subject 'it' ('it is possible/likely/clear'). The narrow class that reliably stays below the line is text with no letters at all: comma-separated lists of plain numbers (integers or decimals, optionally negative, with no '$', '=', '*' or words), which peak at 0.0-0.6 because commas and digits copy almost nothing.
