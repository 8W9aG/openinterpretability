## Hypothesis

> N520+ is a sub-word fragment neuron: it fires on a token that is a non-final piece of a multi-token word - the opening or middle fragment the byte-level BPE leaves when it splits a name, foreign word, technical compound, hyphenated compound or code identifier (e.g. ' Par' in 'Parramatta', ' st' in 'stucco', ' Ch' in 'Chagall', a single Cyrillic or Greek letter inside a Russian or Greek word, or a byte-fallback token inside a CJK character), most strongly on the word's first one or two pieces and especially when that piece ends on a consonant. It is near-silent on the piece that completes a word (word-final pieces and suffixes such as 'ing', 'ion', 'ies') and on words that fit into a single token, which is why ordinary English prose sits at its baseline of about -0.15; ablating it removes probability from the fragment that completes the current word ('ram' after ' Par', ' Paul' after ' St', month names after '22nd', 'right'/'sufficient' after 'self-').
