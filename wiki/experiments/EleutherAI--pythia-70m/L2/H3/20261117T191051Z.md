## Hypothesis

> Most-recent-noun copier: from each token the head attends back to the nearest noun or pronoun that came after the text's first token and writes it, so it crosses the line on such a noun and the 1-3 tokens after it. It is reliably above the line when a common noun brought in by a determiner, adjective or comma (the weather, further studies, the results, 'eggs, flour,') is followed by its verb or auxiliary (peak 0.9-2.6 on was/are/is/have/did and the next word), and on the random letters of a URL ID after '/' (t.co/aB3dE5); it reliably stays below when no noun or pronoun follows the first word: one- or two-word texts, a single clause opened by a pronoun or name followed only by verbs, adverbs and adjectives ('He never came back again after all.', 'John is always early and never late.'), a subject noun that is itself the first word ('Rain then stopped.'), and runs of single letters, plain numbers, one repeated word or one repeated symbol; later personal pronouns (I, you, he, she, we, they, him, them) as the only subjects give 0.3-0.8 and cross about half the time, 'it'/'this' subjects usually cross, object nouns late in a clause and short code usually stay below, and almost any natural prose of 12+ tokens containing 'the + noun + verb' crosses.
