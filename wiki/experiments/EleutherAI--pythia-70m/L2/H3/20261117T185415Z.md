## Hypothesis

> Subject-to-predicate head: on the verb or auxiliary right after a full-noun subject, and on the next 1-3 predicate tokens (e.g. 'Further studies| are| needed| to', 'the problem still| remains', 'the court should| have'), it attends back to the subject's head noun, skipping an attached 'of'/PP, and writes that noun. It also fires on the characters of a random URL path after '/' (t.co/I8uOok), attending to the '/'. It is near zero on determiners, sentence-final periods, objects late in a clause and most code, and on predicates whose subject is a personal pronoun (I, we, you, he, she, they). Plain prose often crosses the 0.25 x max line: most sentences with a noun subject followed by a verb reach about 0.25-0.5 on the verb, as do lists of nouns ('eggs, flour, sugar,'). Pronoun-only sentences and code usually stay below. Top strengths (0.7-1.0) need a plural or abstract subject ('studies', 'research', 'results', 'reasons', 'question', 'rumors') followed by an auxiliary ('are/is/can/should/did') in a longer context, or a random URL path.
