## Hypothesis

> Two-part retrospective detector. (1) The capitalised word-start piece 'Cont'/' Cont' of a word the tokenizer splits (Controversy, Contribution, Containing, Contested, Contra) fires strongly, at about 0.5-0.9 of max. A few other split word-starts fire more weakly or depending on context: ' Prov'(ided), ' en'(listing), Dutch ' op'. Other split word-starts (' b'lemish, ' enc'rusted, ' ent'hralled, ' Pro'vision, ' In'comprehensible) and whole-word lowercase cont-words (' contract', ' controversy', 'Continue') stay inactive. (2) Digits and separators inside machine-formatted reference or metadata strings fire at about 0.5-0.95 of max: ISO dates and clock times (the '-' and ':' separators and the digit groups), DOIs, the number after 'no.'/'vol.' in journal citations, legal reporter citations ('Cal. Rptr. 797'), timestamped email headers with dash rules, figure-ID markup ('){#'), and URL pieces (en.wikipedia). Counts and years in ordinary prose ('bought 5 apples', 'hired 150 workers in 2010') stay inactive or weak (below about 0.25 of max). Wikipedia boilerplate headings ('External links', 'Further reading', 'See also') are moderately active.
