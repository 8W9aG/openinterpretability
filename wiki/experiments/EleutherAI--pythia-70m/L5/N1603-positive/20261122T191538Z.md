## Hypothesis

> The pole is a graded dial for human-scale prose about people and everyday life (news, biography, history, reviews, anecdotes, recipes, travel and how-to writing): it fires on the token that names, introduces or appraises an individual — personal names and name fragments, individual role/title/family words ('Dr.', 'Professor', 'journalist', 'singer', 'wife', 'spokesperson'), quantifiers and indefinite determiners ('a', 'an', 'couple', 'few', 'several', 'one', 'another', 'two'), evaluative or magnitude adjectives ('famous', 'wonderful', 'remarkable', 'stunning', 'former', 'subsequent'), and the linker just before a person reference ('by' in a credit, 'and' between two names, '.' after a title). Each of these about doubles as personal references accumulate in the preceding ~30 tokens (determiners 0.21 → 0.41 mean and 7% → 18% active; prepositions 0.07 → 0.23), so person-centred passages peak at 1.5–2.9 and everyday instructional prose at 0.7–1.5, while legal, code, product-spec and organism/taxonomy text stays at 0.2–1.0 — a lone determiner or numeral inside a technical sentence can still reach 0.8–1.3, and a personal name fires about 1.0–1.5 even inside a scientific sentence; the negative pole never exceeds 0.17 in the scan, so there is no symmetric opposite register.
