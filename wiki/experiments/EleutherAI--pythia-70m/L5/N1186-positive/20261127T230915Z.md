## Hypothesis

> In English, the pole fires on the quantity word of a count/amount phrase, i.e. 'few' (in 'a few', 'next few', 'past few'), spelled or digit cardinals ('two', 'three', 'ten', '5') and to a lesser degree 'several'/'many', peaking on that token itself, strongest (~2-3) when a temporal/duration frame precedes it (next, past, over, within, for, lasted, returned ... later) and weaker (~1) for plain object counts ('bought two apples', 'have a few questions'); 'some', 'little', determiners and adjectives in the same slot stay near 0-0.9. Separately it is broadly active on Romance/Dutch function words in quantity/partitive phrases ('de', 'las' after 'millones de', 'cada una de', 'dizaine de', 'meer dan'), which carries much of the token-identity variance.
