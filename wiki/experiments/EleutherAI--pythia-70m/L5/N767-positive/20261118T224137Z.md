## Hypothesis

> Strongly active (about 2.5-5) on a count word that quantifies a following noun after at least one earlier token: a cardinal above one in digits or words, in English or French (' two', ' three', ' 12', ' thirty', ' trois'), or a plural quantifier (' few', ' several', ' many', ' couple of'). This holds even inside an otherwise inert sentence. Without a count word, English prose is irregular: many speech, wanting, emotion and activity verbs (' said', ' waited', ' wanted', ' laughed') and prepositions or linkers such as ' about', ' for', ' from', ' over', ' after', ' on', ' so', ' why' and clause- or adjective-joining ' and' give a weak 1.3-2.7 that is left unclaimed. The second is French, Spanish, German, Dutch or Portuguese prose with no number word.
