## Hypothesis

> In prose about concrete physical things (places, buildings, objects, food, nature, durations of time), in English or Italian, it fires on the word that opens a further noun phrase once one is already under way: mainly 'a'/'the'/'una', a numeral or ordinal ('two', 'tre', 'second', 'five-'), or the adjective after 'a' ('a long trunk'). It is strongest on the second or later item of a series or a phrase after 'and'/a preposition ('a pear, two plums, and a lemon', 'past a rocky island and into a quiet bay', 'built a house in a week'). It also fires on 'may'/'has'/'have' right after a non-personal subject ('It may', 'there have', 'The tree has'). It stays below a quarter of max on the same words in technical, data or institutional sentences ('built a server in a week', 'The data drifted past a fixed limit'), on auxiliaries after personal pronouns ('He may', 'they have', 'I may'), on 'final'/'old' where an ordinal would fire, after a copula plus noun ('She was a teacher'), and in code.
