## Hypothesis

> In English text that physically describes a scene, it fires on the words that open the next noun phrase or clause: 'a'/'the', the adjective, numeral or ordinal after them, and 'and' joining two such phrases. The scene is landscape, buildings, rooms or objects, with a body or thing moving or resting somewhere ('drifted past a rocky island and into a quiet bay', 'stood beside a muddy pond', 'The kitchen had a stone floor and a wide window', 'hung a coat on a hook'), and the pole is strongest on the second phrase. It also fires on time spans after staying or spending ('stayed for a month', 'spent a few weeks') and on 'may' after 'It'. The same determiners stay below a quarter of max in transactional, communicative, institutional, academic or technical sentences ('signed a contract', 'sold a coat to a man', 'approved a loan', 'describes a method', 'sends a request over a secure channel'), after a copula ('She was a teacher'), in memorized quotes, in lists and in code.
