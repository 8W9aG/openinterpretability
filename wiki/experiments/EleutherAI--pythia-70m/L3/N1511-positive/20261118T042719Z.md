## Hypothesis

> Fires on the middle and final subword pieces of an unfamiliar word that the tokenizer breaks into 3 or more short fragments. Examples are Polish/Turkish/Cyrillic/Greek words (mie|ś|cie, mü|ş|ter|i, Sz|cz|e|br|ze|szyn, жи|в|ё|т) and byte fragments of Hindi, Tamil, Korean or Thai text. It peaks above 0.5 x max on the 3rd-4th fragment and builds up when fragmented words follow one another. Minimum context: Polish/Turkish/Cyrillic/Indic/Korean/Thai fire within 5-10 tokens, but Greek stays below 0.25 x max unless it has about 20+ tokens of long words (a 12-token Greek sentence measured 1.1, a 39-token one 3.3), and Japanese/Chinese written mostly in whole kana/kanji tokens stays below 0.25 x max even at 30 tokens. Predicted inactive (peak below 0.25 x max, about 1.6): single-line Python/JS/C/HTML code of 2-9 tokens built from keywords, single letters and common whole-word identifiers, e.g. 'def compute(x, y):', 'y = x + 1', 'let count = 0;' (measured 0.0-0.65). Outside that class: plain English prose is NOT predicted inactive (it often sits at 2-3, i.e. 0.3-0.45 x max), and neither are SQL, `new X()` constructors, fragmented abbreviations like 'qty', or the unexplained English offshoot 'share and respond to other classes' (about 0.75 x max).
