## Hypothesis

> N1542- is a retrospective burst detector for tokens that break the surface pattern of ordinary lowercase English: it fires on a capitalized word in mid-sentence position where running text would use lowercase (the capitalized function words On, In, At, A, And, For, With; capitalized common nouns and names such as Museum, President, Committee, Melanie, Gregory, Mill), on rare proper-name pieces such as 'ahmad' (which fires in either case), on non-ASCII script and byte-fragment tokens (Greek, Arabic, Persian, Japanese, accented Spanish), on all-caps strings and camelCase/snake_case code identifiers (ToString, myVariableName), and, separately, on the first '.' or '\n' token of a text (~0.09-0.10); the burst builds at the trigger and decays over the next two to four tokens, so the following function words (of, the, and, at) also read as active. It stays well below the active line on prose made only of frequent lowercase words with no marked token ('the room was warm and the milk was cold so we left it out' 0.006; 'she opened the door and looked outside at the quiet street' 0.013), on lowercase spellings of common names and on nonce strings (melanie, john, james, zyxwv), on the capitalized determiner 'The' and on day names in their ordinary capitalization (on Friday, on Monday), on commas, semicolons, question marks and digits, and on the second and later periods/newlines; sentence-initial capitals can spill a weak 0.04-0.06 burst onto the next token or two, and a few common content nouns (president, report, bread, plan, city, book) give a weak 0.04-0.07 tail even in lowercase, so clean negatives should open with a lowercase function word and avoid those nouns.
