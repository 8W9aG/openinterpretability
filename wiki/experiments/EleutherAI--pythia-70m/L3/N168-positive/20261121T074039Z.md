## Hypothesis

> N168+ is a sub-lexical 'glue token' detector: it fires retrospectively on tokens that carry no lexical content of their own, above all on single-letter and byte-fragment pieces of non-Latin-script words (the Tamil piece 'à®', Greek ν/ί, Cyrillic в, Arabic م, Bengali া) inside a sustained run of foreign script, where activation builds over a run of several foreign-script tokens to 0.5-1.9 (Tamil, Bengali, Arabic, Greek, Cyrillic, Thai, Georgian, Armenian, Hebrew, Korean, Vietnamese; weaker, 0.8-1.1, for CJK) while a lone foreign word embedded in English stays near 0. In Latin-script text the same glue condition appears as closed-class words that complete a frame opened by the previous token - a pronoun after a modal/auxiliary ('Can you', 'Does anyone', 'Should you') or a preposition/determiner after a collective noun or quantifier ('everyone in', 'members of', 'writers at') - at 1.4-2.5, and near zero for the same words in other frames ('If you', 'kind of', 'dozens of') or for English content words; it is retrospective, not lookahead (zero-ablating the pole moves the next-token distribution by only KL ~1e-3, and the whitespace tokens its Jacobian lens promotes never follow a peak).
