# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the project uses [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

The project has two version numbers:

- **Package version** (`openinterp.__version__`): the code. Bump it in `openinterp/__init__.py`, which `pyproject.toml` reads.
- **Protocol version** (`openinterp.core.schemas.PROTOCOL_VERSION`): the review decision rule. It is stored in every verdict and documented in `docs/PROTOCOL.md` §6. A protocol change always needs a package release too, but not the other way round.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Transcoder baseline on GPT-2 small: latents beat positive neuron poles, 37 of 40 against 20 of 40** (`docs/analyses/transcoder-baseline.md`). This is the full run of the learned-basis baseline in `TODO.md`. It audited 40 random `gpt2-small-transcoders` latents and 40 random positive neuron poles under protocol 0.7. Scans read `corpus.jsonl` in 128-token windows. The proposer was claude-opus-5-5, the reviewer claude-sonnet-5-5 and the judges claude-haiku-5-5. Every draw is logged in order in `transcoder-baseline.jsonl`, with its verdict and record.
  - *Result.* Latents: 92% VERIFIED (Wilson 80–97%), mean balanced accuracy 0.95. Positive poles: 50% (35–65%), 0.90. Fisher p = 4.3 × 10⁻⁵.
  - *Dead latents.* 21 of 61 latent draws never fired on the scan. Counting them as not verified gives 37 of 61 (61%), and p = 0.31.
  - *Why poles fail.* 13 poles failed specificity: they are broad families, and hard negatives outside the described family fired. Six poles in layers 1–2 stated a first-token floor above the activity line, so they could have no hard negatives. One failed the confound baseline. Three latents failed: sensitivity, counterfactual reproduction and lookahead consistency, one each.
- **The baseline's native arm is positive poles for GELU models.** `Comparison.p_positive` tests latents against positive poles, and the home page reports that p first, then the p against all poles. `docs/PROTOCOL.md` §1 says why: GELU's negative pole sits in its dip on most tokens, so no hard negative can pass. p-values below 0.001 now show in scientific notation instead of rounding to 0.000.

- **Transcoder baseline pilot on GPT-2 small** (`docs/analyses/transcoder-baseline-pilot.md`). 20 random units were audited, with seeds drawn in order and every draw logged in `transcoder-baseline-pilot.jsonl`. The scans read `corpus.jsonl` in 128-token windows. Proposer was claude-opus-5-5, reviewer claude-sonnet-5-5 and judges claude-haiku-5-5.
  - *Result.* Transcoder latents: 9 of 10 VERIFIED (90%, Wilson 60–98%). Neuron poles: 2 of 10 (20%, 6–51%), split 2 of 4 positive and 0 of 6 negative. Fisher p = 0.0055 overall, 0.18 against positive poles only.
  - *Dead draws.* 4 of the first 14 latent draws never fired on the scan; no neuron draw was dead.
  - *Why negative poles fail.* GPT-2's negative poles sit in GELU's dip on most tokens, so a probe's peak always clears the activity line. None can have hard negatives that pass, which is a protocol question in `TODO.md` rather than a finding about interpretability.
  - *Label checks.* Probe labels were checked against each hypothesis before review, and the corrections are listed in the analysis.

- **GPT-2 small joins the registry, and the home page compares learned and native bases.** This sets up the transcoder baseline arm in `TODO.md`.
  - *GPT-2 small (`openai-community/gpt2`).* It passed the *Models* checks. Real scans of random neuron poles and transcoder latents ran on `corpus.jsonl`: about 70 s for a neuron on MPS. The final norm resolves to `transformer.ln_f` and the MLP norm to `ln_2`. Its MLP is plain GELU, and the README's new *Models* table notes what that does to the negative pole: in every layer it bottoms out at exactly 0.170, at a pre-activation of about −0.75. It is now in `[tool.openinterp] models` and CI's `ALLOWED_MODELS`, so `openinterp next` explores it. A new `[tool.openinterp.window_tokens]` table gives its explore draws `--window-tokens 128` (see below). Follow-ups on a record scanned in windows rescan with that record's window.
  - *The GPT-2 transcoders fit only short contexts.* Over 200k tokens in 512-token windows, Dunefsky et al.'s transcoders explained 17%, −28% and −154% of the MLP output's variance at layers 0–2, worse than predicting the mean. Binned by position, the fit holds through position 128, falls after it, and collapses past ~200: there the reconstruction's norm is ~500 against a true output of ~15. Layers 7–11 are unaffected. In 128-token windows every layer fits (59–99%), so the preset records `context_tokens = 128` and `audit` refuses a feature scan in longer windows. GPT-2 neuron scans use the same windows, so the two arms are scanned alike.
  - *Learned-basis baseline on the home page.* For each dictionary and scan setting (corpus and window) with randomly selected latents, a table compares them with randomly selected neuron poles of the same model and layers, scanned the same way (`openinterp/builder/learned_baseline.py`). The neuron poles are also split by pole. It shows the share VERIFIED with 95% Wilson intervals, mean balanced accuracy, and a two-sided Fisher exact test, beside the dictionary's explained variance, mean L0 and dead share. Each unit counts once, by its first experiment, so neither side gains from follow-ups. Parquet gains `selection`, `corpus_sha256`, `window_tokens`, `balanced_accuracy` and `dictionary_dead_share`.
  - *Dead latents.* A feature scan now records the share of its dictionary's latents that never fire on the scanned tokens (`DictionaryProfile.dead_share`). A random draw of a dead latent can't be audited. The first three draws were all dead. Over the 200k-token scan the share is 11–67% per layer, about a third on average.

- **Steering samples on experiment pages.** `openinterp audit` now records what the model writes with a neuron pole or dictionary latent clamped high, at baseline and ablated (`NeuronAuditSubmission.steering`). Five fixed neutral prompts ("The weather today is", "Once upon a time,", …) are each continued greedily for up to 24 tokens: untouched; with the unit's own ablation; and with the unit clamped at 1, 2 and 4 times its top activation in the scan, at every position. A neuron is clamped to the pole's signed value; a latent's write is set to that activation times its decoder row. All 25 continuations decode as one left-padded batch with the model's cache, about 3 s on CPU for Qwen2.5-0.5B, recorded as the `steering` cost stage. Like the Patchscope readout, they are taken after the proposer has answered and appear in neither agent's prompt. Wiki pages show a table per experiment, and site pages show each prompt's continuations side by side, ablated through ×4, with text identical to the baseline faded. On L14/N2611+ (crops), clamping at ×1 turns "Once upon a time," from a girl named Lily into a farmer who wants his crops healthy and strong, and at ×4 "The report concluded that" ends in the worst drought in the region's history. Ablation leaves all five continuations unchanged. Heads get no samples, since a head has no single "high" setting. Existing records have none until their units are audited again. Reported, not gated; the protocol version is unchanged.
- **Patchscope readout of a unit's output direction.** `openinterp audit` now records the model's own description of what a neuron pole or dictionary latent writes, with no examples (`NeuronAuditSubmission.patchscope`). The direction (a pole's signed `w_out`, or a latent's decoder row) replaces the residual stream after a layer's block at the `?` of an inspection prompt, scaled to 1, 2 and 4 times the norm it replaces, and the model continues greedily (Ghandeharioun et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024). There are two prompts, a few-shot `word: category` list and `The meaning of the word "?" is`. Each is read at the unit's own layer and at layer 2. Every reading is paired with a fixed random direction injected the same way, so text that comes from the prompt shows up in both. On Qwen2.5-0.5B the category prompt at layer 2 names the concept of several VERIFIED poles where the control does not: *crop* for L14/N2611+ (crops), *newspaper* for L5/N4579− (periodicals), *official* for L3/N3738− (*official*) and *culture* for L5/N2131− (art and folk culture). Poles from layer 17 up, injected at their own layer, mostly read back the control's text. The readout is taken only after the proposer has answered (in both the one-step and the `--evidence`/`--hypothesis` flow), is absent from evidence files, and appears in neither agent's prompt. That makes it a blind baseline for a later comparison with the agent's hypotheses. It takes about 5–8 s per unit on CPU, recorded as the `patchscope` cost stage. Wiki and site experiment pages show a table of unit and control text by prompt, layer and scale. Heads are not read, since they write a subspace rather than one direction. Reported, not gated; the protocol version is unchanged.
- **`openinterp patchscope-baseline`: the Patchscope readout as a blind proposer baseline.** It turns each unit's readout into one explanation by a rule fixed before scoring: the few-shot category reading at the lowest layer read, at ×2 the replaced norm, as `Fires on tokens related to "<reading>".` That explanation is scored on exactly the held-out windows the agent's explanation was scored on. Records store those windows with their labels, so the comparison is paired and nothing is re-measured; re-deriving all 99 archived records' scores from their own stored answers reproduces them exactly.
  - *Steps.* A first run reads every unit (cached under `--work-dir`) and writes judge prompts for the experiments whose agent explanation the `--judge-model` judged. A second run, after `explanation-judge` subagents answer, scores everything into `docs/analyses/patchscope-baseline.{md,jsonl}`. The 9 poles inspected while choosing the prompts are reported apart as a dev set.
  - *Result.* The backfill covered 94 units over 99 archived experiments. On the 44 held-out experiments judged by Claude Haiku 5.5, the baseline scored at chance (detection 0.51, fuzzing 0.52) against the agent's 0.60 and 0.62. It matched or beat the agent on 20% of experiments for detection (sign test p = 1e-8) and 30% for fuzzing (p = 2e-4). Embedding AUROC, over all 89 held-out experiments, was 0.53 against 0.55 (p = 0.24).
  - *Why.* The readings are mostly generic categories: 38 distinct over 99 experiments, led by *country*, *noun* and *animal*.
  - *Judges.* Judges split on grammatical readings, taking *noun* or *verb* as the word or the part of speech. One answer arrived malformed and was judged again: 26 texts where the malformed one held 12.

  This closes the first *Analyses per experiment* item in `TODO.md`. Its Activation Oracle half moves under *Models*, since it waits on a model that has a released oracle.

### Changed

- **A unit's status no longer follows its newest experiment.** A weaker or self-reviewed rerun could overturn a cross-model VERIFIED: L16/N2071+ went VERIFIED → NEEDS_REVISION on one re-review. The status (its *standing*) is now derived from all of a unit's experiments (`docs/PROTOCOL.md` §3 step 9). Only the newest hypothesis counts, each probe set once (a re-measurement replaces its earlier reading), and independent reviews outweigh self-reviews. If the reviews left disagree on VERIFIED, the unit is **CONTESTED** and not counted as verified. On the 191 archived experiments this changes two of 38 multi-experiment poles: L16/N2071+ (VERIFIED by Sonnet 5.5, NEEDS_REVISION by DeepSeek) and L22/N4198− (self-reviewed VERIFIED, then REJECTED) are now contested. 82 units are still verified. Unit and layer pages show the standing. A unit's card lists the reviews it rests on, and each experiment is tagged *counts toward status*, *re-measured since*, *outweighed by an independent review* or *earlier hypothesis*, replacing *latest*/*superseded*. The home page lists contested units and reports reviewer agreement: 11 of the 16 hypotheses reviewed with more than one probe set got the same verdict from every review, and pairs of reviews agree 69% of the time (73% across reviewer models). Parquet gains `unit_status` and `counts_toward_status`; `is_latest` stays. Verdicts are unchanged, so the protocol version is too.

### Fixed

- A dictionary-feature scan on MPS crashed casting the fit statistics to float64, which MPS lacks. They are now accumulated on the CPU.
- `openinterp reproduce --allow-model` limits which models CI downloads, but a feature record could name any dictionary repository. Reproduce now refuses a feature record whose weights aren't its preset's.

## [0.0.7] - 2026-10-22

Ships with protocol version **0.7**.

### Added

- **`wikitext` corpus preset:** `openinterp corpus --dataset wikitext` samples whole articles from [WikiText-103](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Salesforce/wikitext) (`Salesforce/wikitext`, `wikitext-103-raw-v1`, train split): 28,475 Good and Featured Wikipedia articles, long and clean, for windows of 512 tokens or more and for entity and fact neurons. The dataset stores one row per paragraph or heading, so a new `--layout wikitext` (a preset field, recorded as `layout` in the corpus's source sidecar) joins each row group's rows back into articles, keeps only the articles that start and end within the row group, writes headings as plain lines with blank lines between paragraphs, and undoes WikiText's tokenisation (`1 @,@ 000` → `1,000`, `well @-@ known` → `well-known`, `lobster ,` → `lobster,`, `ca n't` → `can't`, `( 24 in )` → `(24 in)`). Single-quoted phrases keep their spaces, because apostrophes such as *K'iche'* make them ambiguous. 500 documents take about 20 seconds. Rebuilding at the recorded commit gives a byte-identical corpus. With the default `--max-chars 3000`, 478 of the 500 documents have at least 512 Qwen2.5 tokens (median 683); `--max-chars 100000` keeps whole articles (median 2,846). Licence: CC BY-SA 3.0 and GFDL, so excerpts in the wiki need attribution and the same licence.
- **Per-layer scan cache.** A scan's forward pass already captures every neuron in the layer, so `openinterp audit` now stores the whole layer's activations on each real token, with token ids, positions and the pieces scanned. The cache is in `~/.cache/openinterp/scans` (`$OPENINTERP_SCAN_CACHE` moves it, `off` disables it, `--no-scan-cache` skips it for one run). A later audit of any neuron in that layer replays the cache instead of running the model. An entry is keyed by the model, revision, dtype, device type, torch and transformers versions, layer, batch size and the exact pieces (texts and windows), so a replayed scan is bit-for-bit the model's: top examples, histogram, partners and confounds match exactly, which the tests check for whole documents and windows, both poles, and float32 and bfloat16 weights. Entries are written to a temporary directory and renamed into place, so concurrent worktrees never read a partial one. The cache is capped at 40 GB (`$OPENINTERP_SCAN_CACHE_GB`) and evicts the least recently used layers first. A 200,000-token Qwen2.5-0.5B layer in float32 is about 4 GB. On `corpus.jsonl` with Qwen2.5-0.5B on CPU, the first audit of L13 scanned in 347 s, including writing the cache. Audits of L13/N1706+ and L13/N42− from the cache then scanned in 13 s and 7 s, and their whole audits fell from about 10 minutes to about 4½. Their evidence and proposer prompts were identical to fresh scans' (N42− checked with `--no-scan-cache`: 342 s). The lenses and input attribution, about 2 minutes each, now take most of an audit's time. `openinterp scan-cache` lists or clears the cache. `openinterp next` notes when a follow-up audit's layer is cached; its draw is unchanged.
- **Vocabulary projection bars on experiment pages.** The Jacobian- and logit-lens token lists are now diverging bar charts in their own *Vocabulary projection* section: one panel per lens, the 10 most promoted tokens above zero and the 10 most suppressed below, bar length the token's z-score over the whole vocabulary. Z-scores are scale-invariant, so both lenses share one axis and the Jacobian lens's sharper token sets stand out against the logit lens's noise in early layers. Spaces show as ␣; a *Values* table holds the numbers. To draw them, `VocabularyLens` gains `logit_promoted_z`, `logit_suppressed_z`, `jacobian_promoted_z` and `jacobian_suppressed_z`, recorded by every new audit. Records are write-once, so earlier experiments keep the token lists, labelled as predating the scores. Recomputing three archived records' lenses with the new code reproduced their token lists exactly. Wiki pages print the z-scores beside the tokens, and Parquet gains `jacobian_promoted_z` and `logit_promoted_z`. Reported, not gated; the protocol version is unchanged.
- **`openinterp activations`** prints a neuron pole's per-token strengths over the scan corpus (document, window, index, token, strength, left context), from the layer's cached scan when there is one, so agents no longer write their own full-corpus scan loops for diagnosis. It shows the strongest `--top` tokens, or every token with `--top 0`, and filters by `--token` and `--min-strength`, as TSV or `--format jsonl`. It takes the audit's corpus options, so it reuses the audit's cache entry. The `native-neuron-audit` and `neuron-family-triage` skills point to it.

- **`openinterp next` explores configured models, weighted towards little coverage.** The explore pool fell back to the models already in the registry, so with every record on Qwen2.5-0.5B the planner could never start on a new model. It now reads `[tool.openinterp] models` in `pyproject.toml` (next to `--wiki-dir`) and draws a model with weight `1/√(1 + units studied or claimed by open PRs)`; `--top` lists each model's share, the rationale and `Plan.explore_models` record it, and `--model` still overrides. A test keeps the list equal to CI's `ALLOWED_MODELS`, so CI can reproduce every record the planner asks for. The list holds only Qwen2.5-0.5B until a model under *Models* in `TODO.md` passes its checks.
- **A transcoder baseline arm: sparse-dictionary latents can be audited.** The registry's premise is that native neurons are already interpretable; the natural test is to audit a learned basis on the same layers under the same protocol and compare the share VERIFIED among random units. A latent of a published transcoder or SAE is a third unit kind, `FeatureIdentifier` (`kind = "dictionary_feature"`, `L<layer>/F<idx>` plus a dictionary name). `openinterp audit --dictionary gpt2-small-transcoders --random` samples a layer and a latent uniformly (`--layer`/`--feature` to choose), and `score`, `review`, `reevaluate` and `reproduce` work as for neurons. A latent's strength is its activation; its ablation subtracts its write `a_f · W_dec[f]` from the MLP output; its causal metrics rank it against the 32 latents of the dictionary whose write changes most like its own. The submission's `DictionaryProfile` pins the weights (Hugging Face repository, commit, file) and how they are read: the input site (`mlp_in`, `mlp_in_normalized` or `mlp_out`) and activation (ReLU or JumpReLU). The scan also measures how well the whole dictionary fits there (explained variance of the MLP output, mean L0, the latent's density; reported, not gated). The first preset is Dunefsky et al.'s GPT-2 small transcoders (arXiv:2406.11944). They read the LayerNorm-standardised input without its gain and bias, TransformerLens's `ln2.hook_normalized`: on 16 `corpus.jsonl` documents at layer 8 they explain 66% of the MLP output's variance that way, and none of it from the MLP's own input. A dry run on L5/F4943, a latent for superlatives after *the*, scanned 200,000 tokens in 85 s on CPU (explained variance 0.53, L0 41), was reviewed with causal scores against 32 matched latents, and reproduced exactly. Feature pages sit at `models/<model>/L<layer>/<dictionary>/F<idx>.html` and in the wiki under `…/L<layer>/<dictionary>/F<idx>/`. Parquet gains `dictionary`, `feature_idx`, `dictionary_explained_variance`, `dictionary_mean_l0` and `feature_density`. Features are kept out of the home page's native totals, verified list and false discovery estimate, and reported on their own line. The decision rule is unchanged for every kind, so the protocol version is too.

### Changed

- **Reviewer independence is encouraged, not claimed.** `docs/PROTOCOL.md` §2 said the reviewer must not be the proposer's model, but 96 of 162 experiments (2026-10-22) were self-reviewed, including 25 of the 67 current verified claims, mostly `deepseek-flash` reviewing itself. The rule now matches practice: a different reviewer model is preferred, a self-review is allowed and recorded as before (`independent_review`, gated only under `require_independent_review`), and the decision rule is unchanged. In return, self-reviews are visible:
  - experiment, neuron, layer and home pages badge them *self-reviewed*, and the home page reports how many experiments and current verified claims are self-reviewed, with a third count in the *Submitting agents* list;
  - wiki pages mark them in the Reviewer row, the history tables and *Next steps*;
  - Parquet gains `self_reviewed`, and records gain `AuditRecord.self_reviewed`;
  - `verify` (and `scripts/experiment-loop.sh`) warn more specifically when the reviewer model is the proposer's, and the home page no longer calls every reviewer independent.

### Fixed

- **Vocabulary lenses no longer crash on GPT-2 (SIGBUS).** transformers memory-maps safetensors weights, and GPT-2's tied embedding starts at a misaligned address (19 bytes past a 64-byte boundary). A matrix-vector product on it, such as the lens's unembedding, died with a bus error in Apple's BLAS. Misaligned parameters are now copied into fresh memory when a model is loaded, which keeps tied weights tied.
- **Whole documents longer than the model's context fail clearly.** GPT-2 reads 1,024 positions, and scanning a longer document failed deep in the embedding with `IndexError`. `audit` now says which document is too long and suggests `--window-tokens`.
- **`openinterp next` now proposes re-reviews of self-reviewed experiments.** It read the `independent_review` *gate*, which passes self-reviews under the default policy, so it offered no `independent_review` candidates at all on today's registry; it now reads whether the review was independent and offers 72. `replicate` also fires for a VERIFIED pole whose every review was a self-review, even when several models reviewed it, and `challenge` needs at least one independent review.

## [0.0.6] - 2026-10-21

Ships with protocol version **0.7**.

### Added

- **`pile-uncopyrighted` corpus preset:** `openinterp corpus --dataset pile-uncopyrighted` samples [Pile Uncopyrighted](https://huggingface.co/datasets/monology/pile-uncopyrighted) (`monology/pile-uncopyrighted`), the Pile without its Books3, BookCorpus2, OpenSubtitles, YTSubtitles and OWT2 subsets, for when `pile-10k`'s 10,000 documents are too few to find a rare concept's activations. Its main branch is zstd-compressed JSONL, so like `c4` the preset reads the Hub's Parquet conversion (`refs/convert/parquet`, `default/partial-train`), which holds the first 891,748 training documents, about 89 times `pile-10k`. The card's licence is `other`: each Pile component keeps its own terms, so check an excerpt's source before redistributing it in the wiki. 500 documents take about a minute, and rebuilding at the recorded commit gives a byte-identical corpus.
- **Causal effects chart** on experiment pages. Each non-control minimal pair gets a sufficiency bar and a necessity bar: the percentile of the unit's |effect| among its matched same-layer units, drawn against the 0.5 line a unit must beat to be more influential than one that changes as much, with the medians (the recorded scores) in a last row. Hovering a bar shows its effect and prompts, and a table below gives every number. To draw it, verdicts now store each pair's effects as `causal_metrics.pairs` (`CausalPairEffect`: the pair's index and, for each direction, the signed effect, the percentile and the matched units' median and largest |effect|; optional, so older records validate). The decision rule is unchanged. Records without per-pair effects show the median row alone, `openinterp reevaluate` fills them in, and pre-0.5 records, whose scores have no 0.5 baseline, get no chart. Wiki pages gain a per-pair table. Recomputing L1/N12− and L13/N3725+ reproduced their recorded scores exactly.
- **Protocol 0.7: bounded scans.** `openinterp audit` scans a seeded sample of whole documents within a token budget instead of the whole corpus, so a scan's cost, and the scale (`max_activation`) every "active" threshold is a fraction of, no longer grow with the corpus. `--max-tokens` (default 200,000) and `--sample-seed` (default 0, so every audit of a corpus reads the same documents) set it, and `--max-tokens 0` scans everything. Documents are visited in seeded order and each is taken if it fits in what's left of the budget, counted with the model's tokenizer. Provenance records the sample as `corpus_sample` (`CorpusSample`: seed, budget, document and token counts, and the SHA-256 of the sampled documents as JSONL; optional, so older records validate), and experiment pages show it next to the corpus. `openinterp reproduce --corpus <corpus>` re-draws each record's sample and checks that it hashes the same and holds every top example. The decision rule is unchanged, but every existing verdict was judged under 0.6, so `openinterp next` now proposes re-evaluating them; re-evaluating keeps a record's full-corpus scale. On today's 500-document `corpus.jsonl` (212,232 Qwen2.5 tokens) the default sample is 477 documents and 199,896 tokens, so scans are about 6% shorter. The saving grows with the corpus, and a smaller `--max-tokens` trades scan time for a lower scale.
- **Revisions link the experiment they replace.** `openinterp audit --supersedes wiki/experiments/<slug>/<id>.json` records that experiment's id in the new submission's `supersedes` field (optional, so older records validate). It must be a reviewed experiment on the same unit, and in the two-step flow it goes on the `--hypothesis` step. `openinterp next` adds it to the commands it prints for `revise` and `new_hypothesis`. `openinterp check` requires the id to name an earlier experiment in the same unit's folder, and the page's front matter to match the record. Wiki pages gain a *Revises* row and the unit's history links each revision. Neuron and head pages show "Revises …" and "Revised by …" with links between the two experiments. Parquet gains `supersedes`. Which experiment sets a pole's status is unchanged: still the newest.
- **Input attribution** for neuron poles, reported, not gated (docs/PROTOCOL.md §4). `openinterp audit` now attributes the peak of each of the first 5 top examples to its context tokens. It uses integrated gradients over the input token embeddings (Sundararajan et al., 2017; implemented directly, no `captum`), from a pad-token baseline that keeps the first token, the attention sink. Steps double from 16 to at most 64 until the attributions sum to within 0.1 of the gap. Each claim is checked by replacing single tokens: the peak, the 4 before it and the 5 most attributed. The scan evidence and submissions store it as `input_attribution` (`InputAttribution`, optional, so older records validate). That holds each example's most attributed tokens with their ablation effects, the share of |attribution| by distance from the peak, the mean distance and the attribution–ablation Spearman ρ. The proposer's and the reviewer's prompts list those tokens, and the reviewer is told that a probe contains only the context it writes. The `interpretability-illusions-review` skill says how to use them. Experiment pages and wiki pages gain an *Input attribution* section. Parquet gains `current_token_share`, `context_mean_distance` and `context_ablation_agreement`. A new cost stage, `input_attribution`, takes 6 s to about 2 min per neuron on CPU for Qwen2.5-0.5B (deeper layers are slower and converge less often). On four registry neurons, L4/N1889+ converges on every example (47% on the peak token, ρ = 0.76), while layers 11–20 converge on 1–3 of 5 examples. Without the fixed first token, the integral did not converge on most deep-layer examples even at 256 steps, and it credited the first tokens of each window.
- **Re-audit of Qwen2.5-0.5B L11/N4255+** (`20261021T150406Z`): VERIFIED (balanced accuracy 0.81) under protocol 0.6. It used a 512-token-window scan, a narrowed claim (", including" after a plural class noun, with ", such as" / ", like" / ", notably" as weaker members, and amount-sense "including", "for example" and ", e.g." excluded) and a reviewer model different from the proposer (claude-sonnet-5-5 against claude-opus-5-5). The held-out explanation scores are at chance (detection 0.50, fuzzing 0.54): most of the pole's activity on ordinary text is a diffuse background the claim names but does not explain.

### Changed

- **README premise softened.** It no longer says native representations are "remarkably clean" as an established finding. It presents Goodfire's *Already Clean* essay (Cammarata, 2026) as a working hypothesis the registry tests, since the essay's technical companion paper has not been published, and drops the Bereska & Gavves (2024) citation, a review that does not make that claim.

## [0.0.5] - 2026-10-20

Ships with protocol version **0.6**.

### Added

- **Protocol 0.6: attention heads can be audited.** `openinterp audit --model M --layer L --head H` scans one head (`--unit head --random` samples one uniformly), and `score`, `review`, `reevaluate`, `reproduce` and `next` take head submissions like neuron ones. Submissions and scan evidence take any `ComponentIdentifier`; a head's carries a `HeadProfile`. A head's strength at a token is `|W_O (z − z̄)|`, the distance between its write to the residual stream and its mean write z̄ over the scan, which the record stores so every later measurement shares the zero point. The raw write norm doesn't work: on GPT-2 small L5H1 the median token sits at a quarter of the maximum, so half of all text would be "active". Against z̄, the five known GPT-2 small induction heads are active on every second-half token of repeated random tokens and almost no first-half token. A head is ablated by setting z to z̄ (mean ablation), and its causal metrics rank it against the other heads of its layer, matched on the change in each head's write. The scan also records the tokens each top example's peak attends to and the head's previous-token, duplicate-token and induction scores (Olsson et al., 2022); heads get no vocabulary lens or partner search. Head commands load the model with eager attention. Head pages live at `models/<model>/L<layer>/H<idx>.html` and in `wiki/experiments/<model>/L<layer>/H<idx>/`. Parquet gains `kind`, `head_idx`, `model_n_heads` and the three pattern scores (`neuron_idx` and `pole` are null for heads), and the home page counts heads studied. The decision rule for neuron poles is unchanged, but every existing verdict was judged under 0.5, so `openinterp next` now proposes re-evaluating them. A positive-control dry run on GPT-2 small L5H1 scanned 235,650 tokens in about 2 minutes on CPU (induction score 0.89) and the proposer identified it as an induction head unprompted. It was NEEDS_REVISION after two rounds, on specificity alone (see TODO).
- **Explanation scores on held-out text** (Delphi; Paulo et al., 2025), reported, not gated. `openinterp score` cuts the corpus snippets the proposer never saw (everything but the scan's top examples) into 32-token windows. It measures each window on its own, as the judge reads it, and draws active windows evenly from 5 activation bins, plus inactive ones. Three scores:
  - *detection:* a judge given only the explanation picks the windows that activate the pole;
  - *fuzzing:* the judge decides whether the marked tokens in a window are the ones that activate it;
  - *embedding:* the AUROC of explanation–window cosine from a local sentence encoder (`sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2`), with no LLM.

  The judge is a LiteLLM model, or two-step: `--emit-task` writes the windows and two prompts (no activations), and `--task` scores the answers. The new `explanation-judge` subagent answers in Claude Code. **Every review records them.** `review --probes` requires `--explanation-scores`, and a one-step `review` scores the explanation itself on `--corpus`, with `--judge-model` defaulting to the reviewer. `reevaluate` stays LLM-free, so `--explanation-scores` is optional there. When no held-out window is active, detection scores false positives only and fuzzing is skipped, so scoring never blocks a review. Both commands record them as `VerificationVerdict.explanation_scores` (`ExplanationScores`, optional, so older records validate). `openinterp reproduce` re-measures the windows and re-derives the scores from the stored answers, warning on differences. Experiment pages gain an *Explanation scores on held-out text* section. It has tiles for the three scores, detection recall per activation bin, and the judged windows as token heatmaps, with the explanation's misses and false alarms first and fuzzing marks dashed. `make sample-run` includes synthetic scores. Parquet gains `detection_score`, `fuzzing_score` and `embedding_auroc`. Scoring takes about 2 minutes on CPU for Qwen2.5-0.5B. On L1/N12− (VERIFIED, a bigram detector), only 69 of 4000 held-out windows were active, and a literal reading of the explanation found none of them. Its held-out activity is mostly ` in` after verbs other than *look*, which top examples alone don't show.
- **Windowed scans:** `openinterp audit --window-tokens N [--stride S]` scans each document as overlapping N-token windows that start every S tokens (default N/2), instead of whole. Long documents are read in full, and the scan's top examples become windows rather than whole documents. Each token counts once in the histogram, confound profile and partner search: in the first window, or else in the fresh tail of the first later window that reaches it, so it has at least N − S tokens of context. Top examples never overlap, and they are decoded back to text, so `reproduce` re-measures them exactly as the scan did. On Qwen2.5-0.5B, a 256/128 scan counts exactly the tokens a whole-document scan does. Provenance records `snippet_tokens` and the new `snippet_stride`, and experiment pages show them. The vocabulary lens then reads each of the first 100 documents' first window. `openinterp score` now holds out every document that contains a top example, not only exact matches. Whole documents stay the default, so existing scans are unchanged.
- **Experiment costs**, reported, never gated. Every record stores what its experiment cost in `submission.provenance.costs` (`ExperimentCosts`, optional, so older records validate). That is wall time per stage (`model_load`, `scan`, `lenses`, `proposer`, `counterfactuals`, `heldout_windows`, `judge`, `embedding`, `remeasure`, `reviewer`, `probe_measurement`, `gates`, `causal_metrics`) and, per LLM call, the model, attempts, prompt characters, prompt/completion/cached tokens and cost from LiteLLM (read through an instructor response hook, summed over retries). The two-step flow carries costs through its files: evidence → submission, score task → scores → record. A prompt answered from a file is timed from the prompt's mtime to the answer's, with no token counts. Re-reviewing a record or re-evaluating it records only the new run's costs. Experiment pages show a *Run cost* line. With `OPENINTERP_COSTS_LOG=<file>` set, every `openinterp` command (including `check`, `wiki` and `build`) appends its wall time, stages and LLM calls to that JSON-lines ledger, and the new `openinterp costs <ledger…> [--session <file>]` summarises ledgers and `claude -p --output-format json` results. `scripts/experiment-loop.sh` now runs sessions with `--output-format json`, keeps a ledger per experiment in `logs/loop/`, and prints each experiment's cost and a running total. Tests clear `OPENINTERP_COSTS_LOG`, so `make test` inside a loop session doesn't add to it. `make test` and `make lint` are not timed separately; they fall under the session's wall time.
- **Probe results chart** on experiment pages, above the adversarial review table. Every hard negative and positive holdout is a dot on one pole-strength axis, with the reviewer's threshold (`activation_threshold` × max activation) drawn as a line and the active side shaded. Passed probes are green dots and failed ones red crosses, so pass/fail never relies on colour alone. Dots at the same value are dodged vertically rather than hidden, and hovering one shows its strength and prompt. The axis extends past the scan max when a probe exceeds it. It is static SVG rendered at build time, like the histogram; the values stay in the table below. Records without a recorded policy or max activation show the table only.
- **`c4` corpus preset:** `openinterp corpus --dataset c4` samples the English validation split of [C4](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/c4) (`allenai/c4`), the web baseline much of the SAE literature uses. C4's main branch is gzipped JSON, so the preset reads the Hub's Parquet conversion (`refs/convert/parquet`, `en/partial-validation`); presets can now name a default `revision`, and `--revision` still overrides it. That branch has no dataset card, so when the pinned revision has none, the licence comes from the default branch's card (ODC-By here). 500 documents take about 40 seconds, and rebuilding at the recorded commit gives a byte-identical corpus.
- **`land-experiment` skill:** `choose-next-experiment` plus building the site, waiting for CI to go green and merging the PR. It merges only when every check passed on the current head, the PR touches nothing outside `wiki/`, and the agent's `gh` account has write access; otherwise it comments on the PR and moves on to the next experiment.

### Changed

- **`make sample-run` builds from real records.** It imports six experiments from `wiki/experiments/` (VERIFIED, NEEDS_REVISION and REJECTED, a neuron with two experiments, and two with held-out explanation scores) instead of generating synthetic ones. `examples/make_sample_audits.py` and the `examples/audits/` fixtures are gone.
- **Generated wiki indexes are no longer committed.** `wiki/README.md` and each neuron's `README.md` are gitignored and rebuilt locally by `openinterp wiki` (and by `verify`, `review` and `reevaluate`). Every experiment PR used to edit the same index rows, so parallel PRs conflicted. `openinterp check` no longer compares indexes and instead rejects a PR that commits one.
- **`scripts/experiment-loop.sh` lands experiments:** each session follows `land-experiment`, and may also run `gh run` and `gh repo view`. Its result line gains `merged` or `open`.

## [0.0.4] - 2026-10-19

Ships with protocol version **0.5**.

### Changed

- **Deploy on every push to `main`.** The site went stale because the deploy relied on an hourly cron, and GitHub's scheduled workflows are best-effort: on 2026-10-19 it fired once in about 7 hours. Every scheduled and manual run did deploy, so the `R2_BUCKET` guard was not the cause. The schedule stays as a 6-hourly backstop.
- **`openinterp next` explores twice as often:** the default `--epsilon` goes from 0.35 to 0.7, so 70% of draws audit a new random neuron rather than following up on one already studied. `scripts/experiment-loop.sh` uses the same default.
- **Token heatmaps** on experiment pages, Neuroscope-style. Each top example shows 30 tokens before and 10 after its peak, with the full snippet expandable. The peak token is underlined and bold, and hovering a token shows its exact stored strength (before, the value was rebuilt from a capped, rounded shade). A colour legend runs from 0 to the neuron's max, and each example has a values table for keyboard and screen-reader users. Newline tokens show as `↵` so they can be shaded. The tint is capped at 80%, so token text keeps at least 4.5:1 contrast in light and dark mode; the uncapped orange fell to 3.25:1 in dark mode.

- **Protocol 0.5: causal metrics that can see a single neuron.** Sufficiency and necessity are now scored at the position where the pole's strength differs most between a minimal pair's prompts. The target is the claimed upcoming tokens for lookahead claims and next-token KL otherwise. Each score is the median percentile of the pole's effect among the 32 same-layer neurons (`ReviewPolicy.causal_null_neurons`) whose strength changes as much between the pair. Control pairs are skipped. Before, the score was the fraction of the top-1-logit gap restored, which read ≈ 0 for every neuron. On the four Qwen2.5-0.5B neuron poles in the registry the new scores range from about 0.6 (L11/N4255+) to 1.0 (L23/N726−). Still recorded, not gated. `CausalMetrics` gains `target`, `target_tokens`, `sufficiency_effect`, `necessity_effect`, `null_neurons` and `pairs_scored`; all are optional, so older records still validate. Parquet gains `causal_target`.

### Added

- `openinterp corpus`: samples a scan corpus from a Hugging Face Parquet dataset without downloading all of it, by reading seeded random row groups over HTTP. Presets: `fineweb-edu` (`HuggingFaceFW/fineweb-edu`, sample-10BT; the default) and `pile-10k`, or any dataset with `--files`. It writes `corpus.jsonl` and a `corpus.source.json` sidecar (`CorpusSource`: dataset, commit, files, seed, sizes, licence, SHA-256). Audits record the source in `provenance.corpus_source` when the corpus still matches it, and experiment pages show the licence. The same seed and revision rebuild a byte-identical corpus. Replaces the README's Pile snippet.
- **Attention heads, step 1: identifiers and hooks.** `ComponentIdentifier` is a union of `NeuronIdentifier` (`kind = "mlp_neuron_pole"`, the default, so existing records validate unchanged) and the new `HeadIdentifier` (`kind = "attn_head"`, `head_idx`, slug `L<layer>/H<idx>`). `NeuronHookRunner` resolves each layer's attention output projection (Llama/Qwen/Mistral/Gemma/Phi, GPT-2, GPT-NeoX, GPT-J, OPT, Falcon) and gains `head_outputs`, `head_write_norm`, `head_output_weights`, `attention_patterns` and `hooks(head_interventions=…)`, plus `ablate_head`. `_load_runner` takes `attn_implementation` (patterns need `"eager"`). Submissions still accept neuron poles only and reject a head with a clear message, so the decision rule and `PROTOCOL_VERSION` are unchanged.
- **Most recent experiments** on the home page: the latest 10 experiments, whatever their verdict, newest first, with review time (UTC), neuron, status, evidence strength, hypothesis, proposer and reviewer, and a tag for re-measurements. On small screens the date moves under the neuron.
- **Proposer and reviewer as Claude Code subagents, with no API key.** `.claude/agents/neuron-proposer.md` and `neuron-reviewer.md` answer prompt files. The reviewer has no tool that runs the model, so it can't see probe activations before its probe set is final.
- Two-step CLI commands that write a prompt and exit, then take the answer as JSON, so no process waits on an answer file: `openinterp audit --evidence-out` (scan evidence as `ScanEvidence` JSON plus the proposer's prompt), `audit --evidence … --hypothesis …` (builds the submission without rescanning), `review --emit-prompt` (loads no model) and `review --probes …`. `--agent-model` is required with an answer file, so the record names the model that wrote it.
- `scripts/experiment-loop.sh`: for `external/<name>` models the driving session now uses the subagents.
- CI job **Reproduce records**: runs `openinterp reproduce --base-ref origin/<base>` on every pull request, on CPU, with Hugging Face weights cached between runs. Only models in its `ALLOWED_MODELS` are loaded (`openinterp reproduce --allow-model`). Failures appear as file annotations and in the job summary.
- Auto-signing: `verify`/`review` and `reevaluate` sign each new experiment record when the machine holds a signing key (`~/.ssh/openinterp_signing` or `$OPENINTERP_SIGNING_KEY`) that is listed in `.github/allowed_signers`, so a maintainer's own runs and experiment loops skip CI reproduction. `OPENINTERP_AUTO_SIGN=0` turns it off.
- `openinterp sign`: a maintainer vouches for experiment records with an SSH key (`ssh-keygen -Y sign`), writing `<id>.json.sig` next to each record. `reproduce` skips records signed by a key in `.github/allowed_signers`, read from the base branch under `--base-ref` so a pull request can't trust its own key, and fails on a signature that doesn't verify. `openinterp check` flags signatures whose record is missing. A signature doesn't change the verdict.
- `openinterp reproduce`: re-runs a record's scan evidence, counterfactual deltas and review against the model commit and dtype it pins, under its own policy and with no LLM calls. It fails on any number outside tolerance (`--atol` × scale + `--rtol` × |recorded|) and on any differing pass/fail, gate or status. `--base-ref` picks every record a branch adds or changes.
- `RunEnvironment` (device, weights dtype, model commit, openinterp/torch/transformers versions) is recorded on every verdict and on the scan's provenance. The model commit is now resolved from the Hugging Face cache, because `config._commit_hash` is empty in recent transformers: five of the seven records in the registry have no `model_revision`. `_load_runner` takes a `revision`.
- `ActivationPatcher.patch_many`: patch many neurons of one layer, one per batch row, with any effect function (`metric_change`, `next_token_kl`). `logit_direction_metric` scores logits along a centred vocabulary direction.

## [0.0.2] - 2026-10-18

Ships with protocol version **0.3**.

### Added

- README "Get started" guide for contributors: setup, API keys for an independent proposer and reviewer, building a Pile scan corpus, running an experiment with Claude Code or by hand, and claiming and posting it as a PR. `submissions/` and `corpus.jsonl` are now git-ignored.
- **Protocol v0.3: covariance and confounds.**
  - Near-duplicate probes cluster into single observations (`core/stats.py`). The probe-count gates, balanced accuracy and significance use these effective counts.
  - The scan (`harness/scan.py`) gathers in one pass the top examples, the most correlated same-layer neurons (`harness/covariance.py`) and a confound profile: token-identity and position R², frequency correlation and trigger tokens (`harness/confounds.py`).
  - New gate `beats_confound_baseline`: the hypothesis must match or beat token-identity, position and never-active baselines on the reviewer's probes.
  - Joint ablation of the pole with its correlated partners, reporting a redundancy ratio (recorded, not gated).
  - Re-measurements of an earlier probe set are marked as not independent, rank after the original, and are excluded from the most-conclusive list.
  - The home page estimates expected false discoveries among VERIFIED claims.
  - Neuron pages summarise both poles together.
  - The proposer sees the confound profile and correlated neurons.
- **Jacobian lens** (`harness/jlens.py`; Gurnee et al., 2026, arXiv:2607.15495).
  - Reads each pole's output direction through both the context-averaged Jacobian lens and the logit lens, and records `submission.vocabulary_lens` (both token lists, overlap, cos(w, J·w), kurtosis).
  - Estimates J·w from one perturbed forward pass per prompt and sign. Tests check it against exact autograd on Llama-style and GPT-2 models.
  - On Qwen2.5-0.5B the two lenses share 0/10 tokens up to the middle layers and converge only near the output.
  - Shown on the site and wiki, exported to Parquet, fed to the proposer; the vocabulary skill now defaults to it.
- **Rank-based and regime-aware correlation.**
  - Frequency correlation is Spearman's ρ (tie-aware, exact).
  - Partner neurons are found per *pole* by co-activation lift (`PartnerSearch`): a fixed-seed reservoir sample plus the target's top tokens, with chunked Spearman and Pearson kept for reference.
  - Partners are ranked and selected by the 95% Wilson lower bound of the lift, which the new `min_partner_lift` and `min_partner_coactive` policy fields govern.
  - Global Pearson missed real partners: on L11/N4255 every |r| was below 0.07, while co-activation lift was at least 10.
- Every experiment is published, null and superseded results included. The site follows Model → Layer → Neuron → Experiments, with model and layer index pages, and each neuron page lists all experiments on both poles.
- Experiments are ranked by *conclusiveness*: the posterior probability that balanced accuracy exceeds 0.6, folded to |2P − 1|, so strong evidence ranks high in either direction (`builder/significance.py`). The home page features the most conclusive results in both directions.
- A write-once experiment archive: `wiki/experiments/**/<id>.json` stores each experiment's full record next to its page. `openinterp check` validates it and rejects edits to it.

### Changed

- `openinterp build` reads the wiki archive (`--wiki-dir`) instead of `audits/`, and `--include-unverified` is gone because everything is published.
- `registry.parquet` has one row per experiment, with `experiment_id`, `is_latest`, `p_useful`, `conclusiveness` and `evidence_direction`.
- Neuron pages moved from `neurons/<slug>.html` to `models/<model>/L<layer>/N<idx>.html`.

## [0.0.1] - 2026-10-18

First release. Ships with protocol version **0.2**.

### Added

**Harness and protocol**

- Strict Pydantic v2 schemas for neuron identifiers, counterfactual pairs, lookahead profiles, causal metrics, submissions, verdicts and audit records.
- `NeuronHookRunner`: hooks on MLP down-projection inputs for Llama/Qwen/Mistral/Gemma, GPT-2, GPT-NeoX, GPT-J, OPT and Phi, with positive/negative pole isolation and corpus scanning.
- Activation patching: denoising, noising and resample ablation per pole, with optional freezing of downstream MLP outputs to mitigate self-repair.
- Attribution patching for whole layers in one backward pass.
- Lookahead profiling (pole-ablation KL and concentration) and vocabulary projection of neuron output directions.
- `ProposerAgent` and `ReviewerAgent` using LiteLLM and instructor (default model `anthropic/claude-opus-5-5`). Verdicts are computed deterministically by `evaluate_submission`.
- Protocol v0.2 (`docs/PROTOCOL.md`): a gated decision rule using balanced accuracy over hard negatives and positive holdouts, a counterfactual tolerance relative to the prediction, minimum probe counts and an independent-review gate. It replaced v0.1's weighted mean and absolute Δ tolerance of 0.35 before release, after v0.1 verified an over-broad hypothesis.
- Experiment provenance on submissions (model revision, corpus hash, selection method, seed) and the review policy stored on verdicts.
- CLI commands `audit` (including `--random --seed`), `verify`/`review` (which also accepts an existing audit record), `reevaluate`, `build`, `deploy`, `wiki`, `check` and `next`.

- Experiment wiki (`wiki/`): a write-once Markdown page per completed experiment, with per-neuron history pages and a home index. `verify` and `reevaluate` write pages automatically, `reevaluate` saves the superseded verdict before overwriting a record, and `openinterp wiki` backfills existing records.
- `openinterp next` and the `choose-next-experiment` skill: a randomised next-experiment planner (ε-greedy exploration of random neurons, Boltzmann sampling over follow-ups, decay for repeats, recency and open-PR claims). It prints a seed for reproducibility.
- Static site builder (Jinja2 + Tailwind) with per-layer coverage, leaderboards, Pagefind search and a DuckDB-Wasm SQL console. The registry is exported as `registry.parquet` plus content-addressed snapshots.
- The build is deterministic. There are no build timestamps, orderings are total, and stale pages are removed, so unchanged records rebuild byte-for-byte.
- Cloudflare R2 delta sync with per-file cache policies:
  - content-addressed files upload first and HTML last;
  - `pagefind/` only syncs when a source file changed;
  - `data/snapshots/` is never deleted;
  - an optional Cloudflare cache purge covers only the changed URLs.

**Project infrastructure**

- GitHub Actions CI: lint, a 500-line limit on source and documentation files, tests, and `openinterp check` (audit/wiki pairing, stale indexes and write-once pages) on every PR.
- Hourly deploy workflow that rebuilds from `main` and delta-syncs to R2. It is skipped until the `R2_BUCKET` variable is set.
- A PR template with an experiment checklist.
- Ten Claude Code research skills in `.claude/skills/`, each grounded in the interpretability literature.
