## Summary

**Claim.** LoRA gives every adapted weight matrix the same rank. AdaLoRA instead gives more rank to the matrices whose update matters more to the task. It writes each update in the form of a singular value decomposition (SVD), and prunes the singular values with low importance scores during training. At a matched number of trainable parameters, it does as well as or better than LoRA and adapters, most of all at low budgets.

**Method.**

- **Motivation (Figure 1).** On DeBERTaV3-base and MNLI, with 0.28M trainable parameters, the authors apply LoRA to one type of matrix only, or to three layers only. The paper reads from this that FFN matrices and top layers matter more than the others.
- **The update.** Each adapted matrix is W = W⁽⁰⁾ + PΛQ (Equation 3). P and Q stand for left and right singular vectors, and Λ is a diagonal of r singular values. Λ starts at zero, and P and Q start Gaussian, so the update starts at zero. A penalty ‖PᵀP − I‖²_F + ‖QQᵀ − I‖²_F pushes P and Q toward orthogonal (Equation 4).
- **Triplets.** The i-th singular value and its two vectors form one triplet. Pruning sets a triplet's singular value to zero, but keeps its vectors in training. So a pruned triplet can come back later. Section 3.1 contrasts this with pruning LoRA's A and B, which sets all of a pair's entries to zero.
- **Importance.** A triplet's score is the sum of its singular value's score and the mean scores of its vector entries (Equation 7). Each entry's base score is the sensitivity |w · ∂L/∂w| (Equation 8). The paper says this "approximates the change in loss when a parameter is zeroed out". It then smooths the sensitivity by an exponential moving average and multiplies it by an uncertainty term, following Zhang et al. (2022) (Equations 9 to 11).
- **Budget.** The budget is the total count of nonzero singular values over all adapted matrices. Training starts at 1.5 times the final budget and holds it for a warm-up. Then a cubic schedule lowers it, and the last steps keep the allocation fixed (Section 3.3, Appendix A). Pruning happens every ΔT steps, for example 100.
- **Where it adapts.** Wq, Wk, Wv, Wo, Wf1 and Wf2 of every layer. The FFN is two linear maps, Wf1 ∈ R^(d×dm) then Wf2 ∈ R^(dm×d).
- **Models and tasks.** DeBERTaV3-base (183M parameters) on GLUE, SQuAD v1.1 and SQuAD v2.0. BART-large on XSum and CNN/DailyMail.
- **Baselines.** Full fine-tuning, BitFit, the Houlsby and Pfeiffer adapters, and LoRA on all the same matrices. Appendix F shows that LoRA on all matrices beats LoRA on two. Ablations compare AdaLoRA with pruned LoRA, two simpler scores, SVD-LoRA without pruning, and LoRA with the orthogonal penalty.
- **Tuning and statistics.** Each method's learning rate is chosen from a list (Appendix C.2, D.2 and E.2). GLUE results are on the development set, as the mean of 5 seeds. The paper says that all its gains pass significance tests at p < 0.05. It names no test and gives no spread.

## Where the claims go beyond the evidence

- **The text and Figure 3 disagree on the FFN.** Section 4.4 says AdaLoRA "always prefers" more budget for FFNs and top layers. But in Figure 3's extracted cells, Wf2 has a lower rank than each of Wq, Wk, Wv and Wo in 10 of 12 layers. In layer 12, Wf2 has rank 0 and Wf1 rank 2. So the result is that Wf1 receives a high rank, not the whole FFN.
- **The paper shows the "consistent" allocation once.** Section 4.4 says the rank pattern is "consistent across different budget levels, tasks and models". The paper shows one allocation: DeBERTaV3-base on MNLI. It gives no figure or number for the other cases.
- **The motivation rests on one model, one task and one run.** Figure 1 is DeBERTaV3-base on MNLI, with no seeds or intervals. The spread across single matrix types is 1.4 points.
- **The authors tune hyperparameters on the reported set.** GLUE results are on the development set. Each method's learning rate is the best from a list, and AdaLoRA also tunes γ, the schedule steps and ΔT per task. The paper reports no separate test set for GLUE.
- **Significance with no test and no spread.** The paper claims p < 0.05 for "all the gains", but names no test and gives no standard deviation. Only Table 1 states a seed count. Many gaps are small, for example 45.00 against 45.03 ROUGE-1 on CNN/DailyMail, where LoRA is higher.
- **No random allocation.** No baseline prunes triplets at random, or by a fixed per-layer rule, at the same budget. SVD-LoRA keeps a fixed rank. So the share of the gain due to the importance score, not to SVD and the schedule, is uncertain. Table 4's simpler scores come closest, and the authors deserve credit for them.
- **The starting budget is larger.** AdaLoRA trains at 1.5 times its final rank before it prunes. For example, it starts at rank 12 where LoRA uses rank 8 (Table 9). The paper matches the final count only.
- **Small inconsistencies.** Appendix H says the overhead is 16% "on SQuADv2", but Table 15 shows SST-2. Table 14's row says "LoRA (Wq, Wk)", but the text says Wq and Wv. Table 12 gives budgets of 0.08% to 0.65% for summarisation, but Table 3 uses 0.13% to 2.20%.
- **The paper checks orthogonality on two matrices.** Appendix G plots the penalty for Wo and Wf2 of the first layer, on SST-2 only.
- **Nothing about units or forgetting.** The paper measures task scores only. It does not measure what the update does to the base model's other skills, neurons or features. It does not discuss merging the update into the weights.

## Use in this repo

- **The repo has no LoRA or fine-tuning code.** [`fixing-models.md`](fixing-models.md) notes that the repo has no fine-tuning or weight-edit code. DeBERTaV3 and BART are not registry models. So the paper's runs can't be repeated here as they are.
- **The merged update is still additive, so the earlier point holds.** PΛQ adds to W⁽⁰⁾ like LoRA's BA, so it can merge the same way. A rank-r update to Wf1 moves every neuron's input weights inside one shared r-dimensional subspace. This is our inference, and it agrees with [`lora-learns-less-forgets-less.md`](lora-learns-less-forgets-less.md). AdaLoRA adds one thing: it can leave a matrix at rank 0, as Figure 3 does for Wf2 in layer 12. A pole whose input and output matrices both stay unchanged can move only through its inputs.
- **The rank pattern falls on the side of a neuron that its explanation describes.** This is our inference from Figure 3. A column of Wf1 holds one neuron's input weights, and a row of Wf2 its output weights. On MNLI, AdaLoRA gives high rank to Wf1 and low rank to Wf2. A registry explanation says when a pole fires, which the input weights set. So if the pattern holds for a decoder, a tuning would change the part of a neuron that the explanation describes. Qwen2.5 has Wgate and Wup on the input side and Wdown on the output side. #5133 could split Δ's rank by these three matrices to test this on the base-instruct pairs. That is a comment for #5133, not a new issue.
- **The importance score is a first-order loss change, as the repo's pole effect is.** Equation 8 is |w · ∂L/∂w|, a first-order estimate of the loss change when a weight goes to zero. `open-interpretability pole-effects` estimates the loss change when a pole is mean-ablated, as ∂L/∂h × (mean − part) ([`docs/analyses/pole-effects.md`](../analyses/pole-effects.md), `openinterpretability/pole_effects.py`). On Qwen2.5-0.5B that estimate ranks 60 checked poles at a Spearman of 0.85 against exact ablation. On GPT-2 small it is 0.97. AdaLoRA checks its score only by the task score at the end, never against exact pruning.
- **The differences matter.** AdaLoRA scores adapter weights on the task's loss, during training, with smoothing and an uncertainty factor. The repo scores activations of the base model on `corpus.jsonl`, once. AdaLoRA's score is not the diagonal Fisher of #5118. The Fisher is the expected squared gradient, with no weight factor, and so it is a measure of curvature. AdaLoRA's score is a weight times a gradient, so it estimates the loss change from removing that weight.
- **Two literatures predict opposite signs, and the repo can test both.** In EWC (#5118), high importance marks weights that a new task should not change. In AdaLoRA, high sensitivity draws more of the update. If a tuning acts like AdaLoRA, poles with a large loss effect would move most. If it acts like EWC's premise, they would move least. This is our inference. AdaLoRA's score is on the adapter and the task loss, not on base-model poles and the pretraining loss.
- **The data for that test exist in part.** [`docs/analyses/base-instruct.md`](../analyses/base-instruct.md) has 311 Qwen2.5-0.5B poles, of which 252 keep (81%) and 59 fail with plain probes. `data/pole-effects/Qwen--Qwen2.5-0.5B.json` holds only the top 2,000 of 233,472 poles. So most of the 311 poles have no stored effect, and the command must run again with a larger `--top`.
- **Depth agrees only loosely.** Figure 1b says top layers matter more for tuning on MNLI. On Qwen2.5-0.5B, the middle third keeps least: 63 of 92, against 104 of 112 and 85 of 107. On Qwen2.5-1.5B, three of the five failures are in the last third. The models, tasks and measures differ, so this page reads no link.
- **Evidence for the fix campaign, not a design.** A fix for the #4781 " days" error would be a narrow change. AdaLoRA's allocation shows one way to put a small budget where a task needs it. It measures no side effects, so it says nothing about which VERIFIED poles a fix would break. [`fixing-models.md`](fixing-models.md) holds the repo's synthesis on fixes.
- **Question for the maintainer.** Should a weight fix in the campaign report its update's rank per matrix, as AdaLoRA's allocation does, next to its norm? That choice belongs to the campaign, so this page records it as a question and opens no issue.
- **Follow-up: test the pole effect as a predictor of keep or fail.** Run `open-interpretability pole-effects` on Qwen2.5-0.5B with every pole kept. Then read each base-instruct pole's estimated effect, and test it against keep or fail in `base-instruct-0.5b.jsonl`. Use a null of shuffled poles, and share the rows with #5118, #5133 and #5141. The test has two sides: a large effect could predict keeping (EWC) or moving (AdaLoRA). It trains nothing, and is part of #4782.
