# Literature review

One page per paper or post. Each has its citation, a summary written from the source, and a separate discussion of how this repo can or can't use it. The issue *A literature review in docs/literature* sets the scope. Papers and posts waiting for a page are in [queue.md](queue.md). The `literature-review` skill (`.claude/skills/literature-review/SKILL.md`) gives the procedure and the form of a page.

Status marks how the work relates to this repo:

- **used**: code, a skill or the protocol depends on it.
- **planned**: an open issue would use it.
- **not applicable**: reviewed, and the repo has no use for it. The page says why.

## Explanation scoring

| Page | Status | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Scoring Explanations Without Reading Them](scoring-explanations-without-reading-them.md) (Chen, 2026) | used | Evaluation items can be scored by rules that never read the explanation. Our null-explanation control comes from it, and its check found that our fuzzing decoys leak contiguity. |
| [Beyond Top Activations](beyond-top-activations.md) (Oikarinen, Yan, Kulkarni and Weng, 2025) | planned | Evaluating on top activations alone measures only recall. Model-guided importance sampling finds false positives cheaply. Our detection already stratifies active windows, but draws inactive ones uniformly (#3595). |
| [Evaluating Neuron Explanations](evaluating-neuron-explanations.md) (Oikarinen, Yan and Weng, 2025) | used | Two sanity tests for explanation metrics, which `scripts/score_sanity.py` runs on our scores. | [Faithful and Stable Neuron Explanations](faithful-stable-explanations.md) (Yan, Oikarinen and Weng, 2025) | not applicable | Sampling bounds and a bootstrap for vision-neuron explanations. Our P(useful) already carries the probe-count uncertainty: with 20 hard negatives, the paper's rate gives ±0.30. Replicate reviews test stability. |
| [Robustness of Model-Graded Evaluations](model-graded-robustness.md) (Lermen and Kvapil, 2023) | not applicable | Injected text sways LLM graders of quality. Our verdicts are measured, our scores are checked against measured activations, and prompts fence untrusted text. |
| [Causally Grounded Explanations](causally-grounded-explanations.md) (Mahale, 2026) | not applicable | An MSc thesis on six IOI heads in GPT-2 small, with no held-out tests. Its sufficiency is 100% by construction, which shows why our causal scores rank against matched neurons. |
| [Understanding AI from the Inside](understanding-ai-from-the-inside.md) (Barez, Wu et al., 2026) | planned | A 183-page survey with an evaluation protocol and guardrails for automated interpretability. The registry already has most of both. Its missing guardrail, a blind human audit of a random sample of verdicts, is #4034. |

## Probing

| Page | Status | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Designing and Interpreting Probes with Control Tasks](control-tasks.md) (Hewitt and Liang, 2019) | used | A control task gives each word type a random output, so only the probe can learn it. Selectivity is task accuracy minus control accuracy. `papers/default-on-poles` cites it correctly. The probe comparison has no control task yet. |
| [Activation Probes Are Reliable With a Handful of Positive Examples](activation-probes-few-positives.md) (Tyagi and Heimersheim, 2026) | not applicable | Train on all the negatives, with a class-weighted loss, when positives are rare. `probe-compare` already does. Our thin probe rows lack held-out positives, which the paper does not address. |
| [Is a Linear Probe Evidence of a Linear Representation?](linear-probe-evidence.md) (Kutluozen, Internò and Klindt, 2026) | planned | A random feature map also decodes factors linearly, so probe accuracy needs a random-init control. Our verdicts have one (the untrained null). `probe-compare`'s dense probes don't yet (#3559). |
| [Pretty Linear Liars: Unregularized Probes Favor Intervention-Unfaithful Features](pretty-linear-liars.md) (Emneby, Yocum and Allen, 2026) | planned | Unregularized probes decode best, but their directions are inert when intervened on. Ridge with a swept penalty comes close to causal search. It names a risk for the probe comparison's weakly regularised logistic probes, not for any verdict. |
| [Probing Classifiers: Promises, Shortcomings, and Advances](probing-classifiers.md) (Belinkov, 2022) | used | The review behind `papers/default-on-poles`' random-feature null and its tests by intervention. A probe needs baselines and controls, and it shows what is decodable, not what the model uses. Both of our quotes match the source. |

## Neurons, circuits and dictionaries

| Page | Status | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Convergent Learning: Do Different Neural Networks Learn the Same Representations?](convergent-learning.md) (Li, Yosinski, Clune et al., 2016) | used | Matches units across vision networks from different seeds by correlation. The sister search's mutual best match is a strict form of its one-to-one matching. It has no random baseline. Our citations check out. |
| [Efficient Streaming Language Models with Attention Sinks](attention-sinks.md) (Xiao, Tian, Chen et al., 2023) | used | Initial tokens draw attention whatever they mean, because softmax must put weight somewhere. The protocol calls the first token "the attention sink". The source didn't test GPT-2, so one preprint sentence now names the models it did test. |
| [Finding Neurons in a Haystack: Case Studies with Sparse Probing](neurons-in-a-haystack.md) (Gurnee, Nanda, Pauly et al., 2023) | used | The method behind `sparse-probing-search`. Across seven Pythia sizes, code languages and facts become more local with scale, and some features split. At k = 1 the mean-difference probe reaches F1 0.832, against 0.573 for random neurons. |
| [Intriguing Properties of Neural Networks](intriguing-properties.md) (Szegedy, Zaremba, Sutskever et al., 2014) | used | The claim that a random direction reads as well as a unit, judged by eye on 4 units and 4 directions per image model. The registry measures it. Our two quotes match. |
| [Language Model Circuits Are Sparse in the Neuron Basis](neuron-basis-circuits.md) (Arora, Wu, Steinhardt and Schwettmann, 2026) | planned | Task circuits of about 10² MLP neurons, the protocol's unit, control Llama 3.1 8B's behaviour. Its RelP attribution is a candidate for our wiring and attribution selection (#3582). |
| [Many Transcoder Features Are Neurons in Disguise](neurons-in-disguise.md) (Shabalin and Piotrowski, 2026) | used | On gated models, 14–59% of late-layer active transcoder features align with one neuron, and only those match the MLP across training runs. On GPT-2, 1 of our 40 audited latents is one (`docs/analyses/neuron-features.md`, #3608). |
| [Universal Neurons in GPT2 Language Models](universal-neurons.md) (Gurnee, Horsley, Guo et al., 2024) | used | 1–5% of GPT-2 neurons recur across seeds, measured by maximum correlation against a rotated baseline. The sister search uses the same method across model families. Our citations check out. |
| [What Survives Distillation?](what-survives-distillation.md) (Faumi and Sumanathilaka, 2026) | planned | DistilGPT-2 keeps GPT-2's early circuits and loses its late ones, by heads, CKA and SAE features. No neurons are studied. It makes DistilGPT-2 a cheap sibling for the sister search (#3853). |
| [Neurons Speak in Ranges](neurons-speak-in-ranges.md) (Haider, Rizwan, Sajjad, Ju and Siddique, 2026) | not applicable | Concepts occupy distinct activation ranges of residual-stream dimensions, and masking only the range spares other concepts. Not MLP neurons, but range-split modes are one form for #3215. |
| [Massive Activations in Large Language Models](massive-activations.md) (Sun, Chen, Kolter and Liu, 2024) | used | A few residual-stream values are huge and near-constant, on GPT-2's starting token. They act as biases and draw attention. One preprint sentence now names them apart from neuron activations. |
| [Neurons as an Interpretable Basis](neurons-as-an-interpretable-basis.md) (Tigges, Kowal, McGrath, Lewis and Cammarata, 2026) | planned | The companion to the essay behind `native-neuron-audit`, on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B. 47% of poles have one mode. The double-negative SwiGLU regime is a third of firings but rare in top examples. Its mode split is a template for #3215. |
| [Calibrated Concept Profiles](calibrated-concept-profiles.md) (Wilam, 2026) | not applicable | It reads every neuron's response to a named Python construct in OpenCoder-1.5B, which is the opposite direction to ours. Its twin draws, nulls and untrained reference match controls we run. Its untrained probe result (0.80) supports #3559. |
| [Interpreting Any Residual Stream Direction by Scaling Activation Inversion](activation-inversion.md) (De Schamphelaere et al., 2026) | not applicable | A 27B generator, trained for one model, writes text that drives a chosen residual direction. No generator exists for our models. It reports EPO, our dreaming optimiser, far below it, so dreaming's miss rate is a floor (#3779). |
| [The Hydra Effect](hydra-effect.md) (McGrath, Rahtz, Kramár et al., 2023) | used | When one attention layer is ablated, a later attention layer takes over, and late MLPs push the top token down less. The harness freezes later MLPs, which stops their response but not the attention layers'. Our citations check out. |
| [Toy Models of Superposition](toy-models-of-superposition.md) (Elhage, Hume, Olsson et al., 2022) | used | Its toy ReLU networks store more sparse features than they have dimensions, which explains polysemantic neurons. The privileged-basis argument behind our rotated-basis null and the superposition entity comes from it. `papers/why-neurons-rest` tests its negative-bias prediction. |

## Fixing models: editing and where it fails

Part of the review of how to fix a model's behaviour (#4784). These pages report evidence for the fix campaign, which the maintainer designs. They do not design it.

| Page | Status | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Does Localization Inform Editing?](does-localization-inform-editing.md) (Hase, Bansal, Kim and Ghandeharioun, 2023) | planned | Causal Tracing does not predict the best layer to edit a fact. The edit layer explains 94.7% of the variance in ROME's success, and tracing adds 0.1%. It tests the premise of a fix guided by an explanation (#4784, #5015). |
| [Generalization through Memorization: Nearest Neighbor Language Models](knn-lm.md) (Khandelwal, Levy, Jurafsky et al., 2019) | planned | A frozen model mixes its next-token distribution with a nearest-neighbour search over its own last-layer FFN inputs. Test perplexity falls from 18.65 to 16.12 on Wikitext-103. Its fixed λ acts on every token, so arm H of #5220 would need a gate. |
| [Net2Net: Accelerating Learning via Knowledge Transfer](net2net.md) (Chen, Goodfellow and Shlens, 2015) | planned | It grows a trained network wider, with copied units and outgoing weights divided by the count. Or it grows it deeper, with an identity layer that is exact only for activations such as ReLU. The patch format's zero output scale is a simpler exact start for arm I of #5220. The paper never measures what training does to the old units. |
| [The Mirage of Model Editing](mirage-of-model-editing.md) (Yang et al., 2025) | not applicable | Teacher forcing and truncation inflate editing scores. Llama-2 single-edit success is 38.5% under free decoding, and sequential editing collapses by 1,000 edits. Our answer measures read one token, so a fix to #4781 also needs a free-decoded check. |
| [Editing Factual Knowledge in Language Models](editing-factual-knowledge.md) (De Cao, Aziz and Titov, 2021) | planned | A hyper-network edits one fact under a KL constraint on other outputs. Its success, retain, paraphrase and deterioration scores are a ready test for a fix of the #4781 prompt (#5015). |
| [Fast Model Editing at Scale](fast-model-editing.md) (Mitchell, Lin, Bosselut, Finn and Manning, 2022) | not applicable | MEND turns one example's gradient into a weight edit, and checks reliability, generality and locality. Its random locality inputs are too easy. The shortlist prompts that copying answers right are harder negatives for a #4781 fix. |
| [Memory-Based Model Editing at Scale](memory-based-model-editing.md) (Mitchell, Lin, Bosselut, Manning and Finn, 2022) | not applicable | A black-box editor that keeps edits in a memory and leaves every unit unchanged. Its hard out-of-scope test still bears on a fix: three shortlist prompts are right because the model copies the first unit. |
| [Aging with GRACE](aging-with-grace.md) (Hartvigsen et al., 2023) | not applicable | A codebook at one layer swaps that layer's output inside a key's radius, with no change to the weights. It uses no explanation, so it could be a control for the fix campaign. It reports generality in figures only. |
| [Transformer-Patcher: One Mistake worth One Neuron](transformer-patcher.md) (Huang, Shen, Zhang et al., 2023) | planned | One trained neuron in the last FFN layer for each mistake, kept silent by a memory loss on 40,000 stored queries. Edits hold (ER 0.97 to 1.00) and paraphrases reach GR 0.82. It is the closest method to #5220's arms G and I, and its depth agrees with the copy-gate result. |
| [WISE: Knowledge Memory for Lifelong Model Editing](wise-lifelong-editing.md) (Wang, Li, Zhang et al., 2024) | not applicable | Sequential edits go in a gated side copy of one FFN matrix. It scores reliability, generalisation and locality together, and studies no units. Its untested near-edit locality case bears on a fix. |
| [Evaluating the Ripple Effects of Knowledge Editing](ripple-effects-of-knowledge-editing.md) (Cohen, Biran, Yoran, Globerson and Geva, 2024) | planned | An edit must also change the facts it implies. MEND, ROME and MEMIT fail most of these tests, and an in-context baseline does best. A #4781 fix needs such neighbour prompts (#5015). |
| [MQuAKE](mquake.md) (Zhong, Wu, Manning, Potts and Chen, 2023) | planned | Editors that recall over 88% of edited facts answer only 0.3–16.0% of the multi-hop questions that follow. So a test on the edited prompt alone overstates a fix. Its single-fact control bears on #5015. |
| [Editing Large Language Models: Problems, Methods, and Opportunities](editing-llms-survey.md) (Yao et al., 2023) | planned | A same-conditions comparison of about a dozen editing methods, on four properties. Single-neuron edits did poorly on GPT-J. Its Distract-Neighbour test is the copy habit behind #4781 (#5015). |
| [Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights](models-rewrite-own-weights.md) (Fan et al., 2026) | planned | A 27B model edits a direction or clips weight outliers, with no gradients. Its edit gains 6.6 points only after a framework check, and 1.1 without one. No random-direction control, so it bears on #5069 and #4782. |

## Fixing models: why models give wrong answers

Part of the review of how to fix a model's behaviour (#4784). These pages read the #4781 failure, where pythia-70m copies " days" from the first clause, in each paper's terms. They report evidence for #5015 and do not design the fix campaign.

| Page | Status | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Arithmetic Without Algorithms: Language Models Solve Math With a Bag of Heuristics](arithmetic-bag-of-heuristics.md) (Nikankin, Reusch, Mueller and Belinkov, 2024) | planned | In Llama3-8B, about 1.5% of MLP neurons carry arithmetic, and 91% of them match a simple heuristic such as an operand range. Knocking them out leaves about 66% accuracy. So a fix must route around many units, and Qwen's single-digit tokens may not follow the paper. |
| [In-context Learning and Induction Heads](induction-heads-in-context-learning.md) (Olsson, Elhage, Nanda, Joseph et al., 2022) | used | Our head profiles measure its prefix-matching pattern on repeated random tokens, but not its copying score. Its fuzzy [A*][B*]…[A]→[B*] copy predicts the pythia-70m " days" answer of #4781, including the cost when the true continuation differs. |
| [Copy Suppression](copy-suppression-head.md) (McDougall, Conmy, Rushing, McGrath and Nanda, 2023) | planned | GPT-2 Small's L10H7 lowers in-context tokens that earlier layers predict, and CSPA explains 76.9% of its effect. In pythia-70m only L4/H1 has this OV signature, and it is too weak and too early to stop the #4781 copy (#5015). |
| [Characterizing Mechanisms for Factual Recall in Language Models](factual-recall-mechanisms.md) (Yu, Merullo and Pavlick, 2023) | planned | Attention heads in Pythia and GPT-2 promote the memorized or the in-context answer, and scaling one head's values switches between them. It is the closest known mechanism to the #4781 copy failure, and a value-scaling sweep on the copy heads would test #5015's first question. |
| [Dissecting Recall of Factual Associations](dissecting-factual-recall.md) (Geva, Bastings, Filippova and Globerson, 2023) | planned | Factual recall runs in three steps: early MLPs enrich the subject, attention moves the relation, and heads extract the attribute. It gives #5015 two places to look for the missing " months", and two tools the repo lacks: edge knockout and a head vocabulary lens. |
| [Language Models Use Trigonometry to Do Addition](trigonometry-addition.md) (Kantamneni and Tegmark, 2025) | planned | GPT-J represents numbers 0–99 as a helix with periods 2, 5, 10 and 100, and adds by combining helices. About 1% of MLP neurons carry it, and periodic fits recover about 75% of their effect. It needs single-token numbers, as Pythia-70m and GPT-2 have and Qwen does not. |
| [Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability](grokking-progress-measures.md) (Nanda, Chan, Lieberum, Smith and Steinhardt, 2023) | planned | A fully reverse-engineered Fourier algorithm for modular addition in a one-layer model: 433 of 512 neurons follow one key frequency. Keeping only those frequencies cuts the loss by 70%. It sets the standard a compiled core in #5220 would meet. |
| [Transformer Feed-Forward Layers Are Key-Value Memories](ffn-key-value-memories.md) (Geva, Schuster, Berant and Levy, 2021) | used | The source of the key-value view in the `vocab-projection-lookahead` skill: keys detect prefix patterns, and values push next tokens. All of it is observational, from one 16-layer model with a uniform-random null. The page reads the #4781 " months" gap in its terms. |
| [Mechanistic Understanding and Mitigation of Language Model Non-Factual Hallucinations](non-factual-hallucination-mechanisms.md) (Yu, Cao, Cheung and Dong, 2024) | not applicable | Factual errors split into two types: lower MLPs fail to retrieve the answer, or upper attention fails to select it. Its errors come from recall, not in-context copying. Its logit-lens rank test could still sort the " days" failure (#5015). |
| [Do I Know This Entity?](knowledge-awareness-entities.md) (Ferrando, Obeso, Rajamanoharan and Nanda, 2025) | planned | SAE directions at an entity's last token signal whether Gemma 2 recognises it, and they gate how much attribute-extraction heads attend to it. That gives #5015 a test: do pythia-70m's copy heads attend to " days" because nothing at " year" recalls " months"? |
| [On the Biology of a Large Language Model](biology-of-a-large-language-model.md) (Lindsey, Gurnee, Ameisen et al., 2025) | planned | In Claude 3.5 Haiku, "can't answer" features are on by default and "known entity" features inhibit them, and a misfire gives some hallucinations. Its stated limits (copy completions, attention circuits, "why not X") match the #4781 copy failure. Its known/unknown contrast is a method for #5015. |
| [Does Fine-Tuning LLMs on New Knowledge Encourage Hallucinations?](fine-tuning-new-knowledge.md) (Gekhman et al., 2024) | planned | Fine-tuning on facts a model does not know is learned slowly and then makes it wrong on other answers. Its few-shot knowledge test (SliCK) would show whether pythia-70m knows "twelve months" before any fix by training (#5015). |
| [Why Language Models Hallucinate](why-language-models-hallucinate.md) (Kalai, Nachum, Vempala and Zhang, 2025) | planned | A theory that wrong answers are forced classification errors, made in pretraining and kept by benchmarks that give no credit for "I don't know". It names no units. Its "poor model" factor frames the copy-the-first-unit failure and predicts a scale test (#5015). |

## Fixing models: fixes guided by an explanation

Part of the review of how to fix a model's behaviour (#4784). These pages cover fixes that use an explanation, and the tests that show whether a fix removed a behaviour or only hid it. They report evidence and do not design the fix campaign.

| Page | Status | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Inducing Causal Structure for Interpretable Neural Networks](interchange-intervention-training.md) (Geiger, Wu, Lu et al., 2021) | planned | IIT trains hand-chosen neurons to carry a causal model's variables under interchange. It helps held-out splits, most when combined with probe training. It never trains only an interface around a frozen core, which phase 5(b) of #5220 would need. |
| [Sparse Feature Circuits](sparse-feature-circuits.md) (Marks, Rager, Michaud, Belinkov, Bau and Mueller, 2025) | planned | SHIFT ablates SAE features that the authors judged irrelevant: Pythia worst-group accuracy goes from 24.4% to 76.0%. Its test design and matched random-ablation null fit the fix campaign (#4784). Its neuron comparison is not matched, so it is no fair test of native neurons. |
| [Mechanistic Unlearning](mechanistic-unlearning.md) (Guo, Syed, Sheshadri, Ewart and Dziugaite, 2024) | planned | It tests Hase et al.'s premise again. Edits at the fact-lookup MLPs hold in a second format and resist relearning better than edits placed by Causal Tracing or attribution patching. Editing all MLPs is still a strong baseline, and the paper reports no seeds (#4784). |
| [Steering Out-of-Distribution Generalization with Concept Ablation Fine-Tuning](concept-ablation-fine-tuning.md) (Casademunt, Juang, Karvonen, Marks, Rajamanoharan and Nanda, 2025) | planned | CAFT removes interpretable directions during fine-tuning, so the model learns the task without an unwanted generalisation. VERIFIED units could supply those directions, but the repo has only test-time ablation and no training loop (#4784). |
| [Gradient Routing](gradient-routing.md) (Cloud, Goldman-Wetzler, Wybitul, Miller and Turner, 2024) | planned | Masking gradients by data point during training puts a capability in a chosen region, which can then be removed. It cannot apply to the already-trained pythia-70m of #4781. Its retrain attack and its check without the routed tokens are tests that a fix report could use (#4784). |
| [Persona Vectors](persona-vectors.md) (Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans and Lindsey, 2025) | planned | Difference-of-means directions track traits. Steering at inference costs MMLU at large coefficients, and steering during fine-tuning keeps it. Its rule, to report the target, coherence and a capability score for each fix, bears on #4784. |
| [Improving Alignment and Robustness with Circuit Breakers](circuit-breakers.md) (Zou, Phan, Wang, Duenas et al., 2024) | planned | Representation Rerouting trains adapters so that harmful representations turn orthogonal, with a retain loss to keep capability. It fixes by stopping a behaviour, not by giving the right answer, and it picks layers by hand, not from an explanation (#4784, #4781). |
| [The WMDP Benchmark and RMU Unlearning](wmdp-rmu-unlearning.md) (Li et al., 2024) | planned | RMU pushes one layer's activations on forget data toward a random vector and keeps others close to the frozen model. It checks the fix with probes and an attack, and its near-topic test shows a loss that overall MMLU hides. That is a baseline and a set of checks for a #4781 fix (#4784). |
| [Eight Methods to Evaluate Unlearning in LLMs](robust-unlearning-evaluation.md) (Lynch, Guo, Ewart, Casper and Hadfield-Menell, 2024) | planned | An unlearned model loses 77% on its target test. Yet in-context examples, a small relearn and linear probes bring the knowledge back, and related domains suffer. A fix for #4781 needs the same checks beyond its own target (#4784). |
| [Distillation Robustifies Unlearning](distillation-robustifies-unlearning.md) (Lee, Foote, Infanger, Shor et al., 2025) | planned | Unlearning by behaviour training only suppresses a capability, and 500 finetuning steps bring it back. Distilling into a fresh or noised model (UNDO) matches a model that never saw the data. It gives a test of whether a fix removed a capability or only hid it (#4784, #5015). |

## Fixing models: activation steering

Part of the review of how to fix a model's behaviour (#4784). These pages cover fixes at inference time by steering, and how to tell a steering effect from what any push of that size does. They report evidence and do not design the fix campaign. The synthesis of all four areas is [Fixing models](fixing-models.md).

| Page | Status | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Abstention Direction in Large Language Models](abstention-direction.md) (Itai, Hirako and Yamagiwa, 2026) | planned | Ambiguity, knowledge limits and safety each have an abstention direction in Qwen3-4B, partly shared (cosines 0.09 to 0.19). Steering moves abstention across causes. The rates are in figures only, and nothing measures right or wrong answers, so it gives #5220 no fix rate. |
| [Can Mechanistic Interpretability Help Explain LLM Agent Failures?](agent-web-element-hallucination.md) (Aghzal, Geva, Stein and Yao, 2026) | planned | A probe predicts Qwen2.5-7B's web-agent hallucinations (AUC 0.80), and steering along it fixes up to 85.4% while breaking 7% of valid actions. That is the best cell of a sweep, with no random-direction control. Paired patching failed, and no head, neuron or SAE feature explains the decision. |
| [Steering Language Models With Activation Engineering](activation-addition.md) (Turner et al., 2023) | used | The steering samples and the `neuron-steering-tests` skill cite it, but they clamp one unit instead of adding a contrast-pair vector. Its random-vector control of the same norm points to a null that our steering samples lack. |
| [Steering Llama 2 via Contrastive Activation Addition](contrastive-activation-addition.md) (Rimsky/Panickssery et al., 2024) | not applicable | Mean-difference residual vectors from contrast pairs shift Llama 2 Chat behaviours, but the paper has no random-vector null and only 50 test items per behaviour. ## Catastrophic interference and forgetting

Part of the review of interference, weight-space programming and low-rank fine-tuning (#4782). These pages ask what a change to a network's weights does to what it already knows.

| Page | Status | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Why There Are Complementary Learning Systems in the Hippocampus and Neocortex](complementary-learning-systems.md) (McClelland, McNaughton and O'Reilly, 1995) | planned | Slow, interleaved learning finds shared structure, and focused learning of one new fact damages related knowledge where the answers differ. That is the risk for a fix of the #4781 error, and it fits the late changes in the neuron-development paper. |
| [Catastrophic Forgetting, Rehearsal and Pseudorehearsal](rehearsal-and-pseudorehearsal.md) (Robins, 1995) | planned | Rehearsing a few old items with each new one, drawn fresh every epoch, almost removes forgetting in a 32-16-32 network. Rehearsing on random inputs labelled with the network's own outputs keeps much of that benefit. The paper checks only the outputs, so it is no evidence that a VERIFIED pole's explanation survives a change (#4782). |
| [Catastrophic Forgetting in Connectionist Networks](catastrophic-forgetting-connectionist.md) (French, 1999) | planned | The standard 1990s review: shared, overlapping weights cause sudden forgetting, and rehearsal, sparser codes or dual networks reduce it. It frames #4782. Its exact-recognition and savings measures bear on how we count a pole's explanation as lost. |
| [An Empirical Investigation of Catastrophic Forgetting in Gradient-Based Neural Networks](catastrophic-forgetting-empirical.md) (Goodfellow, Mirza, Xiao, Courville and Bengio, 2013) | planned | Across three task pairs, dropout forgets least, and the best activation function depends on the pair. Pythia has no dropout, and our base-instruct analysis already measures unit stability under a fine-tune (81–88% of hypotheses keep). |
| [Overcoming Catastrophic Forgetting in Neural Networks](elastic-weight-consolidation.md) (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) | planned | EWC slows learning on each weight in proportion to its diagonal Fisher, which protects old tasks on permuted MNIST and Atari. | [Continual Learning Through Synaptic Intelligence](synaptic-intelligence.md) (Zenke, Poole and Ganguli, 2017) | planned | Each weight sums gradient × update over training to rate how important it is. The page notes that Pythia-70m's poles keep changing late, so a low importance need not keep an explanation true. |
| [Learning without Forgetting](learning-without-forgetting.md) (Li and Hoiem, 2016) | planned | It keeps old-task skill by distilling the network's own recorded outputs on the new task's inputs: pseudorehearsal with a distillation loss. | [Progressive Neural Networks](progressive-neural-networks.md) (Rusu et al., 2016) | not applicable | Frozen columns with lateral connections can't forget, but only because old units never take input from new ones. | [Gradient Episodic Memory for Continual Learning](gradient-episodic-memory.md) (Lopez-Paz and Ranzato, 2017) | planned | It projects each update so that the loss on a stored memory of past tasks doesn't go up. | [PackNet: Adding Multiple Tasks to a Single Network by Iterative Pruning](packnet.md) (Mallya and Lazebnik, 2018) | planned | It prunes weights by magnitude after each task and keeps the old weights fixed, so old tasks lose nothing, but one pass serves one task. Weight pruning freed 50% of VGG-16, and whole-filter pruning only about 30%. So the spare capacity is spread across units, not held in whole units. |
| [Overcoming Catastrophic Forgetting with Hard Attention to the Task](hard-attention-to-the-task.md) (Serrà, Surís, Miron and Karatzoglou, 2018) | planned | It learns an almost binary mask over units for each task, and blocks the gradient on weights that past tasks use. It is the closest continual-learning method to our neuron grain. But it needs a task label at test time, and a frozen unit's inputs can still change. |
| [Orthogonal Gradient Descent for Continual Learning](orthogonal-gradient-descent.md) (Farajtabar, Azizan, Mott and Li, 2020) | planned | OGD projects each new-task gradient off the stored output gradients, so earlier outputs stay fixed to first order. Holding outputs fixed doesn't hold hidden units fixed, as the neuron-development poles show. Adding a pole's activation gradients to the protected set would hold the pole itself fixed (#4782). |
| [Anatomy of Catastrophic Forgetting: Hidden Representations and Task Semantics](anatomy-of-catastrophic-forgetting.md) (Ramasesh, Dyer and Raghu, 2021) | planned | Layer-level CKA, freezing and reset tests on CIFAR CNNs find that the higher layers cause forgetting. EWC and replay act by keeping them stable. On Qwen2.5-0.5B base against instruct, per-pole change peaks in the middle third, not the last. It bears on #4782 and #5118. |
| [Fine-Tuning can Distort Pretrained Features and Underperform Out-of-Distribution](fine-tuning-distorts-features.md) (Kumar, Raghunathan, Jones, Ma and Liang, 2022) | planned | Full fine-tuning with a random head changes the features of training-like inputs, and it loses to linear probing out of distribution (59.3 against 66.2 average). But it predicts that instruction tuning moves poles most on chat-formatted text. |
| [An Empirical Study of Catastrophic Forgetting in Large Language Models During Continual Fine-tuning](llm-continual-fine-tuning-forgetting.md) (Luo, Yang, Meng et al., 2023) | planned | Sequential narrow instruction tuning of 1–7B models lowers MMLU, reasoning and RACE scores. A general instruction tune, or mixed-in general data, reduces the fall. The size effect rests on a floor, and there is one task order, one run and no unit-level measure. `base-instruct.md` finds that 81–88% of VERIFIED explanations keep after a general instruct tune. |
| [Understanding Catastrophic Forgetting in Language Models via Implicit Inference](implicit-inference-forgetting.md) (Kotha, Springer and Raghunathan, 2024) | not applicable | Fine-tuning moves which task the model infers more than it removes capabilities, and a transformed prompt recovers part of the old behaviour. The repo does no fine-tuning. But the paper predicts that a fix by training hides the #4781 copy rather than removing it. It also predicts that the damage falls first on the nearby right-copy prompts. |
| [LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less](lora-learns-less-forgets-less.md) (Biderman, Portes, Gonzalez Ortiz et al., 2024) | planned | On Llama-2-7B for code and maths, LoRA learns less than full fine-tuning and keeps more of the base model's benchmark scores. | [Loss of Plasticity in Deep Continual Learning](loss-of-plasticity.md) (Dohare, Hernandez-Garcia, Lan, Rahman, Mahmood and Sutton, 2024) | planned | Over many tasks, standard networks gain dead or dormant units, larger weights and a lower effective rank, and they stop learning. | [A Continual Learning Survey: Defying Forgetting in Classification Tasks](continual-learning-survey.md) (De Lange, Aljundi, Masana et al., 2021) | planned | It sorts methods into replay, regularization and parameter isolation, and compares 11 of them with hyperparameters set from the current task only. One run per setting, multi-head image tasks only, and no unit-level measure (#4782). |
| [A Comprehensive Survey of Continual Learning: Theory, Method and Application](comprehensive-continual-learning-survey.md) (Wang, Zhang, Su and Zhu, 2024) | planned | A survey that sorts continual-learning methods into five families (regularisation, replay, optimisation, representation and architecture), with formal scenarios, metrics and bounds. | [Overcoming Sparsity Artifacts in Crosscoders to Interpret Chat-Tuning](crosscoder-chat-tuning.md) (Minder, Dumas, Juang, Chughtai and Nanda, 2025) | planned | With an L1 loss, most of a crosscoder's "chat-only" latents also exist in the base model. | [LoKI: Low-damage Knowledge Implanting of Large Language Models](loki-knowledge-implanting.md) (Wang, Ping, Guo et al., 2025) | planned | It fine-tunes only the W_down rows that an MMLU attribution ranks lowest, spread equally across layers. | [RL's Razor: Why Online Reinforcement Learning Forgets Less](rls-razor.md) (Shenfeld, Pari and Agrawal, ICLR 2026) | planned | Forgetting tracks the base-to-fine-tuned KL on the new task's own inputs, and Fisher-weighted and activation change predict it less well (ParityMNIST). | [LoRA Without Regret](lora-without-regret.md) (Schulman and Thinking Machines Lab, 2025) | planned | LoRA matches full fine-tuning in loss only when it adapts the MLP too, so an adapter that matches it changes the registry's own pole weights. Equal loss does not mean equal units. So #5125's LoRA arm should compare attention-only, MLP-only and all-layer adapters by keep share. |

## Bridges: editing, merging and task vectors

Part of #4782. These pages link weight edits and merged task updates to the units the registry explains.

| Page | Status | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [AlphaEdit: Null-Space Constrained Knowledge Editing for Language Models](alphaedit.md) (Fang, Jiang, Wang et al., 2025) | planned | MEMIT plus one projection: the update is confined to the null space of 100,000 preserved Wikipedia keys. After 2,000 sequential edits on LLaMA3-8B, Counterfact efficacy is 98.90 against MEMIT's 65.65, at the same cost. The keys are the MLP's neuron activations, so the edit writes only through neuron directions that the preserved text leaves silent (our reading). A candidate second baseline for arm F of #5220. |
| [Continual Learning with Hypernetworks](continual-learning-hypernetworks.md) (von Oswald, Henning, Grewe and Sacramento, 2020) | planned | A task-conditioned hypernetwork writes each task's weights from an embedding, and a penalty keeps old tasks' generated weights. Forgetting is near zero on ResNet-32 (82.36 during, 82.34 after) but not on a smaller CIFAR network (74.75 to 71.29). It sidesteps shared-weight interference rather than measuring it, and has no unit content. |
| [Knowledge in Superposition: Unveiling the Failures of Lifelong Knowledge Editing for Large Language Models](knowledge-in-superposition.md) (Hu, Cao, Chen, Liu and Zhao, 2025) | planned | A sequential rank-one edit disturbs each other fact in proportion to the overlap of their whitened MLP keys. Those keys are nearly but not exactly orthogonal, heavy-tailed around zero, in 12 models including GPT-2 Small. The superposition claims are read from figures with no values, and the editing check is 16 cases. The keys are neuron activations, so shared VERIFIED poles could predict which edits interfere (our reading, a question for #5231). |
| [Linking Rapid Learning to Compositional Structure in Neural Networks](rapid-learning-compositional-structure.md) (Tenenbaum and McCoy, 2026) | planned | After MAML, one-step parameter vectors add and subtract like word vectors, with F = 1.00 on 100 ABgeom analogies. Vectors from different starts give about 0. The directions are whole parameter vectors, not units, which supports #5144. |
| [Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT (ROME)](locating-editing-factual-associations.md) (Meng, Bau, Andonian and Belinkov, 2022) | used | The source of causal tracing in the activation-patching-practice skill. Its rank-one edit changes each neuron's output column by a known amount (u_j·Λ, with u = C⁻¹k*). So one edit gives each pole an exact weight change to test against whether its explanation keeps (#5133). |
| [Mass-Editing Memory in a Transformer (MEMIT)](memit.md) (Meng, Sen Sharma, Andonian, Belinkov and Bau, 2023) | planned | A closed-form update over a range of middle MLP layers inserts 10,000 facts at once into GPT-J (CounterFact S 85.8). But neighbourhood specificity falls from 83.5 to 73.7. The fix plan #5220 names it as the arm F baseline. Only poles at or below the lowest edited layer keep their activations by construction. |
| [Model Editing at Scale leads to Gradual and Catastrophic Forgetting](editing-at-scale-forgetting.md) (Gupta, Rao and Anumanchipalli, 2024) | planned | When facts are edited one after another, ROME and MEMIT first forget slowly and then collapse. ROME's collapse comes after 100 to 1,000 edits, and a single "disabling" edit can be about 409 times larger than a typical one. In pythia-70m, poles at or below the edit layer fire as before at any edit count. So the paper's untested drift explanation can be tested on the poles above it (#5362). |
| [Superpose Task-specific Features for Model Merging](superpose-task-features.md) (Qiu, Wu, Li, Guo and Yao, 2025) | planned | Merges each linear layer by keeping every task's singular directions: one linear system per layer, with no data. It leads the best baseline by 0.3 to 1.1 points on ViT and 0.8 on T5-base, on single runs. It is below baselines on several tasks its text says it wins. Its features are singular vectors, mixes of neurons, so the interference report remains the unit test. A candidate merge rule for low-rank patches in #5298. |
| [TIES-Merging: Resolving Interference When Merging Models](ties-merging.md) (Yadav, Tam, Choshen, Raffel and Bansal, 2023) | planned | Trim each task vector to its top 20%, elect one sign per parameter, and average only the agreeing entries. With validation it beats Task Arithmetic on all five settings by 0.7 to 3.6 points, on single runs. Its "interference" is two parameter statistics, and it measures no units. It is the second resolution rule that #5298 would add to the patch format's `additive`. |
| [Task Singular Vectors: Reducing Task Interference in Model Merging](task-singular-vectors.md) (Gargiulo, Crisostomi, Bucarelli, Scardapane, Silvestri and Rodolà, 2025) | planned | Keeps a few singular vectors of each task's layer change and orthogonalises them across tasks, with no tuning. On ViT-B/32 it reaches 85.86 over 8 tasks against 70.79 for task arithmetic, on single runs and CLIP only. Its interference measure, STI, needs only the weights, so it could warn about a patch merge before any run (a question for #5298). It measures no units. |
| [Task Arithmetic in the Tangent Space: Improved Editing of Pre-Trained Models](tangent-space-task-arithmetic.md) (Ortiz-Jiménez, Favero and Frossard, 2023) | planned | Task arithmetic works when a change moves the model only on its own task's inputs (weight disentanglement), not because fine-tuning is linear. Fine-tuning the linearised model raises 8-task addition on ViT-B/32 from 71.4 to 76.5, at two to three times the inference cost. Its disentanglement error is an output-level keep test that a patch merge's evidence could report (#5298). It measures no units. |
| [Editing Models with Task Arithmetic](task-arithmetic.md) (Ilharco, Ribeiro, Wortsman et al., 2023) | planned | A task vector is fine-tuned weights minus pre-trained weights. Negating one cuts GPT-2 Large's toxic generations from 4.8% to 0.8%, with perplexity 16.4 to 16.9. Adding eight reaches 91.2% normalised accuracy on CLIP, against 99.4% for multi-task fine-tuning. Its "minimal interference" is a speculation from near-orthogonal vectors, not a measure, and it looks at no units. It gives #5298 its scaled-sum, negation and analogy rules, and #5404 its negative and extrapolated points. |
| [Model soups: averaging weights of multiple fine-tuned models improves accuracy without increasing inference time](model-soups.md) (Wortsman, Ilharco, Gadre et al., 2022) | planned | The greedy soup of one task's fine-tunes beats the best single model by 0.5–0.7 ImageNet points (ViT-G 90.94 against 90.72). The uniform soup is worse than the best model on all 20 GLUE model-task pairs, and the paper measures no units. The patch format's `additive` merge is a sum (θ₀+Δ₁+Δ₂), not a soup's mean, so the evidence bears on a mean rule for #5298, not on `additive`. |
