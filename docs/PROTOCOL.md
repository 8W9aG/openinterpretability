# Audit protocol (v0.8)

This document defines what one Open Interpretability experiment is, who does what, and how a verdict is decided. The code implements it: the decision rule is `openinterp.agents.reviewer.evaluate_submission`, and its parameters are `openinterp.core.schemas.ReviewPolicy`. Every verdict stores the policy it was computed under, so any record can be re-checked with `openinterp reevaluate`.

## 1. Unit of analysis

One experiment tests **one hypothesis about one unit** in one model revision. A unit is a neuron pole or, since protocol 0.6, an attention head. A latent of a published sparse dictionary (a transcoder or SAE) on an MLP layer can be audited too, as the learned-basis baseline for the native-neuron premise: its experiments run under the same rule but are never counted with native units.

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Neuron | One coordinate of the MLP intermediate activation, i.e. the input to the down/output projection (`act(gate(x)) * up(x)` for gated MLPs). |
| Pole | Positive: `clamp(a, min=0)`. Negative: `clamp(a, max=0)`. The two poles are audited separately. |
| Attention head | One head's output z: its slice of the attention output projection's input (`o_proj`, or `c_proj` in GPT-2). Identified by `HeadIdentifier` (`kind = "attn_head"`, `L<layer>/H<idx>`). A head has no sign, so no poles. |
| Dictionary feature | One latent f of a sparse dictionary trained on a layer's MLP or on the residual stream after it. Identified by `FeatureIdentifier` (`kind = "dictionary_feature"`, `L<layer>/F<idx>` plus the dictionary's name, a preset in `harness/dictionaries.py`). The submission's `dictionary_profile` pins the weights (repository, commit, file) and how they are read. That covers the activation function and the input site: `resid_mid` (the residual stream before the MLP's norm), `mlp_in`, `mlp_in_normalized` (the norm's input standardised without its gain and bias, the TransformerLens `ln2.hook_normalized` that most public transcoders were trained on), `mlp_out` for an MLP-output SAE, `resid_post` for a residual-stream SAE, or `mlp_hidden` (the down projection's input, where native neurons live) for a `basis`. A basis is the null-calibration control: each latent is the positive part of one direction in the MLP's hidden space, and with the identity basis the latents are exactly the native positive poles (`docs/analyses/null-calibration.md`). It also covers the output site: the MLP's output (after Gemma 2's post-MLP norm), or for a residual-stream SAE the residual stream it reads. Latents are non-negative, so no poles. |
| Strength | Non-negative, per token. Larger always means more active. For a neuron pole, the magnitude of the pole. For a dictionary feature, the latent's activation `a_f = act(W_enc[:, f] · x + b_enc[f])`. For a head, `\|W_O (z_t − z̄)\|`: the distance between the head's write to the residual stream at token t and its mean write over the scan, i.e. how far mean-ablating the head would move the residual stream there. z̄ is measured by the scan and stored in `head_profile.mean_output`, so every later measurement uses the same zero point and a record reproduces without its corpus. |
| Scale | The maximum strength seen during the discovery scan (`max_activation`), over the scan's corpus sample (§3, step 2). |
| Active | A prompt is active if its peak strength over its measured tokens (every token, or every token but the first for a unit with a first-token floor) is at least `activation_threshold × scale` (default 0.25). |
| First-token floor | Since protocol 0.8, a unit active at a text's first position on at least `max_active_share` (default 0.5) of the vocabulary (special tokens left out), against the line set by its scale without first tokens. Attention there sees only the token itself, so this is a property of the weights. A text's first token has no context and is the attention sink, and such a unit sits above its line there whatever the token, so every probe would fire. For it, the first token is never measured: the scan leaves first tokens out of its scale, histogram and top-example ranking, and every peak below skips it. A one-token probe or counterfactual is then recorded as unmeasured (`measured = false`) and counts in no metric. The scan records the judgement in `first_token_floor`; a review of a record without one judges it against the record's scale. Every other unit is measured at every token, so a unit that detects *which* token opens a text can still be audited. |
| Default-on | A unit active on at least `max_active_share` (default 0.5) of its scan's tokens, read from the scan's histogram. At that density a probe of a few words almost always has one active token, so no hard negative can stay inactive and the unit can't be audited as a detector. The scan stops on such a unit, as it does on one that never fires, and a review fails the `not_default_on` gate. In GPT-2 small most negative poles are default-on, because GELU's dip holds them near its minimum on most tokens (`docs/analyses/position-zero.md`). |
| Δ (counterfactual) | `(peak strength on base − peak strength on counterfactual) / scale`. |

## 2. Roles

| Role | Does | Must not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Proposer** | Selects the neuron, scans a corpus, writes the hypothesis, the mechanism claim and the minimal-pair counterfactuals. | Edit a submission after review. A revision is a new submission. |
| **Reviewer** | Writes adversarial probes from the hypothesis text and the top examples. Should be a different model from the proposer (see below). | See probe activations before the probe set is final. |
| **Harness** | Runs every measurement against the model weights and applies the decision rule. | Use LLM judgement anywhere in the verdict. |

Agents only write text: hypotheses and probes. Every number in a verdict comes from running the model.

**Reviewer independence is encouraged, not enforced.** A model probing its own hypothesis tends to share its blind spots, so a reviewer from a different model is stronger evidence. Since protocol 0.4 a same-model review is allowed: the record stores `independent_review = false` and the verdict is computed as usual (`require_independent_review` turns it into a gate, §4). In return, self-reviews are made visible and followed up:
- `verify` (alias `review`) warns when the reviewer model is the proposer's.
- Experiment, neuron, layer and home pages badge the verdict *self-reviewed*, wiki pages mark it, and `registry.parquet` has a `self_reviewed` column. The home page reports how many experiments and current verified claims are self-reviewed.
- `openinterp next` proposes an independent re-review of any unit whose latest experiment was self-reviewed, and a `replicate` of a VERIFIED unit that no other model has reviewed.

"Same model" means the same recorded model ID, so record the IDs the agents report.

## 3. Lifecycle

```
select ─▶ scan ─▶ hypothesize ─▶ submit (frozen) ─▶ probe ─▶ measure ─▶ decide ─▶ record ─▶ publish
                       ▲                                                    │
                       └──────────── NEEDS_REVISION: new submission ◀───────┘
```

1. **Select.** Choose the unit and record how in `provenance.selection`. Agents without an assignment use `openinterp next` (skill `choose-next-experiment`). It explores uniformly random neurons with probability ε, in a model drawn from `[tool.openinterp] models` in `pyproject.toml` with weight 1/√(1 + units studied or claimed), and otherwise samples a follow-up on an existing neuron; re-studying a neuron is valid, and null results count as data. The options are:
   - `random`: uniform over layers, then neurons, then poles (`openinterp audit --random --seed N`), or over layers, then heads (`--unit head --random`), or over a dictionary's layers, then its latents (`--dictionary <name> --random`). Only randomly selected units count towards registry-wide statistics such as "fraction of neurons with a verified explanation", and neurons, heads and dictionary features are never pooled. The baseline is the share VERIFIED among random latents against the share among random native neuron poles on the same model and layers. Each unit counts once, by its first experiment, so neither side gains from follow-ups. In a GELU MLP (GPT-2) the native arm is positive poles (`--random --pole positive`, uniform over layers, then neurons). The negative pole is GELU's dip, and it sits in the dip on most tokens, so a probe's peak nearly always clears the activity line and no hard negative can pass. On the GPT-2 pilot, none of 6 random negative poles was verified (`docs/analyses/transcoder-baseline-pilot.md`). Since protocol 0.8 such poles are default-on (§1) and are not audited; 87% of GPT-2 small's negative poles are. The test reported first is latents against positive poles. A random draw of a latent that never fires on the scan can't be audited. The scan of every live latent therefore records the share of its dictionary's latents that are dead on the same tokens (`dictionary_profile.dead_share`), and the comparison reports it next to the shares VERIFIED. A random draw the scan stops on, dead or default-on, is kept as an outcome of random selection: `audit --random` writes it write-once to `wiki/draws/<slug>/<timestamp>.json` (`UnauditableDraw`, with its provenance), the build exports `data/draws.parquet`, and the comparison counts each arm's unauditable draws.
   - `attribution`: chosen because it ranked highly in attribution patching for some behaviour.
   - `manual`: anything else.
2. **Scan.** Run a sample of the corpus through the model and keep the top-k activating snippets and a histogram of all token strengths.
   - *Sample.* Since protocol 0.7 a scan reads a fixed-size, seeded sample of whole documents, not the whole corpus, so its cost and its scale don't grow with the corpus. Documents are visited in `random.Random(seed)` order, and each is taken if it fits in what is left of the token budget (the first always is). Tokens are counted with the model's tokenizer, without special tokens. The defaults are a budget of 200,000 tokens and seed 0 (`--max-tokens`, `--sample-seed`), so every audit of one corpus with one tokenizer reads the same documents. `provenance.corpus_sample` records the seed, budget, document and token counts and the SHA-256 of the sampled documents as JSONL. `--max-tokens 0` scans the whole corpus and records no sample, as every record before 0.7 did.
   - *Reading.* Documents are read whole, or as overlapping windows (`--window-tokens`, `--stride`) in which each token is counted once and top examples don't overlap. For a unit with a first-token floor (§1), first tokens are left out of the scale and histogram, and top examples are ranked by their peak after them. Record the model revision, dtype, corpus, corpus hash, sample, token count and window length and stride in `provenance`. A head scan first averages the head's output over every counted token (z̄), then measures strengths against it. It also records, for each top example, the tokens its peak attends to most, and the head's previous-token, duplicate-token and induction scores: mean attention weights on 16 sequences of 32 uniformly random tokens repeated twice (Olsson et al., 2022). These are evidence for the proposer, not gates. Heads have no vocabulary lens (a head writes a subspace, not one direction) and no partner search. A dictionary-feature scan reads the dictionary's input in the same pass and also measures how well the whole dictionary fits there: the fraction of its output site's variance it reconstructs (leaving out each piece's first token, an attention sink the dictionaries weren't trained on), its mean L0 and the latent's density (`dictionary_profile`; reported, not gated, but a dictionary that fits badly is a weak baseline). A feature's vocabulary lens reads its decoder row; it has no partner search or input attribution.
3. **Hypothesize.** The proposer states:
   - a natural-language explanation precise enough that a third party can label new text as "predicted active" or "predicted inactive";
   - the mechanism: **retrospective** (reacts to tokens already read) or **lookahead** (prepares specific upcoming tokens);
   - at least three minimal-pair counterfactuals with predicted Δ, including at least one control pair (predicted Δ ≈ 0).
4. **Submit.** The submission is frozen from here on. Changing anything other than provenance metadata produces a new submission.
5. **Probe.** The reviewer writes 10–20 probes (of at least two tokens each, for a unit with a first-token floor) and labels each with what the *hypothesis* predicts, not with what the reviewer expects the neuron to do:
   - **hard negatives** (about two thirds): near-misses that share surface features with the activating examples but which the hypothesis says should not fire;
   - **positive holdouts** (at least four): new contexts, including paraphrases of the claimed feature, that the hypothesis says should fire.
6. **Measure.** The harness runs every probe and counterfactual, measures the lookahead profile (KL between clean and ablated next-token distributions, and how concentrated the change is), and computes the causal metrics. A pole is ablated by zeroing it; a head by replacing its output with z̄ (mean ablation), the change its strength measures; a dictionary feature by subtracting its write `a_f · W_dec[f]` from its output site, which leaves the dictionary's reconstruction error and every other latent in place.
7. **Decide.** Apply the rule in §4.
8. **Record.** Every completed experiment, whatever its verdict, gets a write-once page in `wiki/experiments/<model>/L<layer>/N<idx>-<pole>/<timestamp>.md` (a head: `…/L<layer>/H<idx>/`; a dictionary feature: `…/L<layer>/<dictionary>/F<idx>/`). The page holds the hypothesis, evidence, gate results, failed probes and next steps derived from the failed gates. `verify` and `reevaluate` write it automatically, next to the experiment's write-once record `<timestamp>.json`. Re-evaluations, revisions and re-reviews are new experiments: nothing is ever overwritten. A revision names the experiment it replaces in its submission's `supersedes` field (`audit --supersedes <record>`), so the history reads as a chain. The page's Discussion section is for free-form notes.
9. **Publish.** Every experiment is published, whatever its verdict. The site and `registry.parquet` are built from the archived `<id>.json` records, the registry's only source of truth. A neuron pole or head may have any number of experiments, and its standing (see *Standing* below) is derived from them, never stored. Pages follow Model → Layer → Neuron (`N<idx>.html`), head (`H<idx>.html`) or dictionary feature (`<dictionary>/F<idx>.html`) → Experiments, and each unit's experiments are ordered by **conclusiveness**:
   - With Beta(1, 1) priors on specificity and sensitivity, P(useful) = P(balanced accuracy > 0.6).
   - Conclusiveness is |2·P(useful) − 1|, so a well-probed rejection ranks as high as a well-probed verification, and thinly-probed results rank low whatever their verdict.

   **Standing.** A unit's status is not simply its newest experiment's, or a weaker or self-reviewed rerun could overturn an independent VERIFIED. It is derived in four steps (`builder.standing.pole_standing`):
   1. *Newest hypothesis.* Only experiments on the unit's newest submission count. A new submission replaces older ones (a revision names them in `supersedes`), and their verdicts were about a different claim. Re-reviews of one submission share everything but provenance.
   2. *One reading per probe set.* A re-measurement of a probe set (e.g. `reevaluate` under a newer protocol) replaces that set's earlier reading.
   3. *Independent over self-review.* If any reading came from a reviewer model other than the proposer's, self-reviews are outweighed.
   4. *Agreement.* If the readings left agree, that is the status. If one is VERIFIED and another is not, the status is **CONTESTED**, and the unit is not counted as verified. If none is VERIFIED, the newest reading's status stands: NEEDS_REVISION against REJECTED is not a disagreement about whether the claim holds.

   Unit pages show which reviews the status rests on, and mark every other experiment as re-measured, outweighed or an earlier hypothesis. The home page lists contested units and reports **reviewer agreement**: of the hypotheses reviewed with more than one probe set (self-reviews included, each set read by its newest experiment), how many got the same verdict, VERIFIED or not, from every review, and the agreement rate over pairs of reviews. `registry.parquet` has `unit_status` and `counts_toward_status`.

## 4. Decision rule

Probes are not independent: one reviewer writes them all, often as variations on a theme. Near-duplicate probes (word-set Jaccard ≥ `probe_cluster_threshold`, default 0.5) are therefore clustered, and each cluster counts as **one observation**, scored by its mean pass rate. Every count and rate below uses these effective counts.

Probe accuracy is reported as **balanced accuracy**, the mean of:
- **specificity**: the cluster-weighted fraction of hard negatives that stayed inactive;
- **sensitivity**: the cluster-weighted fraction of positive holdouts that fired.

Balanced accuracy is the `confidence_score`. Using it means many easy negatives cannot hide failed generalisations.

**Confound control.** The scan records a confound profile of the pole: the variance explained by token identity and by position, the correlation with token frequency, and the tokens and positions with the highest mean strength. On the reviewer's probes, the hypothesis is compared with three trivial baselines, each scored by the same cluster-weighted balanced accuracy:
- *token identity*: active if any measured token is a trigger token;
- *position*: active at a high-activation measured position;
- *never active*.

A hypothesis that can't at least match the best of these adds nothing beyond "fires on token X".

A verdict is **VERIFIED** only if every gate passes:

<!-- Defaults quote ReviewPolicy in core/schemas.py; tests/test_single_source.py checks them. -->

| Gate | Default | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `enough_hard_negatives` | ≥ 6 clusters | Specificity is meaningless on a handful of independent probes. |
| `enough_holdouts` | ≥ 3 clusters | The same applies to sensitivity. |
| `specificity` | ≥ 0.80 | The hypothesis is not too broad. |
| `sensitivity` | ≥ 0.60 | The hypothesis generalises beyond its own examples. |
| `counterfactual_reproduction` | ≥ 0.75 of pairs | The proposer's predicted Δ reproduce within `max(0.10, 0.50 × |predicted|)`, with the right sign. |
| `lookahead_consistent` | — | The measured mechanism matches the claim. |
| `independent_review` | off (`require_independent_review`) | The reviewer model differs from the proposer model. Always recorded; gated only when the policy requires it. |
| `beats_confound_baseline` | ≥ best baseline | The hypothesis explains the probes at least as well as token identity, position or "never active". It fails when the submission has no confound profile. |
| `not_default_on` | active share < 0.50 | The unit is not default-on (§1), so a hard negative can stay inactive at all. It passes when the submission has no histogram. |

If the probe set is adequate (both count gates pass) and balanced accuracy is below **0.60**, the verdict is **REJECTED**: the hypothesis predicts the neuron barely better than chance. Every other outcome is **NEEDS_REVISION**, and the failed gates are listed in `verdict.notes`.

### What is not evidence (yet)

- **The proposer's counterfactuals.** The proposer can measure these before submitting, so a match is a reproduction check (it catches measurement or transcription errors) and not a successful prediction. The predictive test is the reviewer's probes, which are written without seeing any activations.
- **Covariance with other neurons.** Pole strengths are sparse and heavy-tailed, so a global correlation mostly measures agreement on "nothing is happening". The scan therefore finds partner neuron *poles* by co-activation lift: P(partner active | target active) ÷ P(partner active).
  - *Target active* uses the protocol threshold; *partner active* means above the partner pole's own 99th percentile.
  - Partners are ranked by the 95% Wilson lower bound of the lift, so a few chance coincidences don't count.
  - Each partner also records Spearman's ρ of the two pole strengths (on a fixed-seed reservoir sample, ties averaged) and, for reference, Pearson's r with the signed activation.
  - The reviewer ablates the pole with up to 3 partners (lift lower bound ≥ 2 on ≥ 5 co-active tokens, or |ρ| ≥ 0.2). It reports the KL for the pole alone, the partners alone and all together.
  - The *redundancy ratio*, (KL together − KL partners) / KL alone, exceeds 1 when partners mask the pole's effect, and is near 0 when the pole's effect overlaps with theirs.

  This is reported, not gated.
- **Vocabulary lens.** Each submission records the pole's output direction read through the logit lens and the **Jacobian lens** (Gurnee et al., 2026). The Jacobian lens transports the direction with the context-averaged Jacobian E[∂h_final,t′/∂h_ℓ,t] over t′ ≥ t, estimated from one perturbed forward pass per prompt and sign. The record includes:
  - both top-10 lists, with each token's z-score over the whole vocabulary;
  - their overlap and cos(w, J·w);
  - the kurtosis of the vocabulary scores (≈ 3 means no specific token set).

  On Qwen2.5-0.5B the lenses disagree completely up to the middle layers and converge near the output, so mechanism claims should rest on the Jacobian lens. It is reported, not gated.
- **Patchscope.** Each submission on a neuron pole or dictionary feature records the model's own description of the unit's output direction (a pole's signed `w_out`, or a latent's decoder row). It uses a Patchscope (Ghandeharioun et al., 2024) in the spirit of SelfIE (Chen et al., 2024). The direction replaces the residual stream after a layer's block at the placeholder `?` of an inspection prompt, scaled to 1, 2 and 4 times the norm of the residual it replaces, and the model continues greedily for up to 12 tokens.
  - *Prompts.* `category` is a few-shot list (`banana: fruit`, `hammer: tool`, `violin: music`, `senator: politics`, then `?:`). `meaning` is `The meaning of the word "?" is`.
  - *Layers.* The unit's own layer, and layer 2 when that is earlier.
  - *Control.* The same with a fixed random direction (seed 0). Text the two share comes from the prompt, not the unit.

  The readout is taken after the proposer has answered, and no prompt shows it, so it is a blind, example-free description that can later serve as a proposer baseline. A proposer revising an earlier experiment can still read it on that experiment's page. On Qwen2.5-0.5B, `category` at layer 2 named the concept of several VERIFIED poles: *crop* for L14/N2611+, *newspaper* for L5/N4579−, *official* for L3/N3738− and *culture* for L5/N2131−, where the control gave *animal*, *emotion* or *period*. Injected at their own layer, poles from layer 17 up mostly read back the control's text. It costs about 5–8 s per unit on CPU and is reported, not gated. Heads write a subspace, not one direction, and are not read.

  *As a proposer baseline* (`openinterp patchscope-baseline`, results in `docs/analyses/patchscope-baseline.md`), each unit's readout becomes one explanation by a fixed rule: the `category` reading at the lowest layer read, at ×2, as `Fires on tokens related to "<reading>".` It is scored on the held-out windows the agent's explanation was scored on, which records store with their labels, so the two are paired and nothing is re-measured. Detection and fuzzing pair only experiments judged by the same model. On the 44 held-out Qwen2.5-0.5B experiments judged by Claude Haiku 5.5, the baseline scored at chance (detection 0.51, fuzzing 0.52) against the agent's 0.60 and 0.62. It matched or beat the agent on 20% of experiments for detection (sign test p = 1e-8) and 30% for fuzzing (p = 2e-4). Embedding AUROC was 0.53 against 0.55 over 89 experiments (p = 0.24). The readings are mostly generic: 38 distinct over 99 experiments, led by *country*, *noun* and *animal*. Judges read grammatical readings like *noun* inconsistently, as the word or as the part of speech.
- **Probe comparison** (`openinterp probe-compare`, results in `docs/analyses/probe-comparison.md`). For a VERIFIED neuron pole whose concept a rule over characters can mark (a regular expression, or human annotations written as spans by `openinterp concepts`; never the model), linear probes are trained on labelled tokens of one corpus and tested on another. They are logistic and mass-mean probes (Marks & Tegmark, 2023) on the layer's MLP hidden activations and on the residual stream after the layer, and k-sparse logistic probes over the hidden layer's neurons (Gurnee et al., 2023). The report gives the pole's own held-out accuracy (at a fitted threshold and at the protocol's line), the cosine between each probe's direction and the pole's (its signed basis vector, or its signed `w_out` on the residual), the neuron's rank by class separation, and the smallest k that comes within 0.02 of the dense probe. It shows whether the concept is localised in the neuron or only partly carried by it. Each text window's first token is left out. Records are write-once, so it is not stored in them; it is reported, not gated.
- **Steering samples.** Each submission on a neuron pole or dictionary feature records five neutral prompts (`openinterp/harness/steering.py`) continued greedily for up to 24 tokens under each of five interventions, applied at every position:
  - *baseline*: none;
  - *ablated*: the unit's own ablation (the pole zeroed, or the latent's write removed);
  - *clamped ×1, ×2, ×4*: a neuron's activation set to the pole's signed top activation in the scan (`max_activation`) times the multiple, or a latent's activation set to that value.

  Coherent drift at ×1–×2 says what the unit makes the model write (Turner et al., 2023; Goodfire, 2026); drift only at ×4 is off-distribution. No effect is common and does not refute a detection claim. The samples are taken after the proposer has answered and no prompt shows them. A steering prediction needs a pre-registered format before it can be gated. It is reported, not gated, and heads, which have no single "high" setting, are not steered.
- **Wiring.** Each unit records the units it connects to most directly along the residual stream (`openinterp/harness/wiring.py`, `wiring_activations.py`), the 10 strongest of each kind, read two ways. A neuron pole reads its MLP input (`gate` and `up` for a gated MLP, else `in`) and writes `w_out`, negated for the negative pole. A dictionary feature reads its site with its encoder row and writes its decoder row. A head reads through Q, K and V and writes the subspace `W_O`, so its own layer's MLP is downstream of it.
  - *weights* (`NeuronAuditSubmission.wiring`): virtual weights (Elhage et al., 2021). Upstream are earlier neurons' `w_out` and earlier heads' OV circuits, against the unit's read. Downstream are later neurons' reads, and later heads' Q, K and V, of the unit's write. Each number is signed when one vector reads another, and a norm otherwise. Each norm before a read is folded in as its gain, plus centring for LayerNorm. Its per-token scale is dropped, so weights rank units but have no absolute scale. Grouped-query heads share their key and value reads. It takes under a second and is read after the proposer has answered.
  - *activations* (`VerificationVerdict.wiring`): attribution patching (Syed et al., 2023) at the unit's peak on each of the review's should-fire prompts (the counterfactuals' base prompts and the holdouts) that reaches the activation threshold, averaged.
    - Upstream is `Σ_s write_s · ∂strength/∂x_s`, the first-order change in strength from removing one unit's direct write at every position.
    - A downstream neuron gets the exact change in its activation at the peak when the unit's write is removed from its input alone.
    - A downstream head gets `|W_O Δz|` at the peak when the unit's write is removed from its Q, K or V input alone, one channel at a time, everything else held fixed.
    - It takes about 8 s for a Qwen2.5-0.5B neuron on CPU, most of it on downstream heads.

  Both readings are direct effects only, not paths through other units, and no prompt shows them. Archived records are write-once, so `openinterp wiring-backfill` takes both readings for records that predate wiring, the same way, at each record's model revision and dtype. It writes them to `docs/analyses/wiring.jsonl`, which `openinterp build` shows, marked as backfilled. Site pages link each edge to the unit's page when the registry has one. Each model's *circuits* page joins units that both have pages: edges are followed from unit to unit into connected groups, laid out by layer. A *hub*, a unit wired to at least 5% of the model's units with pages (and at least 10), would join most of them into one star, so its edges don't join circuits; the page lists hubs and the units wired to each separately. A unit's wiring lists only its top connections, so a missing edge is not evidence of no connection. Reported, not gated.
- **Dictionary latents.** `openinterp crossref` lists, for each neuron pole whose model has a dictionary preset at its layer (`openinterp/harness/dictionaries.py`), the 5 latents whose decoder row best aligns with the pole's write and, for a dictionary reading the MLP's input, the 5 whose encoder best aligns with the neuron's read (`openinterp/harness/crossref.py`). Cosines are taken in residual-stream coordinates with norms folded in as for wiring, and oriented: a latent fires non-negatively, so it matches a pole only along the pole's own write (`±w_out`), and a read along `in` or `up` with the pole's sign, along `gate` positively. Each side records *chance*, the 99th percentile of the best cosine a random direction reaches, and the share of the layer's neurons whose best match is weaker. Weights only, so it holds for every experiment on the pole: rows go to `docs/analyses/dictionary-alignment.jsonl`, keyed by pole and preset, with the dictionary's file pinned. They name each latent by index, never copy its description. Neuron pages list them, linked to the latent's page here (if it was audited) or on Neuronpedia (where the preset names a Neuronpedia source checked to index the same latents). `openinterp aligned-latents <slug>` prints them with descriptions, and `openinterp review --dictionary-latents` shows the reviewer the above-chance ones that have a description, to probe where they disagree with the hypothesis. That flag is off by default, so a reviewer's prompt doesn't depend on a third-party service. Reported, not gated.
- **Sister neurons.** `openinterp sisters` matches each VERIFIED neuron pole with the poles of another model (`openinterp/harness/sisters.py`): by default every ordered pair of the configured models (`[tool.openinterp] models`), and each model with its larger siblings (`SIBLINGS`, Qwen2.5-0.5B → Qwen2.5-1.5B). Both models read the corpus (`corpus.jsonl`) a whole document per forward pass, cut at the model's context. They are compared at *shared boundaries*: character offsets where a token ends in both tokenizations (the last token, where several end at one character). Both models are causal, so at a shared boundary each has read exactly the same text, whatever its tokens. Models with one tokenizer share every boundary; each row records its *coverage*, the share of the source model's tokens that paired. Each text's first token is left out, as many units take extreme values there. Each pole's strength is correlated (Pearson, over all paired positions) with every pole of every layer of the other model, and its 5 best matches are kept. A second pass correlates each match with every pole of the first model: a match whose own best partner is the pole is a *mutual* best match (Gurnee et al., 2024). *Rotated* is the best correlation the pole reaches with any unit of a Haar-random rotation of its top match's layer, seeded by model and layer: as many candidates, built from the same activations, but not the other model's own neurons. A mutual best match above it is a *sister*. Rows go to `docs/analyses/sister-neurons.jsonl`, one per pole and other model, with both models' revisions and the corpus's SHA-256. Neuron pages list each pole's matches, and a matched pole's page lists the poles that matched it, linked where the registry has a page. A sister is a candidate for the same feature, not a test that the explanation carries over. `openinterp audit` and `openinterp review` show their agents a pole's linked poles that this registry has studied, with each one's standing and explanation: its sisters either way, and any other match with r of at least 0.5 (`MIN_PROMPT_PEARSON`; weaker links mostly join unrelated features). The proposer is told to treat them as leads; the reviewer, to probe where an explanation differs from the hypothesis. `--no-sisters` leaves them out.
  - *Sister study.* Whether a sister represents the same concept is an experiment: `openinterp audit --sister-of <VERIFIED record>` audits the sister with that record's explanation as its hypothesis, recorded word for word whatever the proposer drafts (`sister_of` names the experiment). The proposer picks the activating tokens, mechanism and counterfactuals the explanation predicts for the sister, and the review is the usual one. VERIFIED says the explanation carries over; any other verdict says where it doesn't. `openinterp next` offers a `sister` draw for each VERIFIED pole's unstudied sister in a configured model only, so CI can reproduce the record. Neuron pages give each tested pair's verdict, and the study's experiment links to the record it tested. Results: `docs/analyses/sister-neurons.md`. Reported, not gated.
- **The other pole.** The scan of a neuron pole reads the whole signed activation, so it also records the neuron's other pole at no extra cost: its 5 strongest non-overlapping examples and its histogram over the same tokens (`opposite_pole`; left out if that pole never fires). The neuron page shows both poles' top examples side by side, since one pole often encodes the contrast the other draws. Neither prompt shows them. Reported, not gated.
- **Label confounds.** A scan (of a neuron pole, a head or a dictionary latent) can label every counted token from a label set, and records what each set explains of the unit's activation (`ConfoundProfile.labels`; `openinterp/harness/annotations.py`). A tagger labels character spans of a document, and the scan aligns them to the model's tokens once per piece through the tokenizer's character offsets. Special tokens, characters no span covers and windows whose text can't be found in their document stay unlabelled and are left out.
  - *R².* Variance explained by a per-label mean, as `token_r2` is for token identity.
  - *Mutual information.* I(strength; label) in bits. Strengths are sparse and heavy-tailed, so the estimate is binned: zero is a bin of its own and positive strengths fall in 8 equal-count bins. Each entropy has the Miller–Madow correction, but small scans (a few hundred tokens) still read a little high.
  - *Per label.* The 50 most frequent labels, each with its token count, mean strength and number of tokens at or above the protocol's activation line.
  - *Top examples.* Each top example records its peak token's label in each set (`ActivatingExample.peak_labels`; a document label is its document's).

  The only label set so far is `language`, from a corpus's `lang` field. `openinterp corpus --dataset wikipedia-multilingual` writes it, taken exactly from each document's Wikipedia subset. For other datasets, `openinterp corpus --detect-lang` detects it per document with fastText's `lid.176`, and labels a document only at confidence ≥ 0.8, so most code and markup stay unlabelled (`openinterp/langid.py`; the choice is in `docs/analyses/language-detection.md`). The corpus's source sidecar records the detector. A corpus without the field records no labels, which includes the registry's scan corpus. The proposer sees each label set's R², mutual information and active tokens per label, and each top example's language. The reviewer sees each example's language, and is asked for positive holdouts translated into other languages when a hypothesis claims a feature whatever the language, and for hard negatives in another language of the same script when it names one. The site lists each corpus with its language mix: per model, its largest scan's labelled tokens by language. Reported, not gated.
- **Input attribution.** For a neuron pole, the scan attributes the signed activation at the peak of each of the first 5 top examples to every token up to the peak. It uses integrated gradients over the input token embeddings (Sundararajan et al., 2017). The baseline replaces every token but the first with the pad token. The first token stays because it is the attention sink: replacing it sends the path off-distribution and the integral does not converge. Each attribution is a share of the gap between the example and the baseline, so the shares sum to 1 when the integral has converged.
  - *Convergence.* The steps double from 16 up to 64 until the shares sum to within 0.1 of 1. Only converged examples count towards the summary numbers.
  - *Distance.* The share of |attribution| on the peak token, the token before, and tokens 2–4, 5–16, 17–64 and 65+ back, with the weighted mean distance. This separates "fires on the current token" from "fires because of earlier context" (compare `token_r2`).
  - *Ablation check.* The peak, the 4 tokens before it and the 5 most attributed tokens are each replaced alone with the pad token and re-measured. The record gives Spearman's ρ between attribution and this effect.

  The proposer and the reviewer see the most attributed tokens of each example, with both numbers, so probes can vary the context that matters. On Qwen2.5-0.5B, L4/N1889+ converges on all 5 examples at 16 steps: 47% of its attribution is on the peak token and ρ = 0.76. In layers 11–20 the integral converges on 1 to 3 of 5 examples within 64 steps. There, most attribution often sits more than 16 tokens back, spread so thinly that replacing any one far token changes almost nothing. Replacing one of the few tokens just before the peak often removes it completely. It costs 6 s (layer 4) to about 2 min (layer 20) per neuron on CPU. It is reported, not gated, and heads are not attributed yet.
- **Causal metrics.** Sufficiency (clean → corrupted patch) and necessity (corrupted → clean patch) are measured on each minimal pair, with downstream MLP outputs frozen against self-repair. They are recorded, not gated.
  - *Where.* The pole is patched at every position and scored at the position where its strength differs most between the two prompts. When the prompts differ in token length, only the final position can be patched and scored.
  - *What.* The target favours no particular neuron. A lookahead claim is scored on its claimed upcoming tokens: their mean logit minus the vocabulary mean (`claimed_tokens`). Anything else is scored on the KL between the unpatched and patched next-token distributions (`next_token_kl`).
  - *Compared with what.* The same patch is applied, one neuron at a time, to the 32 same-layer neurons (`causal_null_neurons`) whose strength changes most similarly between the pair's prompts. A head is patched by swapping its output z and ranked against the other heads of its layer (at most `causal_null_neurons`); the change matched is each head's change in write, |W_O,h (z_base − z_cf)|, which needs no head's z̄. With a dozen or so heads per layer the percentile is coarse. A dictionary feature is patched by adding `(a_f,source − a_f,target) · W_dec[f]` to its output site and ranked against the 32 latents of the same dictionary whose write changes, |Δa| · ‖W_dec[f]‖, are most similar. Each score is the median over pairs of the pole's |effect| percentile among theirs. 0.5 means "no more influential than a neuron that changes as much"; 1.0 means it beats all of them. The raw median effects are recorded too, and so is each pair's effect and percentile in both directions, with the matched units' median and largest |effect| (`causal_metrics.pairs`).
  - *Which pairs.* Control pairs, where the pole moves by at most `delta_abs_tolerance × scale`, carry no causal signal and are skipped. With none left, no causal metrics are recorded.

  Two simpler scores read wrongly on Qwen2.5-0.5B. The fraction of the whole clean/corrupted gap restored (protocol ≤ 0.4, on the top-1 logit) reads ≈ 0 for any single neuron: the gap sums every component's contribution, and the rest of the model often moves the other way. A percentile against *random* neurons reads ≈ 1 for every neuron, because minimal pairs are built to change the audited neuron and most others barely change.

  The `next_token_kl` target shows that a neuron matters, not that it matters *for the reason the hypothesis gives*. A causal test of a retrospective claim would need the hypothesis to name the behaviour it affects. Gating on these scores needs a pre-registered prediction format first (see TODO).
- **Explanation scores on held-out text** (Delphi; Paulo et al., 2025), from `openinterp score`. Every review records them: `review` takes them with `--explanation-scores`, or measures them itself in one-step mode, and refuses to post without them. `reevaluate` makes no LLM calls, so it attaches them only when given. They test the explanation on text nobody chose, where probes test it on text the reviewer chose.
  - *Windows.* The corpus snippets that are not the scan's top examples, so the proposer never saw them, are cut into 32-token windows. They come from the whole corpus, not just the scan's sample. Each window is measured on its own, exactly as the judge reads it, the way probes are. Active windows (peak ≥ the activation threshold) are split into 5 bins by peak strength. Each bin gives 5 windows to detection and 5 to fuzzing, so weak activations count as much as the top ones. Detection also gets as many inactive windows.
  - *Detection.* A judge given only the explanation lists the windows it says activate the pole. Score: balanced accuracy, with recall per activation bin.
  - *Fuzzing.* In each window some tokens are marked: the active tokens, the same number of inactive tokens, or random tokens in an inactive window. The judge lists the windows whose marks are where the explanation says the pole fires. Score: balanced accuracy.
  - *Embedding.* The AUROC of the cosine between the explanation and each detection window, from a local sentence encoder (`sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` by default). It needs no LLM.

  When no held-out window is active, detection gets inactive windows only and scores false positives; fuzzing is skipped. The task a judge answers holds no activations: labels are measured when the answers are scored, and `openinterp reproduce` re-measures them and re-derives the scores from the stored answers (warnings only). 0.5 is chance for all three. A narrow explanation that passes review can still score low here, if most of the pole's held-out activity falls outside it.

## 5. Reproducibility requirements

A record must let a third party re-derive its verdict:

- `identifier`: model name, layer, and neuron and pole, or head.
- `head_profile.mean_output` for a head: the zero point of its strength.
- `dictionary_profile` for a dictionary feature: the weights' repository, commit and file, and the input site and activation they are read with.
- `provenance`: model revision (commit hash), dtype, corpus and its SHA-256, the corpus sample (seed, token budget, SHA-256), snippet and token counts, selection method and seed, harness version.
- The full probe set with labels and rationales, stored as `adversarial_test_results`.
- `verdict.policy`: the exact thresholds, including `protocol_version`.
- `verdict.environment` (and `provenance.environment` for the scan): the device, weights dtype, model commit and the openinterp, torch and transformers versions the measurements ran with.

`provenance.costs` also records what the experiment cost to run: wall time per stage (model load, scan, lenses, proposer, held-out windows, judge, reviewer, probe measurement, gates, causal metrics) and each LLM call's model, prompt size, tokens and cost as LiteLLM reports them. A prompt answered from a file (`external/<name>`) is timed from the prompt being written to the answer being written. Its tokens are recorded when a usage file sits next to the answer (`<answer>.usage.json`, written by `openinterp usage` from a Claude Code subagent's transcript), and priced at list price for the recorded model from a dated table (`openinterp/core/pricing.py`, recorded as `price_table`); without one, only the prompt's length is recorded. A re-review or re-evaluation records only its own costs. These numbers are reported, never gated, and `reproduce` ignores them.

`openinterp reevaluate --record <file>` re-measures a record's stored probes under the current policy, without LLM calls. The result is a new experiment.

`openinterp reproduce <file>…` (or `--base-ref origin/main` for every record a branch adds or changes) checks that a record is what it claims to be. It loads the model commit and dtype the record pins and re-runs everything the verdict rests on, without LLM calls:
- each top example's per-token strengths, and the scale (`max_activation`) that every threshold is a fraction of;
- each counterfactual's observed Δ;
- the review, with the record's own probes, reviewer and policy;
- with `--corpus <the scan's corpus>`, the corpus sample: re-drawn from its seed and budget, it must hash the same and contain every top example. A different corpus is a warning. CI has no corpus, so it skips this check.

Numbers must agree within `atol × scale + rtol × |recorded|` (defaults 0.001 and 0.01). These absorb float differences between devices and library versions, but not an edited number. Every pass/fail, every gate and the status must agree exactly. Causal metrics and joint ablation are not gated, so a difference there is only a warning. A different device or library version is reported as a warning. A record judged under another protocol version can't be reproduced by the current harness, because its decision rule was different: re-evaluate it instead.

A trusted maintainer can instead **sign** a record (`openinterp sign <file>`, with an SSH key listed in `.github/allowed_signers`). This writes `<id>.json.sig` next to the record, over its exact bytes. `reproduce` then skips the record, reporting it as signed. A signature that doesn't verify fails like a mismatch. With `--base-ref`, the signers list is read from the base ref, so a branch can't trust its own key. A signature doesn't change the verdict, which still comes from the review protocol. It only records that the signer vouches for the record.

Reproduction can't show that the reviewer wrote its probes without seeing activations. It shows only that the record's numbers and verdict follow from its probes and the model.

## 6. Changing the protocol

Any change to the decision rule or its defaults bumps `PROTOCOL_VERSION` in `core/schemas.py` and adds an entry below. Records keep the version they were judged under. Re-evaluating them is an explicit step, never a silent one.

| Version | Change |
| --- | --- |
| 0.1 | Weighted mean of probe, counterfactual and lookahead pass rates; absolute Δ tolerance of 0.35. |
| 0.2 | Gated rule with balanced accuracy; relative Δ tolerance; minimum probe counts; independent-review gate; provenance block. |
| 0.3 | Near-duplicate probes cluster into single observations; confound-control gate against token, position and never-active baselines; correlated neurons and joint ablation recorded. |
| 0.4 | `independent_review` is recorded but no longer gated by default (`require_independent_review = False`). |
| 0.5 | Causal metrics (still not gated) are scored on claimed tokens or next-token KL at the pole's most-changed position, as percentiles against activation-change-matched same-layer neurons; control pairs are skipped. Replaces the fraction of the top-1-logit gap restored. |
| 0.6 | Attention heads can be audited. A head's strength is \|W_O (z − z̄)\| against its mean output over the scan (stored in the record), its ablation is mean ablation, and its causal metrics rank it against the other heads of its layer. The decision rule for neuron poles is unchanged; `CausalMetrics.null_neurons` now records how many comparison units were used. |
| 0.7 | A scan reads a seeded sample of whole documents within a token budget (default 200,000 tokens, seed 0), recorded in `provenance.corpus_sample`, instead of the whole corpus. The scale, and so the "active" threshold, is the maximum over that sample. The decision rule is unchanged. A record without a sample scanned its whole corpus, and re-evaluating it keeps that scale. |
| 0.8 | A unit active at the first position on at least half the vocabulary has a first-token floor, and is not measured at a text's first token: its peaks, scan statistics, confound baselines, held-out windows, wiring and input attribution skip it. For it, a one-token probe or counterfactual is recorded as unmeasured and counts in no metric. A unit active on at least half its scan's tokens is default-on: the scan stops on it, and a review fails the new `not_default_on` gate (`ReviewPolicy.max_active_share`). A hypothesis that ties the confound baseline passes, as intended; a rounding mismatch had failed some ties. This settles how GPT-2's negative poles and its early-layer positive poles with a first-token floor are audited (`docs/analyses/position-zero.md`). Re-evaluating an older record keeps its scale, and judges its floor against it. |

### Registry-level dependence

These do not change the decision rule.
- **Re-measurements.** An experiment that re-measures an earlier experiment's probe set (e.g. `reevaluate`) is marked as such. It is ranked after the original and is never counted as independent evidence.
- **Most-conclusive list.** The home page shows one row per neuron pole.
- **Standing.** A unit's status, and with it every verified count and the false discovery estimate, follows the rule under §3 step 9. It reads verdicts and never changes one, so introducing it did not bump the version. A claim backed by several agreeing reviews enters the false discovery estimate once, with its weakest review's P(useful).
- **False discovery estimate.** The home page reports the expected number of false discoveries among current VERIFIED claims, Σ (1 − P(useful)) over one claim per neuron pole.
- **Dictionary features.** Adding them changed the decision rule for no unit, so it did not bump the version. Their verdicts are left out of the home page's native totals, its verified list and the false discovery estimate, and are reported separately.

## 7. Worked example

Qwen2.5-0.5B, layer 11, neuron 4255, positive pole, selected at random (`wiki/experiments/Qwen--Qwen2.5-0.5B/L11/N4255-positive/`).

- **Hypothesis:** fires on the connective that introduces examples of a category just named ("industries, *including* …", "such *as*").
- **v0.1 verdict: VERIFIED (0.91).** Ten easy negatives outweighed three failed holdouts, and Δ predictions of 0.40 and 0.30 "passed" with observed values of 0.11 and 0.08.
- **v0.2 verdict: NEEDS_REVISION.** Specificity was 1.0 but sensitivity only 0.4: "for example", "like" and "among them" did not fire. Counterfactual reproduction was 1/4. Proposer and reviewer were the same model.
- **v0.3 re-evaluation: NEEDS_REVISION.** It also fails `beats_confound_baseline`, because the scan predates confound profiling. A rescan found:
  - token identity explains 24% of activation variance and position about 0%;
  - the mean strength on " including" is 0.37, below the 0.43 "active" threshold, so the token alone does not make the neuron fire and context matters;
  - no layer-11 neuron correlates above |r| = 0.10, yet co-activation tells a different story. Ten neuron poles co-fire on 40–52 of its ~180 active tokens, a lift of at least 10–13 at the 95% lower bound. Global correlation hid them because the coupling exists only when the neuron fires.
  - Ablating the top three partners moves the output about 14× more than ablating N4255 alone, and with the partners removed N4255 adds almost nothing (redundancy ratio 0.02). Its effect overlaps a shared pathway rather than being masked.
  - Its logit-lens tokens ("oor", " Fälle", ".ordinal") were noise. The Jacobian lens shares none of them and is just as flat (kurtosis 3.0), which supports the retrospective claim rather than revealing a hidden prediction role.
- **Next submission should:** narrow the claim to the token *including* (and *e.g.*) after a category noun, and get an independent reviewer.
