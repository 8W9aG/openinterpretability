How to read it

Three views of the same prompts, from this experiment's measures. Pick a prompt to see each view change in place, or play them all. Drag to turn it.

  • The embedding space: the model's representation space, as a cloud of 1,500 corpus tokens at every layer, with its shadow on the floor. The prompt's tokens sit in it, coloured in sentence order and joined by a thin path; each leaves a trail of where it has been. Play the layers and the space shifts as the model reads: every point moves smoothly from the embedding to the last layer. Compare with another prompt to see where the two sentences' states meet and part; its tokens are rings on a dashed path. Each token trails a coloured gas as it moves, like a comet. The axes are, by default, the three directions of most variance within the directions the route's heads and neurons write, drawn as labelled arrows. So the space is shown where the causal units act. Switch to the corpus's axes for the model's own top directions.
  • The route: layers run left to right. Cubes are attention heads and spheres are neuron poles, sized by their strength against the original prompt. Particles flow along each measured edge, faster and denser where it is stronger. Dots push {{ route3d.answer }} up, rings push it down.
  • Read it: as the bracket paper's figure does, a cursor reads the prompt token by token. Under each token, a bar per route unit shows its strength there. The 3D view follows the cursor: the units swell with their strength at that token, and the spectrum shows that token's state through the layers. At the last token the model makes its answer.
  • The spectrum: the state at the cursor's token after each layer, as a waterfall. Layers are time, from the embedding at the front to the last layer at the back. Each ridge is the state on the {{ route3d.prompts[0].spectrum[0] | length if route3d.prompts[0].spectrum else 0 }} principal directions shared by every prompt, the strongest first, as frequencies run in a spectrum. The surface is a smooth curve through the measured values, which the dots mark, and it flows between layers the same way. A curve can swing a little past the values between them, so read the dots.
  • Switch neurons off: set the whole route, or one of its units, to its mean, and watch the spectrum and the lens move. The clean ridges stay as dashed lines, so you see what changed. One unit at a time works on the original prompt; the whole route works on every prompt.
  • The logit lens: the same waterfall, but each ridge is what the model would answer at that layer: the probability of each candidate token. Small spheres follow {{ route3d.answer }} through the layers, so you see where the answer forms.
